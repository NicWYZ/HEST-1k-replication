# Outline for presenting the project to Dr. Zhu and Dr. Zhang

*18 November 2026, drafted by the oversight session for Nicolas's review. A working paper: the deck built from it is the deliverable. It follows `docs/WAYS_OF_WORKING.md`, "Decks". Every number is quoted from `docs/round11/closeout/round11_closeout_report.md` (cited as "report") or `docs/round10/README.md`, each of which names the results file, and for slides 4 and 11 from the round 3 and round 2 files named there; the file is repeated beside the number here. Unless stated: encoder `uni_v2`, scale $\log(1 + y)$, nominal 0.90, estimand $\theta_3$ (donor-weighted mean), 8 labelled donors.*

**The one message.** When expression predicted from histology is used for a statement about a cohort, the unit of uncertainty is the donor and then the center, and predictions shorten an interval only as far as they are accurate at the donor level, which spot-level accuracy does not tell you.

**What the deck asks of the advisors.** Whether this is one paper, what it leads with and where it goes (slide 12).

## Slides

**1. Title and the one message.** The sentence above, and the two questions of the proposal it answers: calibrated uncertainty for predicted expression, and valid inference with it.

**2. The setting.** Why: everything after depends on what is sampled and what is held out. Spots within slides within donors within centers. Tasks and donor counts: CCRCC 24, INDIANA_KIDNEY 25, LUNG_XENIUM 15, bowel Visium 18, kidney Visium 51 over 5 centers (report, section 6.4, `results/round11/closeout/a6/aggregate_v4/a6_conformal.csv`). Say on the slide that kidney Visium contains CCRCC and INDIANA_KIDNEY. The predictor is a ridge head on frozen pathology-encoder embeddings. One diagram, no table.

**3. Spots are not independent.** Why: the usual error bar treats spots as the sample. The donor-clustered standard error of a spot mean is 14.8 (LUNG_XENIUM) to 59.0 (brain Visium) times the i.i.d. one, median over genes (`results/round11/closeout/b1/h1a/b1_h1a_summary.json`). Figure: the ratio by task.

**4. A prediction set for a new donor.** Why: a set calibrated on spots is the default in the field. Formula: the split conformal set with the absolute score. Coverage, calibrated on random spots against held-out donors, per task with its donor count: CCRCC (24) 0.8824 and 0.8640, INDIANA_KIDNEY (25) 0.9070 and 0.8776, LUNG_XENIUM (15) 0.9006 and 0.8872, bowel Visium (18) 0.8984 and 0.8921, kidney Visium (51) 0.9073 and 0.8940 (`a6_conformal.csv`). On three earlier tasks with two donors the shortfall is 0.14 to 0.28 (HCC, SKCM, LUNG), though two others with two donors show 0.0654 and 0.0208 (READ, PRAD) (`results/round3/A1_coverage/a1_by_task__uni_v2__main.csv`). Message: the cost of a new donor is small on every task with 15 or more donors and can be large with a handful, a reading of these rows that no design was run to test; the spread over held-out donors (sd up to 0.144) is the larger risk. Nicolas decided on 18 November that this result is stated per task with its donor count, not as one pooled mean.

**5. A new center.** Why: deployment is at a hospital that supplied no training data. Held out by center on kidney Visium the median Pearson is 0.1231 against 0.4713 held out by donor (`a6_center.csv`; `results/round11/closeout/g2/aggregate_v4/g2_strength.csv`). At nominal 0.80, donor-level sets pooled across centers cover 0.9057 at the median center and 0.6552 at the largest center, which is CCRCC's 24 donors, and sets calibrated over centers cover 1.0, valid and uninformative with five centers (`a6_center_hcp.csv`). Part of the largest center's shortfall is a level shift: removing it raises coverage from 0.6552 to 0.7582, and on the normalised scale from 0.6555 to 0.8184 (`results/round11/closeout/b3/s0006_v1/b3_by_center.csv`, columns `cov_median` and `cov_centered_median`). Holding out a center costs more coverage than holding out a donor in 12 of 12 cells.

**6. Inference on a cohort mean with few labelled donors.** Why: the second question. Formulas on the slide: $\theta_3$, the difference estimator with the coefficient fixed at 1, and its design variance under sampling of donors. On CCRCC it covers 0.8965 against the labels-only estimator's 0.849 (`results/round10/final_runs/F4/paper_numbers.csv`, keys `B8.CCRCC.*`). Say plainly that the estimator is the survey difference estimator, not ours. This cell is the earlier run on 74,220 spots; slides 7 and 8 use a refit on 73,813 spots with other embeddings, where the same cell covers 0.8966 against 0.8493 (`a6_donor_level.csv`).

**7. What predictions buy.** Why: the practical question for anyone who would label fewer donors. Width ratio against the labels-only interval beside the donor-level $R^2_c$: CCRCC 0.8949 and 0.4290, INDIANA_KIDNEY 0.9570 and 0.1390, kidney Visium 0.9367 and 0.1986, bowel Visium 0.6691 and 0.6258, LUNG_XENIUM 0.5853 and 0.7139 (`a6_donor_level.csv`). Across genes the ratio tracks $R^2_c$ more closely than spot-level Pearson in 84 of 84 cells. Figure: width ratio against $R^2_c$, one point per gene, coloured by task.

**8. A stronger predictor does not change it.** Why: the obvious objection to slide 7 is that the predictor is weak. Two stronger predictors raise held-out Pearson (kidney Visium 0.4713 to 0.4816 and 0.5023; bowel Visium 0.5113 to 0.5296 and 0.5943; `g2_strength.csv`). The width ratio moves with $R^2_c$, not with Pearson: on CCRCC it rises to 0.9750 and 0.9984 as $R^2_c$ falls; on bowel Visium it falls to 0.6372 and 0.6044 as $R^2_c$ rises. Conformal coverage for a new donor moves by at most 0.0051. State the limits on the slide: the predictor named in advance gained 0.0106, 0.0186 and 0.0103 over the preset reference values against a bar of 0.02 (`results/round11/closeout/g2/aggregate_v4/g2_p111.json`), so this is supporting evidence, and the three-encoder predictor was carried forward after its results were seen.

**9. Where to spend labels.** Why: a lab chooses between labelling whole donors and a few spots in every donor. At matched cost the second is narrower in 141 of 156 comparisons, median width ratio 0.473 (`results/round10/final_runs/F3/regimeB/aggregate_v1/regcomp/f3_regcomp_checks.json`). On census income by state it is narrower in 3 of 12 under proportional allocation and 12 of 12 under equal allocation, median 0.699 (`results/round11/closeout/b4/equal_v2/b4_summary.json`): the allocation must match the estimand's weights. This slide carries the question of whether the second design can be bought on a spatial platform at all.

**10. Published intervals at few clusters.** Why: a reader would reach for these first. At four labelled clusters the cluster bootstrap covers 0.7145 and the cluster-robust interval 0.699, or 0.875 with the standard small-sample correction (`results/round11/closeout/b1/h5/b1_h5_summary.json`).

**11. The benchmark's labels.** Why: every result above depends on who the donor is. Of 72 samples audited against outside sources, 58 labels verified, 9 unverifiable, 5 contradicted (`results/round2/R5b_audit/donor_audit.csv`). The choice of split moves benchmark accuracy more than the choice of encoder, 0.1586 against 0.0977 (`results/round11/closeout/b1/h1b_h4/b1_h1b_h4_summary.json`).

**12. What this is and is not, and the decisions.**
- Every estimator and interval is classical or in print; the contribution is the measurement, and no method is claimed. That the gain follows donor-level accuracy is classical in substance, the cluster-sampling variance of model-assisted estimators (`results/round11/closeout/lit/lit_B_donor_level_accuracy.md`). The predictor is a linear head, encoder pretraining overlap with these cohorts is not stated by the publishers and so not ruled out (`results/round11/closeout/lit/lit_A7_pretraining.md`), and the fixed-coefficient estimator under-covers the spot-weighted mean on CCRCC (0.848 to 0.869).
- **Framing.** Either the applied paper answering the proposal's two questions in full, or one shorter paper on the message above with slides 3 to 8 as its spine. The oversight session's reading favours the second; Nicolas has not adopted it.
- **Primary scale.** $\log(1 + y)$ or the normalised scale; both are computed, and they differ for the spot-weighted mean.
- **The second labelling design.** Whether a few spots per donor can be assayed, and at what cost; without that, slide 9 makes no cost claim.
- **Venue and lead result,** and whether a preprint goes out first.
