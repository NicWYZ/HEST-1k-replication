## What the round established, as it now stands

Paths start at the repository root unless they start with `i0`. The report named for each finding explains it.

1. **HEST's patient labels do not mean what they say on two tasks, and the donor is now audited.** COAD's metadata puts three donors under one label; its own `subseries` strings and HEST issue #133 give the three, so COAD's shipped `test_0` holds out three donors and trains on one. COAD's random-minus-patient gap is 0.3172, against 0.0504 to 0.1931 on the other nine tasks (`results/round2/R3_splits/r3_per_task_terms.csv`; `results/round2/R5b_audit/r5b_coad_subseries_confirmation.csv`; `i04/exec/round2_R8_stage_report.md`, section 2). READ's two pairs are replicate sections of one specimen each, and its folds group the pairs. Holding the test slide and training size fixed, the partner section is worth +0.0901 (sd 0.0383, range 0.0326 to 0.1503, positive in 12 of 12 cells), the counterfactual value of a same-specimen replicate, which READ's split never realises (`results/round2/R5c_leak/r5c_leak_summary.csv`; R8 report, section 3). The audit of all 72 samples against sources outside HEST's metadata gave 58 verified, 9 unverifiable and 5 contradicted labels and the `donor_id` column every later grouping uses (`results/round2/R5b_audit/donor_audit.csv`). Caveats: round 3's `results/round3/D3_audit/donor_audit_r3.csv` supersedes this file on IDC (below) and records CCRCC's 24 samples as unverifiable, with INT4 and INT24 a possible pair (`docs/round03/i03/exec/round3_A3_decisions.md`).
2. **IDC's four samples are four donors; round 2's one-donor reading was wrong.** Round 2 read TENX95 and TENX99 as two sections of one tumour. That reading came from assigning TENX95 a vendor dataset that belongs to TENX98; HEST's labels were right (`results/round3/D3_audit/d3_notes.md`, section 2). The controlled IDC measurement stands as a number and changes its name: a slide from the same laboratory, instrument, panel and pixel size, from another donor, is worth +0.0652 in training (sd 0.0294, positive in 6 of 6 cells; 0.0651 in the first run under another seed, `results/round2/R5c_leak/r5_idc_replicate_leak.csv`), which is 0.5385 of IDC's gap of 0.121, and the shipped split does not leak. `r5c_leak_summary.csv` still records `leak_realised_in_shipped_split` as True for IDC, because results files are not edited. The partner confusion that was read as same-donor corroboration, 123 of 128 misclassified TENX spots landing on the other TENX slide (`results/round2/R5c_leak/r5d_idc_partner_confusion.csv`), is the scan-session signature of finding 4 across two donors. IDC's random-minus-patient gap of 0.1210 stands as a cross-donor gap; the R5 report's corrected 0.1361 rested on the one-donor reading and was confounded by training size.
3. **How the test set is split matters more than which encoder supplies the features.** Over ten tasks and three encoders (`hoptimus0`, `uni_v2`, `resnet50`) the random-minus-patient gap is 0.1586 (sd 0.0717), 1.6 times the 0.0977 spread between the best and worst of twelve encoders. It partitions exactly into training-set size 0.0126, spatial adjacency 0.0327, adjacency past the block edge 0.0115, and slide plus patient 0.1018 (`results/round2/R3_splits/r3_decomposition_terms.csv`; `i02/exec/round2_R3_stage_report.md`, section 6.2). On the three tasks where a patient has several slides the last step splits into novel slide 0.0148 (sd 0.0179) and same patient, other slide 0.1357 (sd 0.0962). A blocked split without a buffer depends on the grid size (spread 0.0281 over grids 4, 6 and 10), and a 2.5-pitch buffer makes it stable (0.0104) (R3 report, section 6.5). Caveats: the three-task means include COAD, whose 0.2578 is a multi-donor holdout, and READ, whose 0.0910 is a same-specimen term. Only PRAD has verified labels and a novel-slide term resolved from zero (0.0280, sd 0.0019, against 0.0583 for the patient), so "the patient, not the slide, is the unit" rests on PRAD at 2.1 times (R3 report, section 6.4).
4. **Encoders carry a slide and scan-session signature that is in the features and not in the expression.** Within PRAD patient 2, fifteen slides at nearly one pixel size, slide identity is decodable at 0.871 to 0.949 (chance 0.067) and scan session at 0.967 to 0.991, with 15 of 15 slides assigned to the right session (`results/round2/R4_probes/r4_probes_v2.csv`; `i03/exec/round2_R5_stage_report.md`, section 3). Regressing out nuclear morphology removes 0.014 of the accuracy within that patient, against 0.169 to 0.274 on LUNG, PAAD and SKCM, whose slides are different patients. Pixel-size class within PRAD patient 1 is at chance, 0.489 to 0.511 pooled. In expression, with session as the top level, the between-session variance is 0 (its raw estimate is at or below zero for 28 of 50 genes), against 0.04660 between slides within a session and 0.27606 within slides (`results/round2/R6_variance/r6_prad_session_variance.csv`; R8 report, section 4.2). Scan resolution spans 0.1369 to 0.6876 µm/px over the 72 samples and is aligned with patient exactly in PRAD and SKCM and partly in PAAD and IDC, so it is a proxy for scan session (`results/round2/R0_resolution/resolution_by_task.csv`); 31 of 72 samples have no independent pixel size, all of PRAD and LYMPH_IDC among them (`results/round2/R1b_heads/d4_pixel_size_check.csv`). Caveats: the session result rests on one patient and two sessions, and the resolution probe on seven slides split 5 to 2.
5. **The benchmark head has no intercept, and its $R^2$ deficit decomposes.** Fold-median $R^2$ over 12 encoders, 29 folds and 50 genes is $-0.953$ as shipped, $-0.155$ with a training-mean intercept, $+0.030$ with the test fold's own mean and $+0.100$ with the optimal scale as well, which equals $r^2$ and is the ceiling of any level-and-scale recalibration (`results/round2/R1b_heads/r1b_ladder_pooled.csv`; R3 report, section 4.1). The predictions vary 1.84 times more than is optimal given their correlation, from 2.17 for ResNet50 to 1.65 for UNI2-h (R3 report, section 4.2). The float64 exact-solver head `intercept_f64` passes the three-head identity to $10^{-13}$ and is the base predictor of later rounds (R3 report, section 3.1). Pearson is unchanged by the intercept. Under the faithful iterative solver a per-gene Pearson carries about $10^{-3}$ of solver noise (worst per-task mean 0.00311, COAD) and the predictions differ from the exact ridge solution by up to 0.352, so per-gene values are not quoted past three decimals (`results/round2/R1b_heads/r1b_solver_sensitivity.csv`; R3 report, sections 3.1 and 4.3). Caveat: the two oracle rungs read the test fold, so +0.070 is an upper bound on what a calibration layer can recover (`i03/exec/round2_R3_decisions.md`, section 1.6).
6. **Table A13 ranks encoders by embedding width.** H-Optimus-1 is rank 1 of 12 on `pca_ridge` (0.3891 over all ten tasks) and rank 7 on `raw_ridge` (0.259), rank 6 on the paper's nine-task convention; Spearman between width and `raw_ridge` score is $-0.950$ against $+0.727$ on `pca_ridge` over twelve encoders (`results/round2/R8_raw_heads/r8_raw_head_leaderboard.csv`, `r8_task_set_convention.csv`, `r8_width_correlations.csv`; R8 report, section 8). This confirms round 1's D2, whose eleven-encoder values were $-0.954$ and $+0.735$.
7. **Gene panels differ within tasks, and the target list depends on the selection protocol.** Samples share 159 of a union of 919 genes on PAAD, 259 of 823 on LUNG, 343 of 739 on SKCM, 412 of 670 on COAD and 500 of 582 entries on IDC (`results/round2/R2_fold_hvg/r2_panel_heterogeneity.csv`). A reimplementation of HEST's selection reproduces all ten shipped lists exactly. Selecting from training samples only changes the list by about half and moves within-slide Pearson by +0.0091 on average (sd 0.0487, positive in 61 of 116 folds, from $-0.0660$ on READ to +0.0441 on LYMPH_IDC), and on four imaging tasks names genes the held-out slide does not measure (R3 report, section 4.5). This is called selection-protocol sensitivity, not leakage, because the gap tracks the difference in the two lists' genes (Spearman 0.758) (`i03/exec/round2_R3_decisions.md`, section 1.3). With the shipped 50 fixed, the training-only and all-spot rankings correlate at Spearman 0.510 (sd 0.209), and 32 of 50 genes move more than five ranks (`results/round2/R2_fold_hvg/r2_rank_supplement.csv`; R8 report, section 6). Caveat: the mean number of genes the two lists share is 25.2 in the R3 report (`r2_leakage_summary.csv`, per-task means) and 27.2 in the R8 report and "27 of 50" in `i05/exec/round2_results_synthesis.md` (the rank supplement's per-fold rows); the record does not reconcile the two.
8. **The donor carries a large share of expression variance where it can be estimated.** A nested Henderson moment decomposition, checked on 40 simulations, gives a between-donor fraction of 0.3932 on CCRCC (24 donors) and 0.3354 on LYMPH_IDC (4), pooled 0.3761; within-slide variance is 0.6068 to 0.9854 of the total across the ten tasks (`results/round2/R6_variance/r6_variance_by_task.csv`, `r6_pooled_between_donor.csv`; R8 report, section 4). The directed pairing of CCRCC with PRAD gives 0.1217, an artefact of PRAD's two donors truncating to zero, and is reported beside the estimate (`i05/exec/round2_closeout_decisions.md`, section 1.2). Per gene, within task, between-donor variance does not predict which genes lose most from a patient split (+0.0441, p 0.0875), between-slide variance predicts the novel-slide cost (+0.2096, p 0.0003), and the genes the split costs most are the ones a partner slide recovers most (+0.3774) (`results/round2/R7_pergene/r7_correlations_task_centred.csv`). Caveats: five tasks rest on one donor degree of freedom; the pooled estimate's "verified labels" include CCRCC, which round 3 rated unverifiable; the summaries and the deck round the within-slide range to 0.6 to 0.9; and the partner-slide correlation pools IDC, whose partner is now a different donor, with READ.
9. **The Topic B estimand is a slide-level quantity.** The correlation of neoplastic nuclear area with GATA3 is 0.4209 on NCBI785, 0.1515 on NCBI783, 0.0116 on TENX95 and $-0.0649$ on TENX99 under the current morphology build, with the same order as round 1's build (`results/round2/R6_variance/r6_theta_build_comparison.csv`). The acceptance value was restated against that build as 0.410577 (log1p) and 0.420947 (raw), and round 1's 0.457791 reproduces to six decimals on the old build (`results/round2/R6_theta/r6_theta_acceptance.csv`).
10. **Every number leaving the project is swept against its file.** `code/scripts/verify_numeric_claims.py` was written at the closeout; at `round2-final` it verified 296 claims with 0 unresolved and 99 uncited over three documents, and its first finding was fifteen cited evidence files that had never been committed (`i05/exec/round2_closeout_report.md`). The sweep over every document reached zero unresolved at `round2-docs-clean`. Its exception classes are in `round02_methods_note.md`.

## What was tried and did not work

- **Absolute identity tolerances on a float32 pipeline.** $10^{-6}$ and $10^{-4}$ are below the measured accumulation floor; only the float64 pipeline makes them tests. The first float64 run failed because it cast float32 PCA output to float64 (R3 report, section 3.1).
- **Two different gene lists as a leakage measure.** Leakage and gene-set composition cannot be separated by comparing two lists; the fixed-list rank comparison replaced it (R3 memo, section 1.3).
- **Holding out both TENX samples together** (`donor_out`) confounded the drop with training size (7,015 spots against 31,526 to 32,531), and its size-matched variant silently dropped the TENX fold (R5 report, section 6).
- **Per-fold balanced accuracy under leave-one-slide-out.** It is recall on a single class and sat at the majority baseline of 0.714 (R5 report, section 3.3).
- **A ratio against a term within noise of zero.** COAD's "199 times" moved to 115 and 117 with rounding alone and was withdrawn (R3 report, sections 6.4 and 6.6).
- **Seeding from Python's `hash()`.** It is randomised per process, which made the `slide_out` arm irreproducible; seeds now come from `zlib.crc32` (R3 report, section 6.6).
- **Letting pyarrow infer a parquet schema.** Three R3 runs, about 35 CPU hours, died on their last write (R3 report, section 10).
- **Assuming which vendor page a sample came from.** Three audit verdicts were wrong until HEST's `download_page_link1` was read; the same assumption, in reverse, produced the IDC one-donor reading (R8 report, section 2; round 3 D3).
- **Fetching the 10x dataset pages.** Every attempt returned HTTP 429, four logged (`results/round2/R5b_audit/idc_fetch_log.tsv`).
- **`raw_xgb` for H-Optimus-1.** About 70 minutes per split, some 34 CPU hours in all; closed unrun because `raw_ridge` settles D2 (closeout memo, section 1.1).
- **An acceptance value defined on a superseded build**, and a pooled estimate that averaged a well-determined component with a truncated zero (closeout memo, sections 1.2 and 1.3).
