# Round 9, few-cluster inference and a search for a fresh direction (24 October to 6 November 2026, closed)

Round 9 looked for a methodological core for the PPI paper that would survive the literature check. A preparation (24 to 26 October, `00_prep/`) scanned about twenty directions and found none, and found instead where donor-level information lies in the HEST data. On 26 October Nicolas deferred a design and empirical paper (option A) and opened two independent tracks: `fewcluster` (valid inference with few labelled clusters under skew, option B) and `newtopic` (a fresh direction outside clustered PPI and hierarchical conformal prediction, option C). The plan of record is `masterplan.md`. The preparation and the tracks ran on branches `claude-science`, `claude-science-fewcluster` and `claude-science-newtopic`, which were merged into `main` at the close (pull request #36, 6 November). The round has no gate tags. Its documents as they stood before the record was reduced are at tag `record-full-2026-11`.

`docs/round10/00_prep/round10_findings_survey.md` lists the round's findings by question with status labels (rows D10 to D14, A9, A10, B10, B11, B13, B14, E1, E7, E8, and the rediscovered claims of section 3.1). This summary states them as they now stand, with what round 10 changed.

## What the round established, as it now stands

### The preparation

1. **No direction for a methodological core survived.** Fourteen directions went to independent novelty checkers, with four proposers and an open-problem miner, and none left a substantive open remainder (`00_prep/round09_direction_scan.md`, section 4; verdicts in `results/round9/scan/scan_structured_results.json`). The last checker (HCPWIDTH) found no finite-sample width law, switching point or valid optimal merger for hierarchical conformal sets, in a setting GHCP already occupies (`00_prep/round09_direction_scan_addendum.md`). Studentized prediction sets (STUD) are mostly in print (GHCP appendix B.1), and probe-then-label designs (PROBE) are classical two-phase sampling (`00_prep/round09_stud_and_depth.md`, `00_prep/round09_probe_novelty.md`). Every verdict is provisional where sources were read only from abstracts.
2. **A shared technical factor dominates between-donor variation on Visium.** The first principal component of standardised donor means carries 0.650 of between-donor variance on CCRCC, 0.313 on INDIANA_KIDNEY and 0.343 on LUNG_XENIUM (`results/round9/diagnostics/c0_shared_donor_factor.csv`). Library-size normalization lowers it on CCRCC to 0.338 and the median skewness of donor means from 2.053 to 1.416 (`results/round9/norm/norm_summary.csv`, `results/round9/norm/predictions_scored.md`; report `00_prep/round09_normalization_check.md`). Round 10's final runs reproduce 0.650 and 0.338 (`results/round10/final_runs/F4/paper_numbers.csv`, keys `F2.pc1.CCRCC.*`). Every estimand of rounds 3 to 9 is on $\log(1 + y)$ of unnormalized counts, which is decision D5, still open.
3. **The predictions carry little donor-level information for the median gene.** The leave-one-donor-out median $R^2$ of the donor mean under `uni_v2` is $-0.066$ (CCRCC), $-0.039$ (INDIANA_KIDNEY) and $-0.025$ (LUNG_XENIUM) (`results/round9/between/between_head_medians.csv`), and stays below zero after normalization. A donor depth proxy explains a median 0.523 on CCRCC (`results/round9/stud/probe_depth_auxiliary_medians.csv`; report `00_prep/round09_stud_and_depth.md`).
4. **One technical-outlier donor, INT5, breaks few-cluster intervals and hierarchical conformal coverage on CCRCC.** It has the largest average expression ($z = 3.98$) and is not flagged by histology (prediction outlyingness rank 15 of 24, embedding rank 22) (`results/round9/stud/atypical_donor_diagnostics.csv`). HCP covers it at 0.3096 at $o = 3$, and the cross-gene studentized set at 0.959 (`results/round9/stud/stud_per_donor_coverage.csv`). An oracle scale narrows HCP by at most 0.130 at $o = 3$ (`00_prep/round09_stud_and_depth.md`).
5. **A probe of three spots per donor reveals INT5, but regression on it under-covers.** Regression on the probe factor has 0.630 of the plain mean's width on CCRCC at 8 labelled donors, and every regression interval covers 0.739 to 0.892 at nominal 0.90 (`results/round9/probe/probe_medians.csv`). Labelling the probe-atypical donor with certainty lifts CCRCC coverage from 0.825 to 0.885 at 0.779 of the width, and is neutral on the other two tasks (`results/round9/probe/takeall_medians.csv`; report `00_prep/round09_probe_designs.md`). Round 10 reran this as 0.833 to 0.892 at 0.770 of the width (`results/round10/final_runs/F3/aggregate/t28_takeall_vs_r9.csv`). The design is classical.

### Track fewcluster

All statements are for location and scale equivariant intervals at $\alpha = 0.10$, over classes bounded by residual max-share, the largest share of one cluster in the residual sum of squares. Each theorem was proved by the session and again by a fresh agent from the statements alone, with exact enumeration at small sizes. The track's account is `i04/fewcluster/round09_fewcluster_closing_report.md`, section 2.

1. **Theorem 1, the fixed-coefficient frontier** (`i01/fewcluster/round09_fewcluster_frontier.md`). Under simple random sampling of $n$ of $G$ clusters a valid finite interval exists exactly when $\kappa_0 < \kappa_2(k^\ast - 1)$. Above it every valid interval is unbounded with positive probability. Two-point populations are extremal, and simple random sampling is minimax among fixed-size designs. The frontier is 0.158 at 8 of 24 and 0.071 at 8 of 51. A guarantee against residuals of 3 or 4 standard deviations needs 13 or 17 of 24 clusters labelled.
2. **Theorem 2, the tuned-coefficient frontier** (`i02/fewcluster/round09_fewcluster_tuned.md`, with the exact algorithm in `i02/fewcluster/round09_fewcluster_position.md`). The defeating samples are those on which residuals are affine in the predictions, so the frontier depends on them and is about half the fixed one. The exact algorithm matched an independent computation in 15 of 15 cases, and the exact median frontier over genes is 0.0907 on CCRCC, 0.0860 on INDIANA_KIDNEY (both at $n = 8$) and 0.1484 on LUNG_XENIUM (at $n = 6$) (`results/round9/fewcluster/exact/predictions_scored.md`). Not computed for ACS, where enumeration is infeasible.
3. **Theorem 3, the stratified frontier** (`i03/fewcluster/round09_fewcluster_stratified.md`). The frontier is a product over strata, a knapsack for a pooled cap and a count of simultaneously degenerate strata for the box class. It covers take-all clusters and regime B (every cluster labelled, a few units in each). Under the box class at $\lambda = 1$, regime B needs 5 persons per state on ACS and a median of 6, 8 and 8 spots per donor on CCRCC, INDIANA_KIDNEY and LUNG_XENIUM, where regime A (whole clusters) needs 11 of 51 states and 17, 13 and 8 donors (`results/round9/fewcluster/strat/`). With no bound on each cluster's share of the design variance, regime B needs 22 to 143 units per cluster. Caveats: the box class was defined after the pooled cap failed on ACS (prediction R4), and HEST then tested it; regime B's advantage rests on the share bound, which is decision V.
4. **Where the real tasks sit.** For the donor mean at the middle design size, the median residual max-share is 0.533 on CCRCC, 0.336 on INDIANA_KIDNEY and 0.312 on LUNG_XENIUM, and the shares of genes beyond the corrected fixed frontier are 1.00, 0.90 and 0.71 (LUNG_XENIUM at $n = 6$) (`results/round9/fewcluster/hest/i24_task_summary.csv`). INT5 carries the largest share for 0.80 of CCRCC's genes, and with INT5 labelled with certainty 0.94 of genes stay beyond (`results/round9/fewcluster/design/`). **Round 10 qualifies this.** At $n_L = 8$ under `uni_v2`, the shares of genes beyond `kappa_sup` are 0.98 (CCRCC), 0.94 (INDIANA_KIDNEY) and 0.44 (LUNG_XENIUM) for $\theta_3$, and 0.254 on LUNG_XENIUM for the donor mean (`results/round10/final_runs/F2/report_tables/r09_shortfall_uni_v2_primary_n8.csv`), so "practically every gene" holds for the kidney tasks and not for lung at that size. Lying beyond the frontier means no guarantee exists over the class; it does not by itself predict under-coverage at the population in hand. On CCRCC the fixed-coefficient interval covers 0.8965 at 8 donors for $\theta_3$ (key `B8.CCRCC.fix1`), and round 10's prediction FR6, that kidney Visium at 16 donors would lie beyond the frontier and cover below 0.88, was refuted (coverage 0.8924, `results/round10/final_runs/F2/diagnostics/f2_fr6.csv`).
5. **Critical values below the frontier, partly** (`i03/fewcluster/round09_fewcluster_critical_value.md`, `i03/fewcluster/round09_fewcluster_nov12.md`). A quadratic-majorant certificate gives valid critical values only up to about half the frontier (3.78 at 8 of 24, at 0.4 of it). Lower bounds from explicit populations are 1.9 to 3.0 up to three quarters of the frontier and 4.4 to 5.0 near it, against the normal 1.645. Near the frontier the band-lemma certificate gives only $c = 196.1$ at 12 of 24 (`results/round9/fewcluster/crit/part1_table.csv`). No certified value exists for the box class at the real settings. The certificate's method is classical (Pinelis) and the band lemma is in print (Jonsson 2011). The paper has no usable positive interval at the real tasks' sizes.
6. **The estimator** (`i03/fewcluster/round09_fewcluster_williams.md`). Williams' (1961) estimator and the fold-wise form are exactly unbiased (0.999 of 3264 cells) and equally good, and a fixed coefficient does about as well as any tuned form. With a level-free variance the exactly unbiased forms cover 0.84 on CCRCC and 0.86 on INDIANA_KIDNEY at 8 labelled donors. The note found that round 5's variance, as round 9's `R5` arm computed it, grows with the level of the predictions and widened the interval by 1.09 to 1.53 times on HEST and 9 to 20 times on ACS. **Round 10 overturned the reading that rounds 5 to 7 carried this.** Round 9's `R5` arm called round 5's code without round 4's `lin` term, which the reported intervals (label `textbook_t|fpc|lin|xf`) include. With the term, round 5's interval is 0.982 to 1.004 times the width of Williams' form at 8 labelled clusters on all four tasks, and coverage agrees within 0.002 (prediction FR1 held, `results/round10/final_runs/F1/aggregate/f1_fr1.csv`; `docs/round10/i02/final-runs/round10_final_runs_F1_report.md`, section 6). The track's `[proposed]` recommendation to adopt Williams' form was settled in round 10: the paper's primary estimator is the difference estimator with the coefficient fixed at 1, with the two-fold cross-fitted form in Mickey's form and round 9's within-fold variance beside it, credited to Mickey (1959) and Williams (1961) (`docs/round10/round10_methods_note.md`, items 1 and 3; round 10 summary, `docs/round10/README.md`).
7. **The regime comparison in round 10's final runs.** Regime B's fixed-coefficient interval is within 0.03 of nominal at $m \ge 6$ (FR7 held, largest deviation 0.0205), and is narrower than regime A in 141 of 156 cost-matched $\theta_3$ comparisons (`results/round10/final_runs/F3/regimeB/aggregate_v1/`). On ACS by state it is narrower in only 3 of 12, because its allocation is proportional to state size while the target weights states equally (`results/round10/final_runs/F4/aggregate/f4_acs_summary.json`). Round 10's bounded and unbounded columns give a median of 10 against 100 spots per donor on CCRCC and 2 against 75 persons per state on ACS for the donor mean, at their own settings, which differ from round 9's box computation (key `V.*` in `results/round10/final_runs/F4/paper_numbers.csv`). Round 10 did not reproduce round 9's box numbers as a stored column (`docs/round10/i05/final-runs/round10_final_runs_report.md`, section 7, item 4).
8. **A side result** (`i02/fewcluster/round09_fewcluster_sources_and_median.md`). The two-block construction proves Paul and Kuchibhotla's conjecture (arXiv:2403.06357) that no shift-invariant, locally bounded interval for the median exists below $\log_2(2/\alpha)$ observations, checked by a fresh agent.
9. **The paper.** An outline (`i02/fewcluster/round09_fewcluster_paper_outline.md`, `[proposed]`) and a first draft without prediction sets (`tracks/fewcluster/paper_draft_v0.md`). Round 10 reframed the paper as an applied one (`docs/round10/00_prep/round10_reframing.md`), and draft v0 is the record of the methods-first version.

**Novelty.** Thirteen checkers (NOV1 to NOV12 with NOV10b, logs in `results/round9/fewcluster/novelty/`) did not find in print Theorem 1 in its finite-population graded form with its possibility half and design minimaxity, Theorem 2 and its algorithm, Theorem 3 and the regime comparison, the median result, or the certificate's finite-population application. Known and cited are the proof technique (Bahadur and Savage; Gleser and Hwang; Dufour), the unrestricted impossibility, the iid limit (Lanke 1974; Székely 2006), pooling across groups under symmetry or Gaussianity (Ibragimov and Müller; Canay, Romano and Shaikh; Hagemann), the certificate's moment-duality method (Pinelis) and the band lemma (Jonsson 2011). Every "not found" is provisional, since several classical sources were read only from abstracts and nine paywalled sources were not obtained (list in `i03/fewcluster/round09_fewcluster_nov12.md`). Round 10 kept the theorems as candidates, not claims (`docs/round10/round10_methods_note.md`, O5). Claim B (a bounded-residual interval) is in the original PPI paper's appendices, claim D (what the design buys) is mostly in print, and the finite-population Bahadur and Savage lemma is folklore (`i01/fewcluster/round09_fewcluster_decision.md`).

### Track newtopic

1. **No statistics-forward direction with an open core outside clustered PPI and hierarchical conformal prediction** was found in three intervals (`i04/newtopic/round09_newtopic_closing_report.md`, section 2). Five proposer lenses wrote 45 raw ideas and found 24 fully and 21 partly in print themselves, and checkers passed none (`results/round9/newtopic/newtopic_structured_results.json`; `i01/newtopic/round09_newtopic_i01_report.md`).
2. **The thin remainders stopped.** A boundary test on thinned counts loses power at estimated boundaries (F2), a noise ceiling across scales needs a latent shape beyond Poisson noise (F3), and an iid $R^2$ bound over-claims only when the features carry the donor shift, a correction inside Atanasov et al.'s framework (F4). Fresh auditors confirmed the three stops (`i02/newtopic/round09_newtopic_i02_report.md`).
3. **Ridge reduced-rank regression** detects a rank-one response factor below the threshold of PCA of the outcomes, but conditional on the features this is a corollary of Liu, Liu, Pan, Zhang and Zhang (arXiv:2310.13939), confirmed by a fresh auditor with small technical gaps (`i03/newtopic/round09_newtopic_i03_report.md`; `results/round9/newtopic/i03/audit/audit_F1_corollary.md`). Interval 2's reading of it as a candidate core, "not found in print and not a direct case of the spiked separable theorem", is withdrawn; the second half stands only for Ding and Yang's theorem. In interval 2 its subagent first stopped it, a fresh checker overturned the stop, and interval 3 stopped it on other grounds.
4. **On the data,** pathology encoders agree with each other far more than with the labels (median within-slide correlation 0.548 between two encoders against 0.149 with labels on CCRCC), their predictions are smoother than the labels, and the HEST Xenium files on Longleaf are pseudo-spots, so aggregation can only coarsen (`results/round9/newtopic/n3_predictions_scored.md`).

## What was tried and did not work

- Directions the sessions generated themselves near clustered PPI and group conformal prediction were in print, often in survey sampling, econometrics or random-matrix signal processing. Candidates a first search found open fell to a deeper second search in every interval of track newtopic. A later search should start from a problem met in practice, run the deep search before any derivation, and include signal-processing and econometrics venues (`i04/newtopic/round09_newtopic_closing_report.md`, section 4).
- A between-donor prediction head fitted on donor means did worse than the pooled head (P2, P3 refuted).
- Studentizing prediction sets buys little width and is in print.
- Regression on a probe auxiliary narrows intervals that then under-cover; narrower and under-covering is not a result.
- A single pooled cap on every unit's share is the wrong class when strata differ in size: ACS regime B stays beyond it at every $m$ up to 30.
- A certified critical value in the classes the real tasks satisfy was not found; certificates work only deep inside the frontier.
- The exact tuned frontier for ACS (51 states) is infeasible by enumeration.

- The `kappa_star` column of `results/round9/fewcluster/feasibility/s3_frontier_exact.csv` and predictions P5 and P8 use $\kappa_2(k^\ast)$; `kappa_sup` is the corrected frontier $\kappa_2(k^\ast - 1)$ (0.158, not 0.125, at 8 of 24).

- `results/round9/fewcluster/novelty/novelty_NOV10_STRAT_candidates.csv` gives a DOI for Canay, Romano and Shaikh (2017) that Crossref does not know; the paper is 10.3982/ecta13081.
- In `results/round9/fewcluster/novelty/novelty_NOV11_CRIT_queries.csv` the 15 web-search rows are shortened reconstructions of the queries, and the file has 45 rows (20 arXiv rows) against the 44 the checker's counts sum to.
- `results/round9/fewcluster/verify_strat/verify_strat_report.md` checked the monotonicity of $\pi$ and $R \le \pi(\lfloor N/2 \rfloor)$ fully only for even $N$ and odd $N \le 10$, and its claim that defeating populations cover exactly $1 - \prod \pi$ for $c$ up to $10^6$ holds only until $c$ nears the reciprocal of the jitter. Neither affects a conclusion.
- `results/round9/fewcluster/verify_crit/verify_crit_report.md` counts five spot-checked lower bounds and tabulates four.
- The numbers in `results/round9/fewcluster/strat/` were produced with the box script's modes `acs_states` and `hest`; the script's earlier docstring named a mode `acs` that did not work.
- The fewcluster closing report is dated 27 October and was committed on 6 November. The direction scan's section 4 letters its options differently from the masterplan (there, C is the prediction-set half and D a new direction); the tracks follow the masterplan.
