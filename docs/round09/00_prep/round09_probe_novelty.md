# Round 9 preparation, the novelty verdict on probe-then-label designs

*26 October 2026. The claude-science session. It completes `round09_probe_designs.md`, which is not edited. Recommendations are `[proposed]`.*

The PROBE checker finished after Nicolas approved its network access (`results/round9/scan/scan_PROBE_queries.csv`, `results/round9/scan/scan_PROBE_candidates.csv`, entry PROBE of `results/round9/scan/scan_structured_results.json`). Its verdict is mostly taken. The estimator is the two-phase regression estimator with a first-phase auxiliary, and its variance and the split of a budget between phases are classical (Hidiroglou 2001, read in full, and the sources it cites; Mandallaz and co-authors for two-phase two-stage forest inventories). Labelling a few units in every cluster rather than whole clusters is shown to be best for PPI by Broska and Howes (arXiv:2610.09601). A probe-then-label design for unclustered PPI is Kluger and Bates (arXiv:2602.16933). The conformal use of a few target labels is Duchi et al.'s resizing and GHCP. Few-cluster skewness corrections exist in econometrics (Cornish-Fisher refinements for cluster-robust inference, arXiv:2603.24786). Pooling many genes to estimate a shared technical factor is standard genomic practice. The checker found one remainder not in print, a factor-estimation error of order 1/(oJ) when the probe is pooled across J outcomes, which it judged a short proposition rather than a paper's core.

With this verdict, every direction the session has examined is accounted for in `round09_direction_scan.md`, its addendum, `round09_stud_and_depth.md` and this note. None leaves a methodological core with a substantive open remainder. What the session established that the earlier rounds did not have is an empirical account of where donor-level information lies in spatial transcriptomics (`round09_stud_and_depth.md`, `round09_normalization_check.md`, `round09_probe_designs.md`), which could carry a design and empirical paper.
