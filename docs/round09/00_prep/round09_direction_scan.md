# Round 9 preparation, the direction scan

*24 and 25 October 2026. Written by the claude-science session on Nicolas's Mac, linked to Longleaf. Step S1 of CLAUDE.md section 3. Recommendations are `[proposed]` until Nicolas decides.*

## 1. Why and how

Round 8's positioning note (`docs/round08/i01/positioning/round08_positioning_note.md`) left the PPI paper with no methodological core of its own. This session's task, as Nicolas set it, was to look freely for a direction that could carry a statistics-forward methods paper in spatial transcriptomics, with a check of the literature for novelty as a hard rule before and after any work.

The scan ran in four waves. In each, a candidate direction was written as a one-paragraph core claim and sent to its own subagent, which had not seen the session's reasoning and whose only job was to find the paper that already did it. Every checker was told to search under the vocabulary of other fields (survey sampling, stereology, econometrics, psychometrics, neuroscience, genomics, conformal prediction and machine learning), to read at least the method section of every close paper where an open version existed, never to invent a citation, and to save its query log and candidate table. Wave 1 had nine candidates written by the session. Wave 2 had four more and one subagent that mined the limitation and future-work sections of about 22 recent papers for open problems. Wave 3 gave four fresh subagents the data facts of section 3 and the verdicts so far, each with a different lens (the many-genes factor structure, spatial and image structure, the predictor itself, decisions and design), and asked each to propose directions and search adversarially before keeping one. Wave 4 sent the two best proposals of wave 3 to new checkers.

The logs are in `results/round9/scan/` (query logs `scan_<SLUG>_queries.csv`, candidate tables `scan_<SLUG>_candidates.csv`, the proposers' logs `propose_<lens>_queries.csv`, the miner's table `scan_MINER_open_problems.csv`, and every checker's structured verdict with its declared deviations in `scan_structured_results.json`). Full-text copies of papers that some subagents saved stay outside git (`results/round9/scan/.gitignore`).

**Limits.** No checker could use OpenAlex. The credential approval was never answered while Nicolas was away, so forward-citation searches were done through Semantic Scholar where it was not rate-limited, and were missing for several anchors. The arXiv API was rate-limited for most checkers, who fell back on web search and Crossref. Most close papers were read from abstracts and method excerpts rather than in full. A verdict of "open" from these checks is therefore weaker evidence than a verdict of "taken". One checker (HCPWIDTH, a finite-sample width law for hierarchical conformal sets) is parked on an approval card addressed to Nicolas and has not returned.

The proposers of wave 3 graded every one of their own proposals "uncertain" or "likely taken". Their best two went to wave 4 as BETWEEN and FCP. The remainders that the checkers called open are instantiations of known machinery in this setting (cluster sampling, few labelled clusters, many genes), and each checker judged its remainder too thin for a methods paper at the venues Nicolas prefers. Several checkers independently named one remainder as the only part with technical substance. It is valid inference with 4 to 16 labelled clusters when cluster contributions are skewed, under the actual selection design and with a tuned coefficient. They also noted that such inference may be impossible without assumptions that bound the population (a Bahadur-Savage type limit), which is classical.

## 3. What the data show

These diagnostics come from cluster-level summaries built on Longleaf from the per-spot prediction files of rounds 3 and 4 (inputs and their md5 sums in `results/round9/diagnostics/c0_inputs.csv`; the summaries themselves stay outside the repository because they derive from gated data). The script is `code/scripts/round9_c0_diagnostics.py`.

**Donor means are skewed and dominated by one shared factor on Visium.** In `results/round9/diagnostics/c0_task_medians.csv`, the median skewness of donor-mean log1p expression over genes is 2.05 on CCRCC, 1.22 on INDIANA_KIDNEY and 0.17 on LUNG_XENIUM. In `results/round9/diagnostics/c0_shared_donor_factor.csv`, the first principal component of the standardised donor by gene matrix of means explains 0.650 of between-donor variance on CCRCC, 0.313 on INDIANA_KIDNEY and 0.343 on LUNG_XENIUM. Its correlation with the donor's average expression over genes is 0.998, 0.957 and 0.987, so it is a depth or detection factor shared by all genes. On CCRCC it explains 0.727 of the median gene's donor variance. The donor means of the predictions correlate with it at 0.19 to 0.44 in absolute value. On CCRCC with uni_v2, removing it halves the median in-sample cluster-level R^2 of the predictions (0.127 to 0.062), so part of what the encoders seem to know about donors on Visium is this factor.

**Out of sample, donor-level prediction does not beat a constant for the median gene.** The test is `code/scripts/round9_between_head.py`, with predictions written before the run (`results/round9/between/predictions_before_run.md`) and scored after it (`results/round9/between/predictions_scored.md`). Under leave-one-donor-out scoring, the median over genes of the out-of-sample R^2 of the donor mean is -0.097 to -0.032 for the recalibrated pooled head on the two Visium tasks, against -0.089 and -0.085 for a constant (`results/round9/between/between_head_medians.csv`). The in-sample values of 0.05 to 0.13 are near what one predictor fitted to noise gives with 24 and 25 donors. The spread over genes matters more than the median. The pooled head beats the constant for 0.46 to 0.64 of genes, by more than 0.1 for 0.28 to 0.52 of them, and on lung several alveolar markers (CA4, AGER, RTKN2, SFTPD, SFTPC) reach a leave-one-donor-out R^2 near 0.9 (`results/round9/between/between_head_by_gene.csv`, `results/round9/between/between_head_gene_spread_lodo.csv`). Donor-level signal from histology is real for genes whose donor variation reflects tissue composition visible in H&E, and absent for the rest. A head fitted on donor means, with or without shrinkage toward the pooled head, beats the pooled head for at most 0.155 of genes, and with 8 training donors every donor-level fit has a lower median than the constant. Of the four predictions, P1 and P4 held and P2 and P3 were refuted.

## 4. What this means `[proposed]`

1. After 14 candidate directions, four proposers and an open-problem miner, no direction for a methodological core survived the novelty check with a substantive remainder. The pattern matches round 8. The setting of the PPI paper, a finite population of clusters with an auxiliary known for all of them, is two-stage survey sampling, and every design or estimator question in it has prior art. The ML-side questions (multi-predictor, multi-task, cross-fitting, conformal for groups) were all claimed in 2024 to 2026.
2. The data findings of section 3 change what the paper can say empirically. For donor-level estimands on Visium, the predictions carry little out-of-sample information for most genes, the between-donor variance is dominated by a technical factor no image predictor sees, and the gains are concentrated in composition-marker genes. Earlier rounds' width ratios on Visium (`docs/round07/oversight/round07_ppi_results_explained.md`, A1) are consistent with this.
3. The options that remain, for Nicolas to choose between (decision D1), are these.
   - **A.** Present the PPI paper as a design and empirical study of labelling budgets with few clusters, with every component cited to its classical source and the contribution stated as the regime, the measured comparison on real tasks and these data findings. Low novelty risk, modest methods content. A benchmark release fits Mozer's call for clustered-PPI benchmarks (arXiv:2603.19160, section 5).
   - **B.** Work on the one remainder several checkers called substantive, valid few-cluster inference under skew, aiming either at a procedure with a finite-sample guarantee under an explicit, checkable restriction on the population, or at an impossibility result that says what no procedure can do at 4 to 16 clusters. High risk that it is impossible or classical.
   - **C.** Make the prediction-set half the centre (decision D2), where the HCP width-law check is still pending.
   - **D.** Leave clustered PPI and open a new brainstorm on a different statistical question in spatial transcriptomics. This session's waves suggest that the neighbouring ML-statistics space is crowded too.
4. This session does not open round 9 until Nicolas has chosen, since the choice is D1 and possibly needs David.
