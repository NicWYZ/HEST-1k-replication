## 2. The object and the conditional model

With $X$ an $n\times p$ matrix of iid $N(0,1)$ entries, $Y = XB + E$, $B = \sqrt\theta\,uv^\top$, $p/n\to\gamma$ and $q/n\to c$, F1 takes the top right singular vector $\hat v$ of $F = HY$, where $H = X(X^\top X + n\lambda I)^{-1}X^\top$. Since $E$ is independent of $X$, conditional on $X$

$$
\frac{F}{\sqrt q} = A + \Sigma^{1/2} W, \qquad \Sigma = H^2, \qquad A = \sqrt{\theta/q}\; H X u\, v^\top, \qquad W = E/\sqrt q ,
$$

a deterministic rank-one signal plus noise with a general left covariance and iid entries.

## 3. Results

1. **The supplied papers do not cover it.** Bao, Ding and Wang (arXiv:1809.10476) assume white noise, and Bao, Ding, Wang and Wang (arXiv:2008.11903) a spiked covariance with an identity middle weight. Prediction H1 held.
2. **The derivation.** The session's threshold and overlap formulas (plan, section 6) reproduce interval 2's threshold grid to within 0.0012 (H2 partly held, the gap traced to interval 2's edge bisection) and its overlap to four decimals in 48 cells (H3 held). A fresh simulation at $n = 1500$ differs from the theory by at most 0.037 in the 24 cells with $\theta \ge 1.5\theta^\ast$, and by at most 0.023 in the 16 cells with $\theta \ge 2.5\theta^\ast$.
3. **The corollary.** Liu, Liu, Pan, Zhang and Zhang (arXiv:2310.13939) study $A + \Sigma^{1/2}W$ with general symmetric bounded $\Sigma$, deterministic $A$ of rank $O(n^{1/3})$ and real or complex entries with a fourth moment. Their Theorems 1 and 2 give the spiked eigenvalues and the projections of both singular vectors. Their distant-spike condition is exactly F1's threshold. Their Theorem 1 (v2, May 2025) evaluated on realised $X$ ($n = 1500$, 3 seeds) matches the session's limiting overlap within 0.023 in 12 cells. Rotated to $H$'s eigenbasis, the model is also that of Chapon, Couillet, Hachem and Mestre (arXiv:1207.0471), whose outlier equation is the session's term by term, though that paper is stated for complex Gaussian entries and gives eigenvalues only.
4. **The audit.** A fresh agent read both papers' assumptions and theorems and wrote its own check. Its verdict is "corollary with gap", with no new mathematics needed. The gaps are that Liu et al. cover only spikes above the threshold, that the passage from conditional to unconditional limits is routine but unwritten, and that evaluating the limits takes some resolvent algebra. Its Monte Carlo at $\gamma = 2$, $c = 0.1$, $\lambda = 0.3$ agrees with Liu et al.'s formula in replicate means (8 replicates, `liu_check_n1500.csv`) within 0.012 at $\theta = 0.7$ and 0.001 at $\theta = 1.1$, and poorly near the threshold ($\theta = 0.4$), where the theorem does not apply. Its literature search (web search only, so the absence is provisional) found no paper that states the ridge threshold or overlap. The case $\lambda = 0$ is classical. Files in `results/round9/newtopic/i03/audit/`, the note being `audit_F1_corollary.md`.
5. **What is left.** On a realised $X$ ($n = 2000$, $c = 0.1$, finite-$n$ values) the ridge threshold lies below PCA's $\sqrt c = 0.316$, at 0.271 for $\gamma = 2$ and $\lambda = 0.3$ and about 0.258 for $\lambda$ between 1 and 3. At $\gamma = 0.5$ it is 0.194, against 0.231 for least squares, whose limit is $\sqrt{\gamma c} = 0.224$ (audit, `threshold_vs_lambda_gamma*.csv`). The new content is the improvement of ridge over least squares and the interior optimal penalty, a one-parameter optimisation of an explicit formula. That is a proposition with a figure, and it reaches no further than the corollary it rests on.
