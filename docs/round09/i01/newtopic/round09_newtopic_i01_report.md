## 1. What was done

The track looked for a fresh direction for a statistics-forward methods paper, outside clustered PPI and hierarchical conformal prediction, against six criteria fixed before the search (plan, section 2). The search ran in two waves of a different kind, followed by independent checkers, and one data run.

- **Wave 1, proposers.** Five subagents, each with one lens (forecast evaluation, frozen representations, measurement, shift between donors, many outcomes) and the exclusion digest of every direction the project had graded, wrote 45 raw ideas, searched each before keeping it, and kept 10.
- **Wave 2, open-problem miners.** Three subagents scanned recent papers for their stated limitations and open problems in spatial-omics statistics, the evaluation of foundation models and high-dimensional financial econometrics, and ranked 17 problems. They counted 17, 20 and 24 papers as read, by different standards. The spatial-omics miner read limitation sections from full text. The foundation-model miner scanned PDFs for conclusion and limitation passages and read none end to end. Of the financial-econometrics miner's 24, about 17 had limitation passages read, 4 only an abstract or introduction and 3 only in part.
- **Checkers.** Nine candidates went to fresh checkers whose only task was to find the paper that already did the claim, under the vocabulary of several fields. The session chose them by fit to the criteria of the plan's section 2, not by the proposers' or miners' own novelty grades. From wave 1, five of the ten kept candidates were checked (two were merged into SCALE), one was dropped by the session (below), and three were not checked (FORECAST C, a detectability threshold that its proposer found largely in Tipton's October 2026 paper; MULTI D, empirical Bayes with donor-shared error, close to Sun and Stephens; SHIFT D, triple collocation, found in three fields). From wave 2, four of the 17 mined problems were checked. The other 13 rest on the miners' own scoop searches of one to five queries each. Four of these the miners graded partly open. They are neighbour effects under transcript spillover and deconvolved cell-type composition as a generated regressor (spatial omics, both specific to biology and weak on the finance criterion), donor-aware CKA similarity between encoders (whose independence-test half the miner itself found in print), and a ridge signal-to-noise frontier with random group SNR (close to wave 1's ground on ridge under group structure).
- **Data run N3.** Diagnostics of the per-spot prediction files on six tasks and three encoders, with predictions written before the run.

All logs are in `results/round9/newtopic/` (queries and candidates CSVs for each proposer and checker, papers, queries and problems CSVs for each miner, and every structured output with its declared deviations in `newtopic_structured_results.json`).

The session dropped one further wave-1 candidate itself, an asymptotic theory of pooled ridge probes in the number of donors. With $x_{di} = \mu_d + w_{di}$, $y_{di} = \beta^\top w_{di} + \gamma^\top \mu_d + a_d + \varepsilon_{di}$ and many spots per donor, the pooled ridge objective is

$$
(b-\beta)^\top \Sigma_W (b-\beta) + \frac{1}{G}\sum_{d=1}^{G} \big(\mu_d^\top(\gamma - b) + a_d\big)^2 + \lambda \lVert b \rVert^2 .
$$

Writing $P = \Sigma_W + \lambda I$ and completing the square in $b$ gives

$$
(b - b_0)^\top P\, (b - b_0) + \frac{1}{G}\sum_{d=1}^{G} \big(\mu_d^\top(\gamma - b) + a_d\big)^2 + \text{const}, \qquad b_0 = P^{-1}\Sigma_W \beta ,
$$

a generalised ridge regression on the $G$ donor means $\mu_d$ with penalty centre $b_0$. After the change of variables $b' = P^{1/2} b$ it is an ordinary ridge regression with design covariance $P^{-1/2}\Sigma_B P^{-1/2}$, penalty centre $P^{1/2} b_0 = P^{-1/2}\Sigma_W\beta$ and true coefficient $P^{1/2}\gamma$, with unit penalty weight on the $1/G$-averaged loss. The session judged that proportional-asymptotics ridge theory with a general covariance covers it. No checker examined that judgement. It assumes $w_{di}$ has mean zero and is independent of $\mu_d$, $a_d$ is independent noise, and spots per donor are many compared with the feature dimension, which real data (512 to 1536 features) may not meet. The risk on a new donor is a $\Sigma_W$-weighted estimation error about a shifted centre, so a generalised deterministic equivalent would still be needed.

Every checker declared limits, listed in full in `newtopic_structured_results.json`. Semantic Scholar was frequently rate-limited, many arXiv phrase queries returned nothing, forward citations were partial, and most close papers were read from abstracts or targeted passages. Some limits bear on single verdicts. The LLGCLUSTER checker stopped early at the session's request and could not read the February 2026 version of its anchor paper. The RRRIDGE checker's keyword searches on Semantic Scholar all failed, and it read the spiked-covariance and shrinkage papers by abstract only. The BOUNDARY checker searched the finance and remote-sensing literatures with one query each. The WINNER checker read the exclusion digest only in part and took no forward citations of the two most recent competitors. Of the verdicts graded mostly taken, SCALE is the closest call, since its checker said a CLT for the two-scale estimator under count noise could carry a statistics-journal paper. Every "partly open" verdict is provisional, and is weaker evidence than one of "taken".

## 3. What the data showed

The N3 run (Longleaf job 38c99f72, `code/scripts/round9_newtopic_n3_diagnostics.py`, scored in `results/round9/newtopic/n3_predictions_scored.md`) looked at structure no earlier round had examined. Within a slide, two encoders' predictions of a gene correlate far more with each other than with the label, 0.548 against 0.149 on CCRCC and 0.530 against 0.126 on INDIANA_KIDNEY (`results/round9/newtopic/n3_task_medians.csv`). Predictions are spatially smoother than labels in 0.695 to 0.981 of gene-slide pairs. The labels' first principal component across genes tracks spot depth at 0.855 to 0.949, while that of the residuals does so at only 0.371 to 0.763 (`results/round9/newtopic/n3_slide_medians.csv`), which suggests, as an inference the run did not test directly, that the encoders capture part of the within-slide depth variation, plausibly through cellularity, while round 9's donor-level depth factor is invisible to them. Of the three predictions, D1 partly held, D2 mostly held and D3 was refuted. None of this suggested a methods question that the waves had not already examined.

One correction to the brief's inventory. The HEST Xenium files on Longleaf are pseudo-spot binnings (`st/*.h5ad`); the cell-level transcripts were not downloaded. Xenium data can therefore be coarsened but not re-aggregated from single cells, which limits every aggregation-scale idea. There are also twelve encoders, not ten, since `virchow` and `virchow2` are present.

## 4. The predictions, scored

- **W1, refuted on the strict reading.** The prediction was that at least 0.7 of raw ideas would be found in print by the proposers. They found 24 of 45 (0.533) fully in print and the other 21 partly. On the strict reading (fully in print) the share fell short. On a lenient reading (at least partly in print) it was 1.0 and the prediction held.
- **W2, held, on five of the ten kept candidates.** None of the five wave-1 candidates sent to checkers was graded partly open. The prediction allowed at most two. Three kept candidates were not checked (section 1).
- **W3, not supported.** Every checked wave-1 candidate was mostly taken, whether tied to a data fact (LOADING on the depth factor, HIDDEN on the outlier donor, PRANK on the donor nuisance factor) or not, so the comparison the prediction needed did not arise. The three graded partly open came from open problems stated in other papers, though LLGCLUSTER and the RRRIDGE variant are motivated by the donor-level factor.
- **W4, refuted on the count.** The prediction allowed at most one mined problem graded partly open with a remainder able to carry a methods paper. Three were graded partly open (BOUNDARY, LLGCLUSTER, RRRIDGE). All three checkers called the remainder proposition-plus-appendix in size, and each left a conditional route to a paper if the proofs turn out non-trivial.

## 5. What this means `[proposed]`

1. Option C looks as crowded as the ground round 9's preparation searched. Of nine candidates sent to checkers, none reached "open", and the three "partly open" remainders are theory extensions of machinery already in print. Their proposers and miners graded none of the 45 raw ideas and 17 mined problems as open. Of the ten kept candidates and 17 mined problems, 16 were not independently checked (three kept candidates and 13 mined problems) and one was dropped by the session (section 1). The pattern of rounds 8 and 9 holds. Questions that the project's data raise, in a setting of few groups, many outcomes and frozen features, have owners in survey sampling, econometrics, psychometrics or random-matrix theory.
2. Of the remainders, RRRIDGE fits Nicolas's criteria best. It is high-dimensional regression theory used in finance (latent factors from a "factor zoo" of characteristics), it has a natural test in many genes predicted from embeddings, and its open part is stated as open by Wan, Li, Lu and Song. Its checker named a variant with a group-level nuisance factor in the errors as the more defensible core, and warned in the same breath that this variant must first be checked against PRANK's ground (Bing, Ning and Xu's hidden-variable model, and Wu and co-authors' reduced-rank regression with group intercepts), which this track already graded mostly taken. The risks are three. Its limits may follow from published spiked separable-covariance and eigenvector-overlap theory (conditional on $X$ the fitted matrix is a spiked signal plus $H E$ with $H$ the ridge hat matrix), and the checker, reading those papers by abstract, leaned towards the existing tools sufficing. The nuisance variant may sit inside excluded ground. And a random-matrix group could produce the result quickly.
3. BOUNDARY is a plausible theory note for a statistics journal if its results are proved with non-trivial rates, but its checker called the remainder thin and doubted the claimed form of the size inflation (for a local-linear statistic the bias is of order $h^2$ times curvature, not $h$ times gradient, a Matérn field with smoothness at most one has no gradient, and validity must be defined conditional on the latent field). Its transfer to finance is weak, and it needs raw counts rather than the project's log1p labels.
4. The track's options for interval 2 are these, for Nicolas to choose between.
   - **C1.** A short feasibility interval on RRRIDGE with the group nuisance factor. That means a focused novelty check against spiked separable-covariance and eigenvector-overlap theory and against PRANK's hidden-variable ground, a derivation of the risk limit in the simplest case (isotropic features, rank one), and a numerical check against simulation and the CCRCC and lung prediction targets, before any commitment.
   - **C2.** The same for BOUNDARY.
   - **C3.** Close the track with this report as a negative result, and put the effort into option A or option B, which is outside this track's remit.
5. The session's own recommendation is C1, with a stop rule written before it starts. If the focused check finds the risk limit as a corollary of published results, or the rank-one derivation does not give a threshold that differs from PCA of the outcomes, the track closes under C3.
