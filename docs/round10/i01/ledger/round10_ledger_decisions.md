## 1. Question E, the estimator for the final runs (decided by the ledger)

### 1.1 The decision

The final runs use the **two-fold cross-fitted difference estimator in Mickey's form** wherever a coefficient is fitted on the labelled clusters. The $n$ labelled clusters are split at random into two folds $A$ and $B$ of sizes $\lfloor n/2 \rfloor$ and $\lceil n/2 \rceil$. For fold $k$, the coefficient $b_{-k}$ is fitted on the other fold's labelled clusters, the labelled clusters outside fold $k$ are kept as observed, and the mean of the rest of the population is estimated by the difference estimator on fold $k$:
$$
t_k = \frac{1}{G}\Big[\sum_{g \in L\setminus k} z_g + (G - n_{-k})\Big\{b_{-k}\,\bar f_{U \setminus (L\setminus k)} + \frac{1}{n_k}\sum_{g \in k}(z_g - b_{-k}f_g)\Big\}\Big],
$$
with $n_{-k}$ the number of labelled clusters outside fold $k$. The estimate is $\hat\theta = \tfrac{1}{2}(t_A + t_B)$. For even $n$ this is Williams' (1961) eq. (2) with $k = 2$ groups, and for odd $n$ it is the average of two of Mickey's $t_\alpha$ with complementary training sets. Here $t_k$ is indexed by the fold it estimates on. L2 part 1 indexes by the fold the coefficient is fitted on, and since $\hat V$ below is symmetric in the two pieces nothing changes.

The **primary variance for this form** is round 9's within-fold residual variance,
$$
\hat V_w = \Big(1 - \frac{n}{G}\Big)\frac{s_w^2}{n}, \qquad s_w^2 = \frac{1}{n - 2}\sum_{k \in \{A, B\}}\sum_{g \in k}\big(e_g - \bar e_k\big)^2, \qquad e_g = z_g - b_{-k}f_g,
$$
with a $t_{n-2}$ reference. It has no published source. It is the record's plug-in (`code/scripts/round9_fewcluster_williams.py`, `v_within`), with the reference distribution round 9 chose for $n - 2$ pooled residual degrees of freedom, and its support is round 9's design simulation and the enumeration of section 1.2, item 4. Beside it the final runs compute and report the exactly unbiased estimator
$$
\hat V = \tfrac{1}{2}\big(s_A^2 + s_B^2\big) - \tfrac{1}{4}\big(t_A - t_B\big)^2,
$$
where $s_k^2$ is the classical SRSWOR variance of the difference estimator on fold $k$'s residuals, scaled to the mean over all $G$ clusters,
$$
s_k^2 = \Big(\frac{G - n_{-k}}{G}\Big)^2\Big(1 - \frac{n_k}{G - n_{-k}}\Big)\frac{s_{e,k}^2}{n_k},
$$
with $s_{e,k}^2$ the sample variance of $e_g$ over fold $k$ (divisor $n_k - 1$), as in `fold_piece` of `results/round10/ledger/checks/l3_split_variance_check.py`. The final runs also report the nonnegative estimator $\max(\hat V, \hat V_w)$, whose expectation is at least $\text{Var}(\hat\theta)$ because $E(\hat V) = \text{Var}(\hat\theta)$. For this form the interval with $\hat V_w$ is primary, unless the final runs find, in the design simulation over a task's own cluster values, that the median over genes of $E(\hat V_w)/\text{Var}(\hat\theta)$ is below 0.95 in any cell with $n \ge 6$, any estimand, encoder or scale, on any task. In that case the primary variance becomes $\max(\hat V, \hat V_w)$ for every task, so that the paper uses one interval throughout, and the switch is reported. The rule is written here, before the runs. Which estimator is the paper's primary one (this form or the fixed coefficient of section 2) is R7-6, Nicolas's decision.

The coefficient inside the folds is round 5's (least squares with an intercept on cluster means, clipped to $[0, 1]$), unless Nicolas decides otherwise on R7-6 (section 2). The form needs at least 2 labelled clusters in each fold to estimate $s_k^2$. Below 6 labelled clusters a fold of 2 or 3 fixes the slope from 2 or 3 points. There the paper uses the fixed coefficient of 1 if Nicolas accepts section 2, and otherwise round 5's rule, which sets the coefficient to 0 and so gives the classical estimator.

### 1.2 Why this form and not the others

1. **Exactly unbiased under simple random sampling of clusters without replacement, at any $n/G$.** Each $t_k$ is Mickey's $t_\alpha$, unbiased by his (2.6), so the average is unbiased for any coefficient rule computed from the other fold only (Mickey 1959, p. 596; Williams 1961, p. 268; L2 part 1, section 1, item 1; L2 part 2, section 1). Round 9 found Williams' and the fold-wise forms unbiased below three Monte Carlo standard errors in 0.999 of 3264 cells (`results/round9/fewcluster/williams/predictions_scored.md`, W1).
2. **It splits only the labelled clusters.** The design target is the mean over the fixed set of clusters, so only the labelled clusters need to be split. The fold-wise form of Lu, Shi, Liu and Ding (arXiv:2508.15664) and An, Dagdoug, Haziza and Tillé (arXiv:2609.10904) also splits the unlabelled clusters, and its variance estimator is consistent, not design-unbiased (An et al., section 4.2 and Theorem 5; Lu et al., Theorem 4 and Appendix B.10). Round 9 found the two equally good at the median population (`results/round9/fewcluster/williams/i34_williams_summary_hest.csv`, `i34_williams_summary_acs.csv`).
3. **An exactly unbiased variance estimator exists for it, and odd $n$ is covered.** $\hat V$ is unbiased by an elementary identity that needs only that the two pieces have the same mean and that each $s_k^2$ is unbiased for its piece's variance, with no condition on how the two pieces depend on each other. The same form is Mickey's (4.4) and Zhang, Sanguiao-Sande and Lee's (2025) eq. (9). An exact enumeration over every sample and every fold assignment, with equal and unequal folds and with three folds, found $E(\hat V)/\text{Var}(\hat\theta) = 1.0$ in all 30 settings (`results/round10/ledger/checks/l3_split_variance_check_output.txt`; `round10_ledger_L3_reads.md`, section 2, on branch `round10-ledger`). Applying the identity to complementary folds is the oversight chat's reading, `[proposed]`, and is what Nicolas set aside to look at on 8 November.
4. **Why $\hat V_w$ is primary and $\hat V$ is reported beside it.** $\hat V$ is negative in up to 0.217641 of the enumerated cases (same output), and an interval needs a nonnegative variance. $\hat V_w$ is nonnegative, and it is what round 9 ran in its design simulation over the observed `uni_v2` donor means, with 50, 50 and 42 of 343 genes on the three HEST tasks (coverage 0.8372 on CCRCC, 0.8632 on INDIANA_KIDNEY, 0.8911 on LUNG_XENIUM and 0.8914 on ACS by state at 8 labelled clusters, `results/round9/fewcluster/williams/i34_williams_summary_hest.csv` and `i34_williams_summary_acs.csv`, column `median_cov`, form `W_within`), and in the enumeration its expectation was 0.990693 to 1.329553 times the true variance, and 0.994496 to 1.038315 under round 5's clipped coefficient (`results/round10/ledger/checks/l3_split_variance_check_output.txt`). It is a plug-in, so the final runs measure its bias on each task, and the switch rule of section 1.1 decides.
5. **Round 5's estimator is retired.** It is Williams' form without the factor $1 - n/G$ on its correction term, so it is biased where $n/G$ is large (B9). It stays only in the final runs' anchors and in the check of whether the intervals of rounds 5 to 7 carried the level-dependent variance (`docs/round10/i01/final-runs/round10_final_runs_brief.md`, stage F1).

### 1.3 Where the form applies

Regime A, the design target, with whole clusters labelled. In regime B every cluster is labelled and the coefficient inside form C is fitted across clusters. Each cluster's units are sampled independently of the others, so a coefficient fitted on other clusters' labelled units is independent of a cluster's own sample, and each cluster's estimate is conditionally unbiased. That reading is the oversight chat's, `[proposed]`. The variance in regime B treats that coefficient as fixed, and the final runs report the fixed-coefficient form beside it (section 2), so no new estimator is needed there. That variance sums each cluster's conditional variance and drops the small covariances between clusters that sharing fitted coefficients creates, so it is approximate, not exact.

## 2. R7-6, the coefficient (Nicolas's decision), recommendation `[proposed]`

**Recommendation.** The paper's primary estimator is the difference estimator with the coefficient fixed at 1, at every number of labelled clusters, with its exact variance and exactly unbiased variance estimator. The two-fold cross-fitted form of section 1 with round 5's clipped coefficient is reported beside it from 6 labelled clusters, and the classical estimator (coefficient 0) is reported as the control. No coefficient fitted on the same labelled clusters it is applied to is used anywhere.

**The reading behind it.**

1. **The fixed coefficient is classical and exact.** Cochran's Theorem 7.1 (eqs. 7.3 and 7.4, p. 190) and eq. (7.8), Särndal, Swensson and Wretman's Result 6.3.1 (pp. 222 to 223) and Breidt and Opsomer's eq. (4), (5) and the unbiased variance estimator (p. 192) give the difference estimator with a preassigned coefficient as exactly unbiased under any relation between outcome and prediction, with exact variance and an exactly unbiased variance estimator. With clusters as the units, PPI with coefficient 1 is this estimator (L2 part 1, section 1, items 1 and 6; `round10_ledger_L3_reads.md`, section 1.2).
2. **A fitted coefficient costs variance at these sizes.** Cochran section 7.3 to 7.7 gives the fitted coefficient's bias as order $1/n$ and Table 7.1 shows the large-sample variance formula understating by 28% to 38% at $n$ of 6 to 12 (L2 part 1, section 1, item 5). Mani, Xu, Lipton and Oberst (ICML 2026, Proposition 4.1) give a break-even correlation for a fitted coefficient that rises as the sample shrinks (L1 S4, section 4).
3. **The record's own runs agree.** In round 9's design simulation over the observed cluster values, the fixed coefficient had the smallest or near-smallest root mean squared error at every $n \le 10$ on every task (`docs/round09/i03/fewcluster/round09_fewcluster_williams.md`, section 2, item 6). At 4 labelled clusters, where round 5's rule sets the coefficient to 0, the fixed coefficient's median root mean squared error was 0.2033 against 0.2335 on CCRCC, 0.0837 against 0.0880 on INDIANA_KIDNEY, 0.1351 against 0.1623 on LUNG_XENIUM and 0.0424 against 0.0605 on ACS by state (`results/round9/fewcluster/williams/i34_williams_summary_hest.csv` and `i34_williams_summary_acs.csv`, column `median_rmse`, forms `FIX1` and `W_within`). A coefficient fitted on the whole labelled sample (`GLOB`) covered below every other form at 8 labelled clusters (0.8040 on CCRCC against 0.8372 for `W_within`, same file, column `median_cov`).
4. **Round 9's frontier theorems are stated for the fixed coefficient** (Theorems 1 and 3), so the primary interval is the one the paper's explanation of the coverage shortfall is about.
5. **Where it can lose.** A fixed coefficient of 1 beats the classical estimator only when the predictions' covariance with the outcome at the cluster level exceeds half their variance (Särndal et al., Exercise 6.4, p. 243, a coefficient between 0 and twice the optimum). On Visium the predictions carry little donor-level information for the median gene (D12). The final runs therefore report, per task, the share of genes where the fixed-coefficient interval is wider than the classical one, and the paper says it.

**Default if Nicolas does not decide.** Round 5's rule (the clipped cross-fitted coefficient, 0 below 6), as the state block carries.

## 4. V, a bound on one cluster's share of the design variance (settled by the literature and by testing)

**What the literature says.** No source found by the logged searches, in survey sampling, econometrics or probability, gives a finite-sample bound on one cluster's or stratum's share of the design variance under which a few-cluster interval is valid, or tells a user how to choose $v_0$.

1. **Every condition found is asymptotic.** Hájek's condition (via Li and Ding), Bickel and Freedman's "small share or heavily sampled" (Theorem 3, p. 473, read in full), Krewski and Rao's bound on population shares, Yang, Qu and Li's Condition A1(iii), and the many-cluster conditions of Djogbenou, MacKinnon and Nielsen, Carter, Schnepel and Steigerwald, and Chiang, Sasaki and Wang (L1 S5, section 3; L2 part 1, section 1, item 3; L2 part 2, section 2.3).
2. **The one finite-sample bound has no usable constant.** Bloznelis (1999) bounds the error of the finite-population Studentized mean by an absolute constant whose value is never given, and in shares its best possible value is $0.30c$ to $0.65c$ at the project's sizes (`round10_ledger_L3_reads.md`, section 1.3).
3. **The nearest published objects are diagnostics.** The effective number of clusters $G^\ast$, which on the printed definitions equals $1/\sum_g s_g^2$ with $s_g$ cluster $g$'s share (Carter, Schnepel and Steigerwald, as reported in Cameron and Miller 2015, eq. 27), with a simulation finding of size distortion when $G^\ast < 20$ in Cameron and Miller's account and a sharp rise below about 10 in Carter et al.'s section 3 (L2 part 2, item 10; L1 S5, section 3.3), and the conditional bias of Beaumont, Haziza and Ruiz-Gazen (2013).
4. **The survey response to a dominant unit is design.** Practice is to take such units with certainty in a take-all stratum (Hidiroglou and Lavallée 1988; the Insee guide), not to assume them away, and no numeric share rule was found (L1 S5, section 3.3, item 5).

**What this means for the paper** `[proposed]`. A bound $v_h \le v_0$ cannot be presented as a condition the literature supports for a given $v_0$. Regime B's few-cluster numbers (E1) should be reported with the unbounded column as primary, and the bounded column as conditional, with each task's observed largest share and $G^\ast$ printed beside the $v_0$ used, so a reader can see how far the data are from it. The take-all design (E7) is the paper's answer when one donor dominates.

**What the final runs test (stage F3).** On every task, the required units per cluster for regime B under round 9's box class (`code/scripts/round9_fewcluster_box.py`, `docs/round09/i03/fewcluster/round09_fewcluster_stratified.md`) at each population's own observed $(\Lambda_0, v_0)$, the bounded column, and at the same $\Lambda_0$ with $v_0 = 1$, the unbounded column, together with the design simulation's coverage of the regime B interval at each of those $m$. Also each task's largest share and $G^\ast$ per gene, and whether coverage tracks $G^\ast$.

## 5. D5, the scale of the estimands (settled by testing)

The conventions are recorded in L1 S1S2, row S2.4. HEST-Benchmark's target is $\log(1 + y)$ of raw counts, the corpus offers $\log(1 + 10^4 y/n)$ with $n$ the spot's total (its `lognorm`), the single-cell literature recommends the shifted logarithm of size-factor-normalized counts with a stated pseudocount (Ahlmann-Eltze and Huber 2023; Lun 2018), and donor-level inference in the pseudobulk tradition works on summed counts or log counts per million (Squair et al. 2021; Crowell et al. 2020). For imaging panels normalization by the panel total can distort expression (Atta et al. 2024), and pseudo-spots have no cell areas. The final runs carry the two scales the data already provide, $\log(1 + y)$ and the corpus's $\log(1 + 10^4 y/n)$, on every inference cell, and report the difference (stage F2). The paper says which scale is primary once the difference is known, which is Nicolas's decision with David.

## 6. The other recommendations the brief asks for `[proposed]`

1. **R7-7, the balanced design.** Keep the default, `rej_t` where its support and one balance variable allow, the cluster-level cube design with the constant balanced as the alternative, both rerun in the final runs on the same footing. Fuller (2009) advises stratifying first and rejecting second, and Chen et al.'s cube-active design under-covered on 2 of 11 datasets (L2 part 1, section 1, item 8), so the paper reports `rej_t`'s failure with two balance variables and few residual degrees of freedom as a limit.

Claim nothing about the correction as the project's own.
3. **O5, the paper's positioning.** Nothing classical or in print is claimed. The estimator is credited to Mickey (1959) and Williams (1961), who constructed it independently, with Mickey the earlier in print (L2 part 2, section 1). Among the recent papers checked, two cite Mickey, Kwon, Yang and Kim (2026, section 1) and Zhang, Sanguiao-Sande and Lee (2025, p. 406), and Cochran (sections 6.15 and 7.11, with Rao's 1969 comparison) and Särndal et al. (section 7.3.1) cite him too (L2 part 1, section 1, item 1). The paper should cite Mickey for the estimator and his (4.4) for the form of the variance estimator. ## 7. Prediction P10.2, scored
