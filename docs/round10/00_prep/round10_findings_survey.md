## 1. The two questions the project started from

The proposal of September 2026 (`docs/round00/first_year_ST_project_proposal.md`, sections 2 to 4) posed two questions about histology-to-expression prediction in spatial transcriptomics, both statistical rather than biological, both answerable on HEST-1k because it pairs measured expression with the prediction.

**Topic A.** For a frozen encoder and a head trained on HEST tasks, produce for each held-out spot $i$ and gene $g$ a prediction interval $C_{ig}$ with $\mathbb{P}(y_{ig} \in C_{ig}) \ge 1 - \alpha$, and characterise how coverage behaves when the held-out patient comes from a different institution or technology. The proposal asked whether the intervals implied by a model are honest, whether they degrade across sites, and whether the encoder ranking by Pearson matches the ranking by calibration.

**Topic B.** For a scalar $\theta$ defined on the joint law of expression and a covariate available from H&E alone (the examples were the correlation of nuclear area with expression, the difference in mean expression between neoplastic and stromal spots, and a slope on a morphology feature), give a confidence interval that is valid whatever the predictor's accuracy and narrower than the labelled-only interval when the predictor is good, with a small labelled set of spots and a large unlabelled set. The proposal noted that prediction-powered inference assumes independent observations, that spots in a slide are correlated and share a slide effect, that the effective sample size is closer to the number of slides than the number of spots, and that naive PPI would under-cover. The deliverable named was an answer to how many measured slides are needed to make H&E-only inference trustworthy.

The record's framing moved three times. On 24 September, after round 3, the oversight chat recommended one applied paper on inference and design for predicted expression with the donor as the unit, for an applied statistics journal (`docs/round04/00_prep/round3_directions.md`, section 5). On 28 September the originality check found the methods in both tracks to be existing methods, judged that "the problem is a paper whose only contribution is a new application", and reframed the paper as "Labelling budgets for prediction-powered inference with clustered data", methodology first, for an ML venue (`docs/round04/00_prep/round4_originality_check.md`, sections 2 and 3). On 25 October, after round 8 had found every formula classical or in print, option A, a design and empirical paper with every component cited to its classical source, was offered and deferred for two searches for a methodological core (`docs/round09/00_prep/round09_direction_scan.md`, section 4; `docs/round09/masterplan.md`). Neither search found one.

### 2.5 Prediction itself, and the two lines that left this repository

Round 6 found that a size-limited text cheatsheet written by an agent lifts a language model above its zero-shot baseline but not to the pathology head (0.041 to 0.158 against 0.184 on held-out CCRCC patients, at a cost of 15.99 US dollars), and that line is now `NicWYZ/textual-st-histology`. Round 7's federated learning track found that a one-round ridge head from per-center sums equals the pooled fit to machine precision, that accuracy rises steeply to 4 to 8 training donors and slowly after (0.061, 0.119, 0.137, 0.147 at 1, 4, 8 and 17 donors on CCRCC), that pooling across real sources helped one source and hurt one, that spot-level differential privacy costs 0.0225 at $\varepsilon = 10$ and 0.107 at $\varepsilon = 1$ against 0.219, and that partial gene overlap changes within-slide Pearson by $-0.0231$ to $-0.0105$ within centers. That line is now `NicWYZ/ST-Fed-Learn` (`docs/progress-log.md`, 12 and 13 October; `results/round7/fedlearn/F3_anchor_attempt2/f30_summary.json`; `results/round7/fedlearn/V1/CCRCC/v1_summary.csv`; `results/round7/fedlearn/V3/v3_paired.csv`; `results/round7/fedlearn/V4/CCRCC/v4_paired.csv`; `results/round7/fedlearn/V4/INDIANA_KIDNEY/v4_paired.csv`; `docs/round07/i03/fedlearn/round07_fedlearn_final_decisions.md`, section 2).

## 3. The cautionary tales

### 3.2 The other lessons, each tied to its failure

- **A number typed from memory beside an accurate citation.** Round 2's most recurrent defect, and the second case was in a message correcting the first. It is why every number is read back from its file (hard rule 2) and why the numeric-claim sweep exists (`docs/WAYS_OF_WORKING.md`).
- **A headline from one corner of a grid.** Round 4's Q3 report and round 7's first S4 memo both drew a conclusion the full grid reversed, the second with unmatched and unsupported cells (`docs/WAYS_OF_WORKING.md`; `docs/progress-log.md`, 14 October).
- **A weak comparator makes a constant look like a predictor.** Round 4's regime-B gains on Visium were a level term; the constant predictor is now the primary control (`docs/round05/README.md`).
- **Fresh agents catch what the author cannot.** Fifteen independent checks are listed in the progress log, most with a count of findings, and among much else they caught a wrong conclusion and a reversed tissue reading, both after a first commit, which is why a check now finishes before a document is committed (`claude/round10_survey_E_workflow_lessons.md`, section 7(a); `docs/progress-log.md`, 15 October).
- **Reporting before checking.** The oversight chat told Nicolas the Williams finding before the clipping's effect was measured, and first told him four survey-side papers were new when one had been found in round 3 (`docs/progress-log.md`, 19 October).
- **Labels that do not mean what their names say.** "Institution shift" was a resolution contrast within one lab, and HEST's patient labels were wrong in both directions. Every variable that differs between arms is listed before a term is named (`docs/environment.md`; `docs/round01/README.md`).
- **Narrower but under-covering is not a result.** The probe's own scoring says so, and round 9's critical-value note warns that the paper should not present a positive method it does not have (`results/round9/probe/predictions_scored.md`; `docs/round09/i03/fewcluster/round09_fewcluster_critical_value.md`, section 1).
- **A code defect can survive several rounds unseen.** Round 4's `var_textbook` computes a variance that grows with the level of the predictions, which round 9 found by rerunning round 5's code (B10). The inflation lifted coverage toward nominal, which hid it, and whether the intervals reported in rounds 5 to 7 carry it is still unchecked.
- **Tool and access limits bound every literature verdict.** OpenAlex, Semantic Scholar, Crossref and arXiv were rate-limited or blocked, checkers sat on approval cards, papers were read through the browser on the Mac, and ten, four and nine sources were never obtained (`docs/round08/i01/positioning/round08_positioning_note.md`, section 1.3; `docs/round09/README.md`, carried forward).
- **Compute.** About 35 CPU-hours died on a final parquet write, queue waits of 2 h 40 min to 5.5 hours came from oversized asks and 16 h walls, Longleaf was unreachable on 26 October, and the harness clock counts queue time (`docs/environment.md`).
- **Unsupervised sessions drift.** Round 9's checkers saved about 28 paper full texts to the Claude Science artifact store against instructions, the branches removed the standing rules, and the close check found Williams called adopted when it was only proposed (`docs/round09/README.md`).
- **Frozen documents keep withdrawn claims.** The cost is stated rather than hidden, and each round guide lists what later rounds withdrew (hard rule 4).

## 4. The paper the record would write today

### 4.1 The methods-first draft, as it stands

Round 9's track fewcluster wrote an outline and a first draft, "How few labelled clusters are enough? Limits and labelling budgets for prediction-powered inference with clustered data" (`docs/round09/tracks/fewcluster/paper_draft_v0.md`). Its theory section is Theorems 1 to 3 with the required-clusters corollary, its method section the certified critical values and Williams' estimator, its design section the round 7 comparison, and its applications CCRCC, INDIANA_KIDNEY, LUNG_XENIUM and ACS. Prediction sets are deferred to a separate paper. By its own outline's measure the draft is in a weak position. The position note calls the theory "sharp and apparently new" but "modest", and "on its own a short note rather than a paper"; the outline says that without a usable interval "the paper's new content is a negative result with design consequences"; and claim K, the usable interval, ended partly answered with no certified value at the real tasks' sizes (`docs/round09/i02/fewcluster/round09_fewcluster_position.md`, section 4; `round09_fewcluster_paper_outline.md`, section 6). Every novelty verdict in it is provisional and stays so while the decision of 6 November on paper reads stands.

### 4.2 The applied paper the findings support

Read against the two original questions, the record supports a different paper, the one recommended on 24 September and offered as option A on 25 October. Every method in it is cited to its source, and its contribution is the application, the measured answers and the design guidance. In outline, with the status of each part.

1. **Motivation.** Prediction accuracy has a low ceiling and predictions are used downstream as if measured (the proposal's section 2; D2, D3). The donor is the unit of every uncertainty statement, spot-level intervals are off by an order of magnitude, and the valid prediction set costs about twice the width at 10 calibration donors and about 1.4 times at 20 (D9, B1, A4; `results/round4/conformal/C3_real/c3_report_numbers.csv`). These are empirical results on classical theory.
2. **Data.** Three audited HEST tasks (CCRCC 24 donors, INDIANA_KIDNEY 25, LUNG_XENIUM 15) with per-spot leave-donor-out predictions from three encoders and morphology covariates, and ACS PUMS 2018 income by state as a second, public domain. The donor audit, the patient-label quirks, the depth factor and INT5 are facts a user of HEST-1k must know (D6, D10 to D14). All of this is empirical.
3. **Prediction sets for a new donor** (Topic A). Naive spot-level conformal under-covers for a new donor and coverage is a property of the calibration design, not the encoder (A1, A2). Feature reweighting cannot repair it (A3). HCP is the valid method with no labelled target spots and stays the narrowest up to 5 labelled spots at 10 calibration donors, the full conformal set inside the target donor takes over from 9 labelled spots, and GHCP becomes competitive only once there are 12 or more calibration donors, which gives a measured map over $K$ and $o$ and a practical rule for when a few spots of the new donor are worth labelling (A4, A6). INT5 is the case a hierarchical method misses (A10). The methods are in print or classical, and the map is the project's own and provisional.
4. **Valid inference with predicted expression** (Topic B). PPI as the model-assisted difference estimator under two-stage sampling (B2), with cross-fitting or Williams' exactly unbiased form, a fixed or cross-fitted coefficient, the finite-population factor and a $t$ reference (B5, B9, B10), the form C within-cluster estimator and the constant predictor as control (B6). What predictions buy on each task, little on Visium and a good deal on lung and census, and why, through cluster-level accuracy (B4, B7). The methods are classical and the answers empirical.
5. **How many donors, and which.** The coverage shortfall at 4 to 12 labelled donors on real tasks, its cause in skewed donor contributions (B8, B11), and the frontier that says when a finite-sample guarantee is possible at all, with the required number of clusters (B13). Regime A against regime B with costs (E1), the allocation formula (E2), balanced selection with `rej_t` and the cube design against simple random selection, weighting and k-center (E3 to E6), and the probe and take-all design for the atypical donor (E7). The frontier is the one piece of theory that arose from a problem the project met in practice. The designs and formulas are classical or in print, the measured comparisons are the paper's own, and the regime B numbers at few clusters (E1), the joint design (E8), the prediction-set map (A6) and the coverage floor (A8) are the project's own and provisional.
6. **Limits and cautions.** Spot-weighted estimands, the level term, same-cluster tuning, bootstraps, superpopulation intervals at moderate $G$, the published few-cluster comparators at four clusters (B5, B6, B12). These are negative results on methods in print.

What this paper still lacks is stated in `round10_reframing.md`, section 4. In short, the inference numbers of rounds 5 to 7 were produced with an estimator and a variance that round 8 and round 9 corrected, and have not been rerun; the estimands' normalization is undecided (D5); whether regime B can be bought on a spatial platform has not been asked (Q1); and the venue and the advisors' view of an applied paper are open.
