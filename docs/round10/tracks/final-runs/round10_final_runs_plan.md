### 5.2 F2.1, regime A, the design simulation

Script `code/scripts/round10_final_runs_f2_regimeA.py`, importing `round10_final_runs_estimators.py` from the same snapshot. A population is one (task, encoder, scale, estimand, gene), that is a vector of $G$ cluster values $z_g$ with predictions $f_g$. For each population and each $n_L$ the driver draws $R$ simple random samples of $n_L$ clusters without replacement, and for each draw one random split of the labelled clusters into folds of sizes $\lfloor n_L/2 \rfloor$ and $\lceil n_L/2 \rceil$. Every arm is evaluated on the same draws and splits. The seed of each population is `zlib.crc32` of `task|encoder|scale|estimand|gene|n_L`. The truth is the population mean of $z_g$, and the nominal level is 0.90.

- **$n_L$.** $\{4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 16\}$ below $G$, and on corpus tasks with $G > 32$ (kidney Visium only) also 20 and 25, which is $\lfloor G/2 \rfloor$.
- **Analytic arms, at $R = 20{,}000$ draws** (round 9's count) on every population.
  1. `classical`, the labelled mean with $(1 - n_L/G)s_z^2/n_L$ and $t_{n_L - 1}$.
  2. `fix1`, the difference estimator with coefficient 1 and $(1 - n_L/G)s_{z - f}^2/n_L$, $t_{n_L - 1}$. The paper's primary estimator.
  3. `xf2`, the two-fold cross-fitted form of decisions section 1.1 with round 5's clipped coefficient, at $n_L \ge 6$, with the four variances $\hat V_w$ ($t_{n_L - 2}$), $\hat V$, $\max(\hat V, \hat V_w)$ and $\frac{1}{2}(s_A^2 + s_B^2)$. $\hat V$ and the maximum use $t_{n_L - 2}$ as well, and a draw with $\hat V < 0$ forms no interval under $\hat V$. The same form with the coefficient unclipped and with it fixed at 1 runs beside it, since the module carries those rules.
  4. `fix1_nofpc` and `xf2_nofpc`, the same with the finite-population factor dropped (S6-3). On the four tasks in hand the first is also the superpopulation interval of item 8 below.
  5. `bh`, `bh_t` and `bh_t_fpc`, the module's Broska and Howes comparators.
  6. Controls. `fix1` and `xf2` with a constant predictor ($f_g = \bar F$ for every $g$), where `fix1` must equal `classical` exactly in every draw, a check that stops the job if it fails. And with a permuted predictor, $f$ permuted across clusters once per population with a crc32 seed, where coverage should stay near nominal and width should not fall below `classical`'s.
- **Limits arms, at $R = 2{,}000$ draws** (round 5's `n_sub`), on every population. Same-sample tuning by round 4's rules (a) `a_b1` and (b) `b_cluster`, and the cross-fitted rule (c) `c_crossfit_design`, through round 4's `lambda_rule` and `estimate` and round 5's `design_whole_clusters(..., lin=True, xf=True)`, called unchanged from the snapshots F1.2 and F1.3 used. Rule (a) tunes on the spot i.i.d. variance, so these arms take per-donor sufficient statistics from the builder rather than the donor values alone. Kluger et al.'s cluster bootstrap through round 7's `kluger_draw` at round 7's bootstrap count.
- **Bootstrap arms, at $R = 2{,}000$ draws and $B = 999$ resamples**, on every task under `uni_v2`, both scales, and the estimands $\theta_3$, $\theta_2$ and $\mu$, each around `fix1`. They are the pairs percentile bootstrap over labelled clusters, the wild cluster bootstrap-$t$ with Webb's six-point weights on the `fix1` residuals, and Rao and Wu's rescaled bootstrap-$t$ with $n_L - 1$ clusters resampled and the rescaling that carries the finite-population factor (ledger, S6-2). The other encoders are not run for these arms, which keeps their cost within a few jobs, and the report says so.
- **Per cell** the driver writes bias, its Monte Carlo standard error, SD, root mean squared error, coverage with its standard error $\sqrt{p(1 - p)/R}$, mean and median width, the share of draws that formed no interval, and for every variance estimator its mean over draws divided by the empirical variance of the estimate, with the fraction of draws where $\hat V < 0$.
- **Anchor, written before the run.** On CCRCC, INDIANA_KIDNEY and LUNG_XENIUM under `uni_v2` and $\log(1 + y)$ for $\mu$, the driver run with round 9's generator order reproduces `i34_williams_summary_hest.csv` for `FIX1` and `W_within` exactly, and on ACS `i34_williams_summary_acs.csv`. The module already passed this at F1.1, so this anchor checks the driver's population handling. A second anchor reruns round 5's E4b reference cell (CCRCC, $\theta_3$, $n_L = 8$, `c_crossfit_design`, `textbook_t|fpc|lin|xf`) through the limits arm and must give `coverage_median` 0.85775 at round 5's seed.
- **Sizing.** A smoke test on one CCRCC gene at every $n_L$ and arm measures time and memory before the production jobs. Production runs as one job per (task, encoder) for the analytic and limits arms, and one job per task for the bootstrap arms, at 4 CPUs, with time and memory set at three times the smoke test's.

### 5.3 F2.2 and F2.3, the switch rule and the fixed coefficient's cost

- **The switch rule is evaluated first**, from the `xf2` rows alone, before any other F2 table is read. For every cell with $n_L \ge 6$ (task, encoder, scale, estimand) the aggregation computes the median over genes of $E(\hat V_w)/\text{Var}(\hat\theta)$ and the fraction of draws with $\hat V < 0$. If the median is below 0.95 in any cell, the primary variance of the cross-fitted form becomes $\max(\hat V, \hat V_w)$ on every task, and the report says so. The ratio's Monte Carlo error is taken from a bootstrap over draws, and a cell whose median lies within two standard errors of 0.95 is listed as such. The rule decides as written, and the listing is given only as information.
- **The cost of the fixed coefficient.** Per task, encoder and scale, at every $n_L$, the share of genes where `fix1`'s median width exceeds `classical`'s. Since both widths scale with the same factor, this is the share of genes where $E(s_{z - f}) > E(s_z)$ over draws, and the aggregation checks the two readings agree.

## 5c. Option (c), a corpus covariate from what the corpus carries

*Written on 10 November 2026, before any search.* Under the brief's section 7 and the F1 decision file, item 2, option (c) starts with a literature search by sub-agents across survey sampling, econometrics and machine learning, with the queries logged. The search is reported to Nicolas at gate F2, and no covariate is derived or built before he has seen it and agreed the gap is genuine.

**The question the search answers.** The estimands $\theta_1$ to $\theta_3$ need a spot-level covariate that is computed from no label and has a biological reading, as nuclear area and neoplastic share do. What the corpus carries without CellViT is the H&E patch each spot's embedding was computed from, the embeddings themselves, the spot coordinates, the expression panel and library size (which are labels and so excluded), and HEST's sample metadata. The search asks three things.

1. **Image-derived covariates without a trained segmentation model** (computational pathology and machine learning). Which label-free spot or patch features computed directly from H&E pixels, such as colour deconvolution into haematoxylin and eosin channels, haematoxylin optical density, or texture statistics, are used as proxies for nuclear density, nuclear size or tumour content, and how well they agree with segmentation-based counts where both were measured.
2. **A covariate taken from the predictor's own input** (survey sampling and machine learning). What the model-assisted and prediction-powered literature says about estimands defined through a covariate that is also an input to the predictor (for example a principal component of the embedding), for design-unbiasedness and for the reading of the estimand.
3. **Association estimands under cluster sampling with a derived covariate** (survey sampling and econometrics). Design-based inference for a regression slope or a domain difference when the domain or the regressor is itself an estimated or misclassified quantity, and whether this changes the estimand or only its interpretation.

**How.** Three sub-agents, one per field (`Pathology` 0907eef7-a0c0-4d0c-9261-7d9f437cf409, `Survey` d24387cf-ee0b-4127-a924-d18f82c9ed88, `Econometrics` 4fdd907f-4a4f-44ab-89ad-fe7d61f81fbe, dispatched on 10 November with each id in a second message), under one written brief that gives the three questions, the ledger's existing rows to start from (`round10_ledger.md`, S2.4 for scales and D9 for clustering, so the search does not repeat them), and the rules of `docs/EXECUTION_RULES.md`, section 4 (literature) and section 6.
