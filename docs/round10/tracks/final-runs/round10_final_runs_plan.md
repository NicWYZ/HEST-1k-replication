### 5.2 F2.1, regime A, the design simulation

Script `code/scripts/round10_final_runs_f2_regimeA.py`, importing `round10_final_runs_estimators.py` from the same snapshot. A population is one (task, encoder, scale, estimand, gene), that is a vector of $G$ cluster values $z_g$ with predictions $f_g$. For each population and each $n_L$ the driver draws $R$ simple random samples of $n_L$ clusters without replacement, and for each draw one random split of the labelled clusters into folds of sizes $\lfloor n_L/2 \rfloor$ and $\lceil n_L/2 \rceil$. Every arm is evaluated on the same draws and splits. The seed of each population is `zlib.crc32` of `task|encoder|scale|estimand|gene|n_L`. The truth is the population mean of $z_g$, and the nominal level is 0.90.

- **$n_L$.** $\{4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 16\}$ below $G$, and on corpus tasks with $G > 32$ (kidney Visium only) also 20 and 25, which is $\lfloor G/2 \rfloor$.
- **Analytic arms, at $R = 20{,}000$ draws** (round 9's count) on every population.
  1. `classical`, the labelled mean with $(1 - n_L/G)s_z^2/n_L$ and $t_{n_L - 1}$.
  2. `fix1`, the difference estimator with coefficient 1 and $(1 - n_L/G)s_{z - f}^2/n_L$, $t_{n_L - 1}$. The paper's primary estimator.
  3. `xf2`, the two-fold cross-fitted form of decisions section 1.1 with round 5's clipped coefficient, at $n_L \ge 6$, with the four variances $\hat V_w$ ($t_{n_L - 2}$), $\hat V$, $\max(\hat V, \hat V_w)$ and $\frac{1}{2}(s_A^2 + s_B^2)$. $\hat V$ and the maximum use $t_{n_L - 2}$ as well, and a draw with $\hat V < 0$ forms no interval under $\hat V$. The same form with the coefficient unclipped and with it fixed at 1 runs beside it, since the module carries those rules.
  4. `fix1_nofpc` and `xf2_nofpc`, the same with the finite-population factor dropped (S6-3). On the four tasks in hand the first is also the superpopulation interval of item 8 below.
  5. `bh`, `bh_t` and `bh_t_fpc`, the module's Broska and Howes comparators.
  6. Controls. `fix1` and `xf2` with a constant predictor ($f_g = \bar F$ for every $g$), where `fix1` must equal `classical` exactly in every draw, a check that stops the job if it fails. And with a permuted predictor, $f$ permuted across clusters once per population with a crc32 seed, where coverage should stay near nominal and width should not fall below `classical`'s.
- **Limits arms, at $R = 2{,}000$ draws** (round 5's `n_sub`), on every population. Same-sample tuning by round 4's rules (a) `a_b1` and (b) `b_cluster`, and the cross-fitted rule (c) `c_crossfit_design`, through round 4's `lambda_rule` and `estimate` and round 5's `design_whole_clusters(..., lin=True, xf=True)`, called unchanged from the snapshots F1.2 and F1.3 used. The other encoders are not run for these arms, which keeps their cost within a few jobs, and the report says so.

### 5.3 F2.2 and F2.3, the switch rule and the fixed coefficient's cost

- **The switch rule is evaluated first**, from the `xf2` rows alone, before any other F2 table is read. For every cell with $n_L \ge 6$ (task, encoder, scale, estimand) the aggregation computes the median over genes of $E(\hat V_w)/\text{Var}(\hat\theta)$ and the fraction of draws with $\hat V < 0$. If the median is below 0.95 in any cell, the primary variance of the cross-fitted form becomes $\max(\hat V, \hat V_w)$ on every task, and the report says so. The ratio's Monte Carlo error is taken from a bootstrap over draws, and a cell whose median lies within two standard errors of 0.95 is listed as such. The rule decides as written, and the listing is given only as information.
- **The cost of the fixed coefficient.** Per task, encoder and scale, at every $n_L$, the share of genes where `fix1`'s median width exceeds `classical`'s. Since both widths scale with the same factor, this is the share of genes where $E(s_{z - f}) > E(s_z)$ over draws, and the aggregation checks the two readings agree.

**(i) Regime A against regime B, cost-matched as in round 5's E3.** The code is `code/scripts/round10_final_runs_f3_regcomp.py`.

- **Design.** As round 5's E3 part `regcomp` (`round5_ppi_e3_twolevel.py`, line 56). Budgets are $B \in \{2400, 4800, 9600\}$ and cost ratios $c = c_d/c_s \in \{10, 100, 1000\}$. - **Regime A's estimator.** Round 5's code has no two-stage form with the coefficient fixed at 1 at both stages, since its `_tl_fixed` fixes only the within coefficient. F3 uses, for the donor-weighted mean,

$$
\hat\theta_A = \bar F_U + \frac{1}{n_L}\sum_{g \in L} \bar e_g, \qquad \bar e_g = \frac{1}{m_g}\sum_{i \in s_g}(y_i - f_i),
$$

where $\bar F_U$ is the mean over all $G$ donors of each donor's mean prediction. Its variance is the two-stage form of round 5's regime A applied to the fixed-coefficient residual,

$$
\hat V_A = \left(1 - \frac{n_L}{G}\right)\frac{s^2_{\bar e}}{n_L} + \frac{1}{G\,n_L}\sum_{g \in L}\left(1 - \frac{m_g}{M_g}\right)\frac{s^2_{e,g}}{m_g},
$$
