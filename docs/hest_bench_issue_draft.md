## Title

Sample-level metadata issues in HEST-bench affecting patient-split interpretation (IDC, COAD,
READ), plus within-task gene-panel heterogeneity

## Body

Thank you for HEST-1k and for shipping the benchmark in a form that can be run end to end — we
reproduced Table 1 across 12 encoders and 9 tasks with no cell exceeding a 0.03 tolerance before
starting any of the work below, so what follows comes from a replication that matches yours.

While building a variance decomposition on top of HEST-bench we needed the `patient` field to mean
"distinct donor", and found three places where it does not. Two are metadata issues we think are
worth correcting; one is a property of the data that we think is worth documenting. We also have
three questions we could not resolve from public sources.

### 1. IDC: TENX95 and TENX99 are one donor, and the patient split leaks

`sample_metadata` assigns TENX95 and TENX99 the labels `patient 2` and `patient 1`. Both come from
the 10x Genomics dataset **"FFPE Human Breast using the Entire Sample Area"**, whose page carries
`"donorCount":1` in its own embedded metadata and describes the material as

> 5µm sections from a formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded (FFPE) human breast resected tumor mass
> sample

— plural sections, singular sample — presenting the two as "Replicate 1"
(`Xenium_V1_FFPE_Human_Breast_IDC_Big_1`) and "Replicate 2" (`..._Big_2`), with the stated purpose
of demonstrating "the high reproducibility of data generated by the platform".

So IDC has **three donors, not four**, and on 2 of its 4 patient folds the held-out sample's own
donor is still represented in training.

We measured what that is worth. Holding the test slide fixed and the training-set size fixed at
7,015 spots, and varying only whether the same-donor section is available for training:

| encoder | held out | replicate in training | replicate withheld | difference |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| H-optimus-0 | TENX95 | 0.6342 | 0.5758 | +0.0583 |
| H-optimus-0 | TENX99 | 0.4700 | 0.3607 | +0.1093 |
| UNI v2 | TENX95 | 0.6264 | 0.5984 | +0.0281 |
| UNI v2 | TENX99 | 0.4673 | 0.3750 | +0.0923 |
| ResNet50 | TENX95 | 0.5036 | 0.4551 | +0.0485 |
| ResNet50 | TENX99 | 0.4039 | 0.3500 | +0.0539 |
| | | | **mean** | **+0.0651** |

Positive in 6 of 6 encoder–slide cells. IDC's whole `random − patient` gap is 0.1210, so the
replicate accounts for **54%** of what the patient split scores as generalisation to a new patient.

Source: `results/round2/R3_splits/replicate_leak__*.csv`, `docs/r5_idc_provenance.md`.

The IDC task itself remains a valid prediction task and the shipped 50 target genes are unaffected.
What is affected is reading IDC's patient-split score as cross-patient generalisation.

### 2. COAD: the patient field collapses distinct patients and leaves one sample unlabelled

TENX147, TENX148 and TENX149 all carry `Patient 1`, and TENX111 carries no patient label at all.
The consequence is that COAD's shipped patient split does not separate patients, and any per-task
term computed from it cannot be read as a patient effect. In our decomposition COAD's
patient-identity term came out as by far the largest of any task, which was initially reported as a
striking same-patient result until the labels were checked — it is an artefact of the holdout
removing more than one patient's slides.

Source: `results/round2/R1b_heads/d3_coad_patient_rows.csv`, `r3_per_task_terms.csv`.

### 3. READ: the "patients" are same-specimen replicate pairs

ZEN36/ZEN40 (`Patient 7`) and ZEN48/ZEN49 (`Patient 1`) are replicate sections of one specimen
rather than merely two slides from one patient. The label is not wrong, but a same-patient term
computed on READ is a same-*specimen* term and an upper bound on a patient effect. Documenting this
would prevent the reading we initially made.

Source: `r3_per_task_terms.csv`.

### 4. Samples within a task do not share a gene panel

The shipped 50 target genes per task are the variance-ranked top 50 of the gene set common to
**all** samples in the task, held-out samples included. Because the panels differ between samples,
this has two consequences that are not documented:

- The target list's construction depends on the held-out sample's panel, so a leakage-free
  within-fold selection is not well defined without it.
- On four tasks, a selection made from training samples only can name genes the held-out slide
  does not measure at all.

The heterogeneity is substantial — PAAD's three samples share 159 genes against a union of 919 —
and within IDC the four samples each carry 541 entries but only 500 in common:

| sample | entries | control probes | real genes | extras over the 500-entry intersection |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| NCBI783 | 541 | 253 | 288 | 33 control + 8 `antisense_*` probes |
| NCBI785 | 541 | 220 | 321 | **41 real add-on genes** |
| TENX95 | 541 | 261 | 280 | 41 control probes |
| TENX99 | 541 | 261 | 280 | 41 control probes |

NCBI785 measures 41 real genes that none of the other three measure; only TENX95 and TENX99 have
identical panels. The shipped 50 IDC targets all lie inside the 500-entry intersection, so the task
itself is unaffected.

Source: `r2_panel_heterogeneity.csv`, `r5_idc_panels_observed.csv`, `r5_idc_panel_pairs.csv`.

### Three questions, not claims

1. **NCBI784** appears in HEST-1k's metadata but not in the 4-sample IDC benchmark task. If it is
   the other replicate of Janesick et al.'s Sample #1, then the benchmark already excludes one
   same-donor replicate pair while including another — was the IDC pair an oversight, or is there
   a reason we are missing?
2. **Table A4** reports n = 4 Xenium samples for the Janesick publication, while the GEO subseries
   lists 3 and `atlas.py` names 3. Which is right?
3. **H&E scan provenance.** Scan resolution varies 5.02× across the 72 benchmark samples and is
   aligned with patient identity in PRAD, SKCM and PAAD, so those patient folds are also resolution
   folds. We could not find a scanner model or nominal magnification for any IDC sample in the 10x
   pages, the Janesick methods, or the GEO records — GEO's `Sample_scan_protocol` gives only
   "Xenium In Situ Analyzer", the fluorescence instrument. Is the brightfield scan provenance
   recorded anywhere?

### What we are not claiming

We are not claiming these affect the benchmark's headline conclusions or any encoder ranking. Items
1 to 3 affect the interpretation of patient-split scores as cross-patient generalisation; item 4
affects what a leakage-free target selection could mean. We are happy to open a PR adding a
`donor_id` column and the panel-heterogeneity note if that would be useful.
