# Round 7, federated learning for expression prediction, and few-cluster designs for the PPI paper (13 to 15 October 2026, closed)

Round 7 ran two tracks from `main`. Track `fedlearn` (stages F0 to F4, gate tags `round7-fedlearn-F2` and `round7-fedlearn-final`) reviewed the literature on federated learning for predicting expression from histology and ran four scoping experiments, after the advisor meeting of 12 October gave Nicolas that assignment (`round07_advisor_directions.md` (at tag `record-full-2026-11`)). Track `ppi` (stages S0 to S5, gate tags `round7-ppi-S2`, `round7-ppi-S4` and `round7-ppi-final`) compared ways of choosing a few labelled clusters with design-based intervals, and the published few-cluster comparators, after an overlap check (`00_prep/round07_overlap_check.md`) and a full reading of the closest PPI papers (`oversight/round07_ppi_positioning.md`). The federated learning line has continued in `NicWYZ/ST-Fed-Learn` since 26 October. `docs/round10/00_prep/round10_findings_survey.md` lists the round's findings with status labels (rows B12, E3 to E6 and section 2.5), and the paper's numbers are now read from `results/round10/final_runs/F4/paper_numbers.csv`.

Throughout the inference findings, coverage counts a draw in which no interval is formed as a miss, nominal is 0.90, widths are ratios to simple random selection (D0) with the classical interval on the same cells, and "matched, supported" cells are those where every design is computed on the same task, arm, estimand and $n_L$ and rejective selection has a support of at least 1,000 (`i03/ppi/round07_ppi_final_report.md`, section 10).

## What the round established, as it now stands

### Choosing the labelled clusters (track ppi)

1. **Weighting by simulated inclusion probabilities fails under rejective selection.** Under round 5's rejective design D2, some clusters are almost never accepted ($\hat\pi_g = 0$ from 100,000 draws in 225 of 1336 real-task cells) and some pairs never appear together (665 cells), so the Horvitz-Thompson and Hájek means are biased or unstable and the Sen-Yates-Grundy variance loses terms (`results/round7/ppi/S1_weighting/_merged/s1_masking_diag.csv`; `i01/ppi/round07_ppi_S2_report.md`, section 6.1; `i02/ppi/round07_ppi_S2_decisions.md`, section 2). `rej_t` stays D2's interval. The mechanism is classical and the unit-level case is in print (Legg and Yu 2010), and simulated inclusion probabilities and zero pair probabilities have antecedents the round did not cite (Fattorini 2006, Aronow and Samii 2013; `docs/round08/i01/positioning/round08_positioning_note.md`, section 3.6). Round 10 did not rerun it, and its cell counts are those of the rejective rows of that file (`docs/round10/i05/final-runs/round10_final_runs_report.md`, section 7 item 5). Aronow and Samii's conservative variance was not tried.

2. **The designs compared on matched, supported cells.** At $n_L = 8$ and $p_a = 0.01$, rejective selection with `rej_t` covers 0.8885 at width 0.456 on census (4 cells) and 0.861 at 0.888 on tissue (18 cells), where D0 classical covers 0.875. A cluster-level cube design with inclusion probabilities bounded below by half of $n_L/G$, balanced on $\pi_g$, the constant and $\bar f_g$ (S4b), with the Hájek mean and the Sen-Yates-Grundy variance, covers 0.885 at 0.883 on tissue and 0.885 at 0.568 on census, where the D0 tuned interval is 0.894 at 0.531. Balancing the constant halved the cube design's census width, from S3's 0.912 (`results/round7/ppi/S5_numbers/run1/s5_matched.csv`; `i03/ppi/round07_ppi_final_report.md`, sections 6.2 and 10; `i04/ppi/round07_ppi_final_decisions.md`, section 2 items 2 and 3). Caveats. On tissue `rej_t` covers 0.852 to 0.866 at $n_L \ge 6$, below D0, and 0.798 at $n_L = 4$, where only $p_a = 0.1$ has support. The cube design's primary interval is not formed at $n_L = 4$, so it is stated for $n_L \ge 6$, its Horvitz-Thompson mean is unusable on census (width 3.484 of D0 classical), $\hat N = G$ never holds exactly, its residual-form variance is too small, and every cube result rests on one score, one $\tau$, the design target and one skewed simulation law. `rej_t` fails with two balance variables at few residual degrees of freedom (round 5). **Changed by round 10.** Round 10's rerun of the same designs, with its own code on its own matched cells at $p_a = 0.1$ on the $\log(1 + y)$ scale, gives `rej_t` 0.8845 at 0.603 and the S4b cube design 0.8825 at 0.687 on census, and 0.859 at 0.960 and 0.878 at 1.012 on tissue (`results/round10/final_runs/F4/paper_numbers.csv`, keys `E3.*`). Round 7's file gives, at $p_a = 0.1$, 0.8965 at 0.459 and 0.885 at 0.568 on census and 0.8635 at 0.893 and 0.885 at 0.883 on tissue. Round 10's prediction that the ordering of designs on round 7's 18 tissue cells is unchanged was refuted at $p_a = 0.1$ and held at $p_a = 0.01$, and its report says that the estimator code, prediction files, cluster values and number of draws differ between the rounds (`docs/round10/i04/final-runs/round10_final_runs_F3_report.md`, sections 4 to 6). The record does not explain the difference in `rej_t`'s census width. `rej_t`'s variance is Yang, Qu and Li's (JASA 2023) Corollary A5 at the cluster level, and cube selection of primary units with simulated pair probabilities is in print (Breidt and Chauvet 2011, Chauvet 2011), so what remains is the matched comparison at 4 to 16 clusters (round 8's note, sections 3.1 and 3.6).

3. **The mixture of rejective and simple random selection is a control.** With $\epsilon = 0.5$ it gives the most reliable design-based coverage (0.878 to 0.886 on tissue, 0.907 to 0.935 on census) at a width near D0's on tissue (0.98 to 1.03), so bounding every inclusion probability below makes a design-based interval valid after balance and costs most of balance's gain (final report, section 10). The S4 report's proposal of it as the paper's design was not accepted (`i03/ppi/round07_ppi_S4_decisions.md`, section 2 item 4). Only $\epsilon = 0.5$ was run.

4. **Greedy k-center is a negative result.** Computed exactly over the $G$ random starts, the classical interval applied as if simple random covers 0.326 to 0.458 on the two kidney cancer tasks with `hoptimus0` and `uni_v2`, the unbiased $\hat\theta_{\mathrm{HT}}$ is 1.38 to 3.33 times as variable as D0's classical mean, and a median share of 0.732 of pairs at $n_L = 4$ and 0.431 at $n_L = 8$ is never sampled together, so no design-based variance exists (`results/round7/ppi/S4_kcenter/_merged_r2/s4_report_numbers.csv`; `i02/ppi/round07_ppi_S4_report.md`, section 6.3). The principle is classical (Neyman 1934). Round 10 did not rerun it.

5. **Published few-cluster comparators under-cover.** For the design target at $n_L = 4$, Broska and Howes's interval as published covers 0.704 on tissue and 0.717 on census, and Kluger et al.'s cluster bootstrap 0.618 on tissue (uniform design), with 0.565 to 0.808 for the superpopulation target at $n_L$ of 4 to 8. With the divisor $n_L - 1$ and a $t_{n_L - 1}$ quantile (`bh_t`), Broska and Howes's interval covers 0.884 on tissue and 0.882 on census at $n_L = 4$, narrower than the classical interval there (0.867 and 0.444). From $n_L = 8$ it over-covers the design target and is wider than the cross-fitted tuned interval on tissue (1.108 against 0.977), and its finite-population version `bh_t_fpc` under-covers (`results/round7/ppi/S5_numbers/run1/s5_bh_vs_tuned.csv`; final report, sections 6.3 and 10). The correction is classical (Cameron and Miller 2015; Bell and McCaffrey 2002) and was not found published for PPI (round 8's note, section 3.9). Round 10 reran the comparators over HEST and ACS cells and gives 0.699 for the published interval and 0.875 for `bh_t` at $n_L = 4$, and 0.7145 for the cluster bootstrap (`paper_numbers.csv`, keys `S6.bh.n4`, `S6.bh_t.n4`, `B5.kluger_ptd.n4`).

6. **Round 5's worst $\theta_2$ cells were draws with no interval.** `rej_t` forms no interval when the valid labelled clusters leave fewer than 2 residual degrees of freedom, which happens in about a quarter of Indiana's draws at $n_L = 6$, and round 5 counted such draws as misses. In the Indiana cell, round 7's coverage over formed draws, 0.882591, times its share formed, 0.741, gives round 5's 0.654 (`i02/ppi/round07_ppi_S2_decisions.md`, section 3). Outside lung the two rounds agree once counting is the same, to a largest difference of 0.0272 over 368 cells (`results/round7/ppi/S2b_diagnostics/theta2_recount/recount_r2/s2b1_numbers.csv`). Round 5's statement that rejection fails with two balance variables at few residual degrees of freedom stands with this mechanism, and every $\theta_2$ coverage is reported with its share of draws that formed an interval. The lung $\theta_2$ differences at $n_L$ of 6 and 8 are read as ties at a float32 threshold and not checked.

7. **The gains come from the predictor.** With the constant or the permuted predictor no design narrows the interval (`i04/ppi/round07_ppi_final_decisions.md`, section 2 item 8).

8. **The inference intervals compared here used round 5's estimator.** Rounds 8 and 9 corrected that estimator (round 8's summary, `docs/round08/README.md`), and the paper's estimator is now the difference estimator with the coefficient fixed at 1, with the two-fold cross-fitted form beside it from 6 labelled clusters (`docs/round10/round10_methods_note.md`, item 3). Round 10 found that round 5's interval with round 4's linearisation term is 0.982 to 1.004 times the width of Williams' form at 8 labelled clusters on four tasks (`docs/round10/i05/final-runs/round10_final_runs_report.md`, section 5, FR1).

### What the paper can claim (the oversight readings of this round)

The overlap check found that a group member's unpublished manuscript on choosing which slides receive an assay makes no inferential claim, so the paper's direction stands (O1), and that the paper can no longer say PPI under cluster sampling is untreated, since Kluger, Lu, Zrnic, Wang and Bates (arXiv:2501.18577, January 2025) give bootstrap intervals under clustered designs and Broska and Howes (arXiv:2610.09601, 7 October 2026) give cluster-robust PPI++ (`00_prep/round07_overlap_check.md`, section 3). The full check, with the manuscript's details, is kept outside the repository under hard rule 8.

The positioning note (`oversight/round07_ppi_positioning.md`) holds the round's full reading of Broska and Howes, Chen et al.'s balanced active inference and the cluster parts of Kluger et al. These readings stand. Broska and Howes's design formula at whole-cluster labelling gives round 4's gain-theorem endpoint $1 - R^2_{\text{cluster}}\,G_U/(G_U + n_L)$ and its shrunken coefficient (section 2.2). With the unlabelled clusters' term included, regime B never loses on the between-cluster term for a superpopulation target, which supersedes round 4's condition for when it wins (section 2.3; `docs/round04/tracks/ppi/round4_ppi_theory.md`, section 4.3). Broska and Howes have no small-sample correction and cover 0.88 at about 113 labelled clusters (section 2.4). Kluger et al. already weight labelled clusters by known probabilities under independent Bernoulli selection (section 3).

**What does not stand.** The positioning note's section 5 and the round's results explainer called the design-target finite-population factor and cross-fitting term, the design-target regime comparison with costs and the level term, the allocation of a budget between clusters and units, and the balanced selection around `rej_t` the paper's own. Round 8 withdrew every formula among them. The cross-fitting term is the omission of the factor $1 - n/G$ that Williams' (1961) estimator carries, the regime comparison and the level term are classical, the allocation formula is classical (Snijders 2001, Raudenbush 1997), and `rej_t`'s variance is in print (`docs/round08/README.md`). The positioning note's section 5 and section 6 recommendations are therefore a record of what was believed on 13 October, and the note itself recorded on 15 October that its few-cluster claims were too broad once `bh_t` was found to cover at four clusters. What remains the paper's own from this round is empirical: the matched comparison of designs at 4 to 16 clusters, and the measured coverage of the published comparators there (findings 2 to 5).

### Federated learning (track fedlearn)

The review searched seven themes (695 search-log rows, 219 query identifiers), kept 406 papers and cites 91, 72 of them read at full text. An audit found claim-level mismatches in 22 cited papers, all fixed (`results/round7/fedlearn/F1_search/`, `results/round7/fedlearn/F2_audit/`; `i01/fedlearn/round07_fedlearn_F2_report.md`, section 3). Within the sources searched, one paper federates expression prediction from histology (PPML-Omics), and none fits a head on frozen foundation-model embeddings from per-center summary statistics or handles centers with different gene panels. The algebra of such a head is established, so novelty could rest only on the application (`tracks/fedlearn/round07_fedlearn_literature_review.md`, sections 7 and 12). The inventory found 205 samples on disk and two usable source contrasts, kidney Visium and breast Xenium, each mixing lab with study and disease.

On HEST-1k with a ridge head on 256 principal components of frozen embeddings (`i02/fedlearn/round07_fedlearn_final_report.md`, sections 6 and "What the federated learning track established"; `i03/fedlearn/round07_fedlearn_final_decisions.md`, section 2):
- The head fitted once from per-center sums equals the pooled fit to $7.6 \times 10^{-15}$ and reproduces round 1's benchmark at 0.263995 against 0.264 (`results/round7/fedlearn/F3_anchor_attempt2/f30_summary.json`).
- Within-slide accuracy with `uni_v2` on CCRCC is 0.061, 0.119, 0.137 and 0.147 at 1, 4, 8 and 17 training donors, rising steeply to about 4 to 8 donors and slowly after, and still rising at the largest pools held (`results/round7/fedlearn/V1/CCRCC/v1_summary.csv`).
- Pooling three pseudo-centers that differ only in donors beats one by 0.016 to 0.038. Across the two real two-source federations pooling helped one source, left two within 0.003 and cost the kidney atlas 0.021, and a head carried from one source to another lost 0.05 to 0.17 (`results/round7/fedlearn/V2_run2/`). The 10x breast gain rests on one held-out slide per draw from 7 slides.
- Spot-level differential privacy on the released sums costs 0.0225 at $\varepsilon = 10$ and 0.107 at $\varepsilon = 1$ against 0.219, and clipping alone 0.0004; the split between noise and the larger penalty was not measured, and protecting a whole patient would cost more (`results/round7/fedlearn/V3/v3_paired.csv`).
- Fitting each gene only from the centers that measure it, when each of three centers measures half of 50 genes, costs 0.010 to 0.023 within centers (`results/round7/fedlearn/V4/*/v4_paired.csv`), and the review found no method for it.
- Center intercepts do not change the within-slide metric, and over the pooled spots of several centers they help slightly across real sources and hurt on pseudo-centers. The mechanism offered for this is not checked.

The project's data do not show that federation across real centers improves accuracy, nothing is shown beyond three centers or outside HEST-1k, and no novelty is claimed. The oversight reading at the close was that partial gene overlap is the better first method for a federated paper (`i03/fedlearn/round07_fedlearn_final_decisions.md`, section 4 item 1).

## What was tried and did not work

- Inclusion-probability weighting under rejective selection, for the reason in finding 1.
- The S3 cube design without the constant on census (width 0.912), and its residual-form variance, which is too small and fails the synthetic test at $G \le 24$ (`i03/ppi/round07_ppi_S4_decisions.md`, section 2 item 5).
- The Horvitz-Thompson mean under either cube design on census, because $\hat N/G$ stays a few percent from 1 and carries the level of income. A landing that keeps the constant to the end was not run.
- Greedy k-center, and the finite-population version `bh_t_fpc` of the corrected Broska and Howes interval.
- Comparing designs on unmatched or unsupported cells. The S4 gate's first reading did so and drew the opposite conclusion on tissue, and a first explanation of the cube design's census loss by its landing phase contradicted the balance residuals. Both were reversed before the S4 memo was handed over, which is why every design comparison here is on matched, supported cells.
- Explaining round 5's $\theta_2$ failures by a fallback to a last candidate. Round 5's diagnostics show it never fell back, and the difference is the counting in finding 6.
- In federated learning, the centred arm cannot be tested with one left-out center, since the centred and federated predictions then differ by a constant per gene that neither metric sees.
