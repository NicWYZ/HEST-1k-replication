# New directions from the advisor meeting of 12 October 2026

Recorded on 13 October 2026 by the oversight chat, from Nicolas's account in chat the day after the meeting. It is a record of what he reported, not a transcript, and the advisors have not reviewed it. Section 3 is the oversight chat's reading and is marked as such.

## 1. What was said

The meeting discussed round 6's cheatsheet experiments (`docs/round06/README.md`). David explained why he had proposed them. The current way of predicting expression from histology, in which a pathology foundation model turns H&E whole-slide images into numerical embeddings and a prediction head is fitted on the embeddings, has reached a bottleneck in accuracy. The project's own literature search had found the same. The best average correlation on HEST-1k moved from 0.41 to 0.42 over two years and 25 models (`docs/round05/00_prep/deck2/deck2_outline.md`), and the round-3 handoff records the ceiling at about 0.42 (`docs/round03/00_prep/round3_oversight_handoff.md`, section 3, item 1).

**David's proposal, a textual knowledge base.** Writer agents record the relationship between histology and expression for each pair of image and expression in a large set of training data. The records build a textual database, the scaled-up version of round 6's cheatsheets, which encodes extensive knowledge of how histology relates to expression. Predictor agents draw on the database when they predict expression from a histology image.

**Dr. Zhu's objection and proposal, federated learning.** Dr. Zhu objected that encoding pathology images as text loses information. He proposed federated learning instead, gathering training data in the form of summary statistics at scale from centers around the world, with the aim of improving model performance without having to obtain more explicit training data such as HEST-1k.

**Nicolas's assignment for the week.** A literature review of the viability of federated learning for predicting expression from histology. The end goal is a methods paper that proposes such a method and advocates adopting it and continuing research in this direction. The paper is preliminary and a proof of concept, not a technically complex methodology paper.

## 2. What follows for the project

- Federated learning becomes a new line of work, with its own paper, alongside the PPI paper. It does not replace the PPI and conformal work.
- Its first round is a literature review with quick validation and scoping experiments. It is round 7's track `fedlearn` (`docs/round07/masterplan.md`).
- Nicolas decided on 13 October that the detailed literature review is done by a Claude Science session and its sub-agents, and that the oversight chat sets the scope and writes the instructions.
- When the session cannot retrieve a paper's full text, it gives Nicolas a list of at most 10 papers with access links, and he uploads them. Each link uses UNC Chapel Hill's institutional access, and where that is not available, the link goes to a free version of the paper.
- On 13 October Nicolas also decided that the review has two gates, one after the literature review with a proposed experiment design and one after the experiments, and that it includes a short section on the alternatives a paper advocating federated learning has to argue against, David's textual knowledge base among them.

## 3. What the project already holds that bears on the two views (the oversight chat's reading)

- **Round 6 tested a small version of the textual route.** With a size-limited cheatsheet written by a separate agent, Opus 5.5 did not beat the `uni_v2` head fitted on the same spots. In the extension on held-out kidney cancer patients, every rubric-bound cheatsheet from 256 to 65,536 bytes beat the zero-shot baseline, accuracy did not rise with size, and none reached the head, at 0.041 to 0.158 against 0.184 (`docs/round06/i06/cheatsheet/round06_cheatsheet_C7_decisions.md`, section 2). This agrees with Dr. Zhu's point that text loses information against embeddings, in the setting tested. It did not test David's proposal at scale. Round 6 used one model and one trial per cell, the extension's writers learned from seven patients, and a writer's budget was about 60 montages of 12 patches (`docs/progress-log.md`, entry of 12 October, afternoon), while David's database would be written over a large number of training pairs.
- **The predictor's error has a large slide-level component.** Round 3 measured slide offsets of about half a standard deviation (`docs/round04/00_prep/round4_originality_check.md`, section 2), and rounds 4 and 5 showed that what predictions buy under cluster sampling is governed by their accuracy at the cluster level (`docs/round04/tracks/ppi/round4_ppi_theory.md`, section 2, and `docs/round05/i04/ppi/round5_ppi_E5_decisions.md`, section 3). In a federation the centers play the role of clusters, so differences between centers are the first thing a federated method has to handle. HEST-bench's within-slide correlation removes slide-level offsets by construction, so the metric a federation is judged by decides whether those differences matter.
- **Gathering summary statistics fits the current pipeline closely.** The HEST-bench head is principal components followed by ridge regression on frozen embeddings (round 1). For ridge regression computed exactly, each center's sums and cross-products of embeddings and expression are sufficient, so a fit pooled from summary statistics equals the fit on the pooled data (`docs/round07/i01/fedlearn/round07_fedlearn_track.md`, section 2.3). Whether more data from more centers would raise accuracy at all is the question the literature review and the first experiment have to answer.
