# F1 RRRIDGE independent check

## Verdict
Not in print as far as I could find, and not an immediate corollary of the Ding-Yang theorem or of Benaych-Georges and Nadakuditi. A real, but short, new step is needed. The numerical formula itself is right to within finite-size error.

## Literature (17 queries, 6 papers downloaded)
Ding (2020) treats deterministic low rank plus white noise. Ding and Yang (2021) treat $Y=A^{1/2}XB^{1/2}$ with $X$ having independent entries and $A,B$ deterministic. Benaych-Georges and Nadakuditi (2012) need bi-unitarily invariant noise, or perturbation vectors independent of the noise. Kargin (2015) treats the OLS fit $P_XY$ in the regime $\gamma\le1$ and gives outlier singular values and a Tracy-Widom limit, with no singular vector overlap and no ridge. Bing, Ning and Xu (2022) use a multivariate ridge step but only give non-asymptotic rates. Green and Romanov (2025) analyse PCR risk for one response. The forward citations of Kargin, Ding and Bing-Ning-Xu (70 titles scanned) contain no ridge fitted-value BBP paper, and searches on ridge or regularised CCA, projected or instrumented PCA and multi-output ridge returned only methods papers. Absence in search is weak evidence, and I did not read the proofs of Ding-Yang or Benaych-Georges and Nadakuditi line by line.

## Why Ding-Yang does not apply directly
Write $Y=\sqrt\theta\,\tilde x v^\top+W$ with $\tilde x=Xu$. Rows of $Y$ are iid $N(0,I+\theta vv^\top)$, so $F=HY=H Z\,B^{1/2}$ with $B=I+\theta vv^\top$ and $Z$ iid. This has exactly the Ding-Yang form with $A=H^2$, but only if $H$ is independent of $Z$. Here $H$ is a function of $X$, and $Z$ contains the column $Xu$. I computed the threshold that the independent-$H$ model would give. The spike condition is
$$1+\frac{\theta}{1+\theta}\,z\,m(z)=0 \quad\text{at the right edge } z_+,$$
with $m$ the Stieltjes transform of $Z^\top H^2Z/n$, taken from the theory script's own bulk solution. The values at $\lambda=1$ are 0.594, 0.375 and 0.331 for $\gamma=0.5,2,4$ at $c=0.1$, against the claim's 0.184, 0.260 and 0.289, and PCA of $Y$ at 0.316. So treating $H$ as independent makes ridge worse than PCA, while the dependent model makes it better. The simulation below agrees with the claim, not with the independent-$H$ value. The dependence is therefore the whole effect, and no cited theorem covers it. This calculation is my own derivation and is not a quoted theorem.

## What the new step is
Condition on $X$. Then $F=av^\top+HW$ with $a=\sqrt\theta HXu$ deterministic and $HW$ having left covariance $H^2$ and right-isotropic noise. One needs (i) an anisotropic local law or deterministic equivalent for quadratic forms of $a$ against the resolvent of $HWW^\top H$, which is available for separable models with deterministic $H$ (Ding-Yang style, Knowles-Yin), applied conditionally on $X$, and (ii) the almost sure limit of the weights of $a$ in the eigenbasis of $H$, which follows from rotational invariance of $X$. The overlap formula then needs a derivation of the derivative term rather than the factor $c$ fixed by matching the PCA case. The note's own admission that this factor was not derived independently is still open. The nuisance-factor variant is a second spike with the same structure.

## Numerical check
$n=1500$, 20 replicates per cell, Gaussian $X,W$, $\lambda\in\{0.01,1\}$, $\gamma\in\{0.5,2,4\}$, $c\in\{0.1,0.3\}$, 11 values of $\theta$ per cell from 0.4 to 4 times the predicted threshold (132 rows in f1check_numeric.csv). For $\theta\ge1.3\theta^*$ the largest absolute gap between simulated and predicted $|\langle\hat v,v\rangle|^2$ is 0.049, and it exceeds 0.03 in only 4 of 12 cells, all at 1.3 to 1.7 times the threshold. For $\theta\ge2.5\theta^*$ the gaps are at most 0.018. Below the predicted threshold the simulated overlap is smooth and positive (up to 0.11 at $0.9\theta^*$ and 0.14 at $0.97\theta^*$), the usual finite-size rounding of order $n^{-1/3}$, so the threshold location agrees to within that rounding. A finite-size scaling study to pin it down more tightly was not run. PCA of $Y$ shows the same rounding, with gaps up to 0.14 against its own theory.

The advantage over PCA of $Y$ is clearly visible at $\lambda=1$: for $\theta$ between 1.05 and 1.35 times $\theta^*$ the simulated ridge overlap exceeds the PCA overlap by 0.07 to 0.20 at $\gamma=0.5$ and $\gamma=2$, and by 0.04 to 0.06 at $\gamma=4$. At $\lambda=0.01$ the advantage is large for $\gamma=0.5$ (0.06 to 0.20) and negligible for $\gamma=2$ and 4 (below 0.02), as expected since $H$ is then close to the identity or a projection.

## Recommendation
The result is not stated in the sources I found, but it is a modest extension: the genuine contribution is the conditional deterministic equivalent for a design-dependent signal, and a derivation of the overlap factor. It is a defensible short note rather than a main result. Before claiming it, check Bao-Ding-Wang-Wang matrix denoising and the Ding-Yang proofs for a theorem allowing a deterministic signal with a general left covariance $A$.
