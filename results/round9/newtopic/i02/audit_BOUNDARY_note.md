# Audit of the BOUNDARY stop (27 October 2026)

Verdict: stop confirmed, with two corrections to the subagent's reasoning and a narrower statement of what survives.

## What I read
I read the note, the plan and the simulation CSVs. I recomputed the pooled rejection rates from f_BOUNDARY_sim_null.csv and they match the note (selected boundary, local linear: 0.055, 0.056, 0.212 at h=2,3,5; RBC 0.133, 0.083, 0.110). I extracted the text of Cattaneo, Titiunik and Yu (CTY) arXiv 2505.05670 (location-based theory) and 2511.06474 (survey), and read their assumptions, Theorems 1 to 3 and the implementation section. For robust bias correction I used search excerpts of Calonico, Cattaneo and Farrell (arXiv 1809.00236, Remark S.2.1 and the bias expression). I did not read Porter and Yu (paywalled) beyond abstracts and secondary descriptions.

## Does prior art cover it?
1. CTY do not cover it. Their setting is iid sampling, a fixed piecewise-linear boundary, a deterministic smooth mean function, and a vanishing bandwidth. They state that spatial dependence is future work. Nothing there concerns a boundary estimated from data.
2. Porter and Yu do not cover it either. They estimate a cutoff when a true jump exists, and the cutoff converges fast enough for an oracle property. Here the null has no jump, so the estimated boundary is a selection event on a continuous field and its error does not vanish. Different problem, as the note says.
3. The failure of q=p+1 RBC is not a new phenomenon in mechanism. The bias expression of Calonico, Cattaneo and Farrell shows that with equal bandwidths the residual bias after correction is a term in the (p+2)th derivative, here the third derivative of the profile. The note's own odd profile makes exactly that term large, because the second-derivative jump that RBC estimates is zero. So the over-rejection is the known higher-order term of RBC under a profile whose width is comparable to h. The note says it did not diagnose this; the diagnosis is in print.
4. The honest-confidence-interval literature (Armstrong and Kolesar, Econometrica 2018; Kolesar and Rothe, AER 2018; Imbens and Wager 2019) gives non-asymptotic bias-aware intervals under a bound on the second derivative. That is a ready remedy for the odd-profile bias and I could not verify it in this session (cited from memory, not opened). Post-selection inference after segmentation (Hivert et al., Jewell et al.) is same-data and does not address thinning.

## Classification
The statement "local-linear jump bias at a Y1-selected boundary is 2 c3 b3 h^3 with b3 from the Slepian profile" is class (c), a genuine but small new step. Conditional on the thinned part, the test is a CTY fixed-boundary regression (corollary of CTY Theorem 1 and 2 bias expansions applied conditionally on the estimated curve, step: Y2 independent of Y1 given lambda). The new content is only the conditional mean profile of the field given selection, a Gaussian Slepian calculation, plus a random-field smoothness condition. CTY's smoothness assumption fails for Matern nu=0.5, so a bound needs a modulus-of-continuity version. That is an afternoon-to-weeks proposition, not a paper.

## Does the remaining result hold up?
Two weaknesses cut against the subagent. First, the derivation is not validated at the bandwidths that matter: the predicted sign is wrong at h<=3 (mean bias over se is -0.39 and -0.11 at h=2,3 and +0.73 at h=5 by my recomputation), and the correlation with prediction is 0.20 at h=3. A theorem would have to explain this, and it is unexplained. Second, the near-nominal size at h<=3 is partly an artefact of the HC1 variance being conservative (the independent-boundary control rejects 0.01), so size is "not inflated" while bias is still about 0.5 se. This does not rescue a paper, because the practical remedy is simple (h below the profile width, or a bias-aware interval).

## Is "testing at a boundary estimated on a thinned copy" a paper?
Not as a null-continuity test. The null is true by construction for a continuous field, the test has power 0.10 to 0.17 at h=3 even with a true jump of 0.8 (my recomputation of the power CSV, local linear, selected boundary), and the interesting object is a real jump with an estimated boundary. That version (oracle property when boundary error is o(h), curve-estimation rates of Korostelev-Tsybakov type, thinning fraction trade-off) is a plausible extension of Porter-Yu to 2D, but it follows from CTY plus known boundary-fragment rates, and I found no result that makes it more than a corollary-plus-simulation.

## Strongest surviving claim and venue
A short proposition: conditional on the selection event, the local-linear jump bias equals an odd-profile term of order h^3 with explicit coefficient, size is controlled by h relative to the profile width, and equal-bandwidth RBC leaves this term uncorrected. Venue: a section or appendix of an applied spatial-transcriptomics methods paper (Bioinformatics, Biostatistics application). Not NeurIPS, ICML, AISTATS or a statistics journal on its own.
