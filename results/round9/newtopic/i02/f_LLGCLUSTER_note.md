# F4 LLGCLUSTER, feasibility note (27 October 2026)

## 1. What was checked and what the literature says

The anchor is Chen, Kelly and Malamud, "Limits To (Machine) Learning" (arXiv 2512.12735). I read the arXiv v1 of 16 December 2025 and the AFA draft of 18 February 2026, which sits at afajof.org (access was requested and granted). Assumption 1 is the same in both texts: the noise is iid with a finite fourth moment and independent of the signals and of the predictable component, while the predictable component and the signals are left unrestricted. The word "cluster" does not occur in either text and "panel" does not occur either. The bound is $R^2_*\ge (R^2_{OOS}+\hat L)/(1+\hat L)$, where $\hat L=\frac1T\operatorname{tr}(\hat\Psi_{OOS}\hat\Psi(zI+\hat\Psi)^{-2})$ for ridge, and Theorem 3 gives a one-sided normal limit at rate $T^{1/2}$.

I ran 19 logged queries (web search, arXiv API, Crossref, Semantic Scholar; the arXiv phrase queries mostly returned nothing and Semantic Scholar was rate limited, as in interval 1). No paper was found that extends the bound to grouped data. The nearest print is Atanasov, Zavatone-Veth and Pehlevan (ICML 2025, arXiv 2408.04607). It gives sharp ridge risk asymptotics for arbitrary correlation across samples in the covariates and in the noise, and it works through the fact that the only rotationally invariant scalar functional is the trace. I read the abstract and appendix passages, not every proof. Rabinowicz and Rosset (JASA 2022) give the bias of cross-validation under correlated noise. EigenPrism, HEDE, Hu and Li, and the Nakagawa and Schielzeth R2 are about signal-to-noise or variance-share estimation with iid or independent noise and have no lower bound from a fitted predictor. Gu-Kelly-Xiu, Kelly-Malamud-Zhou and Mundlak were not searched in depth. Candidates and queries are in the two CSVs.

## 2. Derivation in the simplest case

Take $y_i=f(x_i)+\eta_{d(i)}+e_i$ with $x_i\sim N(0,I_p)$, equal group sizes $n$, $\eta_d\sim N(0,\tau^2)$, $e_i$ iid with variance $\sigma_e^2$, and ridge fitted on the pooled data without demeaning. Write $\hat K$ for the linear map from training outcomes to test predictions, $Z$ for the $T\times G$ group indicator matrix, and $\Sigma=\sigma_e^2I+\tau^2ZZ^\top$ for the noise covariance. The anchor's step from its equation (21) to the bound uses only the expectation of a quadratic form. With the covariance $\Sigma$ the same step gives

$$
\mathbb E\, b_V=\sigma_e^2\,\hat L+\tau^2\,\hat L_Z,\qquad \hat L_Z=\frac1{T_{OOS}}\operatorname{tr}\big(\hat K Z Z^\top \hat K^\top\big).
$$

This step is routine algebra. The out-of-sample group effects come from new groups, so they are independent of the fit and enter only the irreducible term, and $b_I$ is negligible. With $V=E_{OOS}[y^2]$, $b_B$ the bias term of the anchor, and $\sigma^2=\sigma_e^2+\tau^2$, the out-of-sample error is $\sigma^2+b_B+\sigma_e^2\hat L+\tau^2\hat L_Z$. Substituting into the bound and defining the target $R^2_{tot}=1-\sigma^2/V$, which counts $\eta$ as noise, gives

$$
\frac{R^2_{OOS}+\hat L}{1+\hat L}-R^2_{tot}\;=\;\frac{\tau^2(\hat L-\hat L_Z)-b_B}{(1+\hat L)\,V}.
$$

When $x$ is independent of the groups, $S$ has a law invariant under left rotation, so $\mathbb E\operatorname{tr}(\hat K^\top\hat K\,ZZ^\top)=n\,\frac{G}{T}\operatorname{tr}(\hat K^\top\hat K)$ and $\hat L_Z=\hat L$ for equal groups. The bound then converges to the iid bound with total noise variance $\sigma^2$. It stays below $R^2_{tot}$ by $b_B/((1+\hat L)V)$, and $R^2_{tot}$ is itself below the within-group $R^2_{w}=\operatorname{Var}f/(\operatorname{Var}f+\sigma_e^2)$. So in this case the iid bound cannot over-claim the within-group $R^2$. This is a corollary of the anchor's own derivation and, as far as I can read it, of the trace result in Atanasov et al. with identity feature correlation and block noise correlation.

If instead the features carry a group mean shift, $x_i=\mu_d+z_i$, the group directions have large eigenvalues, ridge barely amplifies noise along them in the test fold, and $\hat L_Z<\hat L$. The display above then turns positive when $\tau^2(\hat L-\hat L_Z)>b_B$. The bound over-claims the total target without any dependence on $G$, because $\hat L_Z/\hat L$ is a leverage ratio, not a sampling error. The fluctuations are different. The out-of-sample $R^2$ contains the realised mean of $\eta_d^2$ over the test groups, which has relative standard deviation $\sqrt{2/G_{test}}$, so the anchor's $T^{1/2}$ normalisation understates the spread when $\tau^2$ is large.

## 3. Simulation

The simulation used Gaussian features, ridge with $z$ set to 0.1 times the mean non-zero eigenvalue, $p=1.5N$, group size 10 (equal) or $2+$ multinomial with Dirichlet(1) weights (unequal), $G\in\{10,25,50\}$, $\operatorname{Var}f=0.2$ of unit total variance, $\tau^2\in\{0,0.1,0.3,0.6\}$, 150 replications per cell, fixed seeds, train and test on disjoint fresh groups. Two designs were run, with features independent of the groups (A) and with a group mean shift of per-coordinate variance 0.5 in the features (B), each with an iid twin in which the group effect is replaced by per-row noise of equal variance. Monte Carlo errors are in the CSV (column mc_se_gap); the standard error of a mean gap is about 0.005 to 0.02.

1. In design A without demeaning the mean gap $B-R^2_{tot}$ is between $-0.03$ and $-0.08$ in every cell. At $\tau^2\ge0.3$ the share of replications with $B>R^2_w$ is at most 0.17 (0.06 or less for $G\ge25$ at 0.3). The prediction that the bound over-claims the within-group $R^2$ at shift share 0.3 is refuted in this design.
2. In design B the ratio $\hat L_Z/\hat L$ measured by direct computation is 1.00 with Monte Carlo error 0.007 or less in A and 0.28 in B at every $G$ (file f_LLGCLUSTER_amplification_ratio.csv). The mean gap against $R^2_{tot}$ is $+0.03$ to $+0.04$ at $\tau^2=0.3$ and $+0.12$ to $+0.14$ at 0.6, with standard error 0.005 to 0.015, so the over-claim is real and does not shrink with $G$. For $G=50$ the share of replications with $B>R^2_{tot}$ reaches 0.97 against 0.16 for the twin. The display above with the measured ratio and $b_B\approx0.1$ reproduces the size (I did not measure $b_B$ separately, so this is a consistency check only). Against the within-group target the over-claim is small ($+0.01$ to $+0.04$ at $\tau^2\le0.3$, and negative at 0.6), and it is present already at $\tau^2=0$, so it does not come from the shift.
3. In design A the standard deviation of $B$ relative to the iid twin is 1.4 to 1.7 at $\tau^2=0.6$ and close to 1 for $\tau^2\le0.1$. The share of replications with $B-1.645\,\mathrm{sd}_{twin}>R^2_{tot}$ is 0.02 to 0.07 in A and up to 0.73 in B.
4. For the demeaned fit, which is the checker's equal-size reduction, the iid bound on within-demeaned data is valid for the within target in every cell (the gap is $-0.08$ to $-0.15$) and far looser than the pooled bound.
5. Replacing $\hat L$ by $\min(\hat L,\hat L_Z)$ is valid for any $\tau^2/\sigma_e^2$ without estimating it, since $\sigma_e^2\hat L+\tau^2\hat L_Z\ge\sigma^2\min(\hat L,\hat L_Z)$. In B it removes the over-claim (share above target 0 to 0.09) but is very conservative (mean gap $-0.19$ to $-0.36$). A tight version needs the variance ratio.
6. My group-mean regression attempt (features $\bar x_d$, outcome $\bar y_d$, heteroskedastic noise) gave $\hat L\approx0.06$ and a bound near zero in every cell, whether or not I whitened, so it does not test the unequal-size claim. I report it as not tested.

## 4. Data checks

Semi-synthetic on real CCRCC uni_v2 embeddings (24 donors, $p=1536$, 12 train and 12 test donors, 30 spots per donor or sizes proportional to the real slide counts, 150 replications). The label-based donor share of variance in CCRCC averages 0.384 over the 50 genes (10th to 90th percentile 0.26 to 0.52), so 0.384 is the CCRCC-like shift. The mean gap against $R^2_{tot}=0.2$ is $-0.09$ (equal sizes) and $-0.03$ (unequal) at 0.384, so on real embeddings the bound does not over-claim on average. The standard deviation of $B$ is 2.5 and 2.2 times the twin's. Share with $B>R^2_{tot}$ is 0.34 and 0.51 against 0.31 and 0.29 for the twin. Using the twin's standard deviation as an iid-based standard error, the normal-approximation miscoverage of a nominal 5 percent lower bound is 0.14 (equal) and 0.19 (unequal) at 0.384, and about 0.2 at 0.6 (computed from the aggregates, not replication by replication).

ACS PUMS 2018 (3.2 million rows, 735 dummy columns, 25 training states and 26 test states, 40 rows per state, 200 splits; the between-state share of log-wage variance in a 300 000-row pool is 0.008). $\hat L=0.61\pm0.02$, $R^2_{OOS}=0.525$, $B=0.704$. The demeaned bound is 0.702, lower than the pooled one in 66.5 percent of splits. No truth is available, so validity is not tested.

Scoring of the data-check predictions in the published file. D1 (share above $R^2_{tot}$ at most 0.10 and mean below target): partly held, the mean is below but the share is 0.34 to 0.51, similar to the twin. D2 (within target over-claimed in at most 5 percent): refuted as stated, the share is 0.29 to 0.41 at 0.384 and 0.5 to 0.6 at 0, but the mean gap is negative at 0.384 and the twin shows the same behaviour at 0, so it is finite-sample noise. D3: held. D4 (real embeddings no less conservative than Gaussian exchangeable): held on the mean gap, since real embeddings give $-0.03$ to $-0.09$ and Gaussian variant B gives $+0.03$ to $+0.13$. D5: held, $\hat L$ ranged from 0.55 to 0.67. D6: refuted, the demeaned bound exceeded the pooled one in 33.5 percent of splits because the between-state share is under 1 percent. D7: not tested.

## 5. Stop rule and plan prediction

The first clause of the stop rule (the iid bound stays valid under group shifts) is not met: in Gaussian variant B it over-claims the total target by up to 0.14 and its spread is understated by the iid standard error. The second clause (the group version is the iid bound on demeaned data and group means, with no new term) is partly met. The new term is $\hat L_Z$, which is the anchor's equation (21) with a non-identity noise covariance, so I count it as a one-line generalisation. The ridge asymptotics that would make it rigorous are in Atanasov et al. The unequal-size and proportional-regime CLT piece I could not test. On the CCRCC-like semi-synthetic the over-claim of the point bound is absent on average. Outcome: **stop**. If someone pursues it, the remaining content would be a short proposition on $\hat L_Z$ and a variance-component estimate of $\tau^2/\sigma_e^2$ for a tight $\hat L_\alpha$, which is note-sized.

Plan prediction F4, scored: the bound fitted without demeaning over-claims within-donor $R^2$ at shift share 0.3 in simulation. Refuted for the within-group target in the Gaussian exchangeable case and on the real-embedding semi-synthetic, held only for the total target when the features carry the group shift, and held for the spread of the estimator.

## 6. What a paper would need

A proposition for $\hat L_\alpha$ with an estimator of $\alpha$, a CLT at rate $\sqrt G$ for the group part, an unequal-size analysis that works (my group-mean attempt was uninformative), a check that the result is not a corollary of Atanasov et al. for Gaussian designs, and real-data validation with ground truth.
