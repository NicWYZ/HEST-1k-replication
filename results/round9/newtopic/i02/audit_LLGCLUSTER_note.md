Audit of the LLGCLUSTER stop (27 October 2026)

Verdict: stop confirmed for the stated aim (a formal result of its own for a statistics venue). Two parts of the subagent's reasoning are loose and are corrected below, but neither rescues the track.

What I read. Chen, Kelly and Malamud (arXiv 2512.12735): Assumption 1, Proposition 1, equation (21), Theorems 2 and 3 in full. Atanasov, Zavatone-Veth and Pehlevan (arXiv 2408.04607, v6): setup, Lemmas III.3 and III.4, Theorems IV.1 to IV.3 and the correlation discussion. Not read: their appendices and proofs, the anchor's Appendix D (the proof of Theorem 3 and the variance estimator), and Rabinowicz and Rosset. One web search for an extension of the LLG bound to dependent noise returned nothing.

1. Is the L_Z over-claim immediate?
The anchor's Assumption 1 requires iid noise. Equation (21) replaces eps' M eps by sigma^2 tr(M), where M = (T/T_OOS) K'K. For noise covariance Sigma the same expectation step gives tr(M Sigma). That is a one-line generalisation, and the note's decomposition (sigma_e^2 L + tau^2 L_Z) follows from it with the test-fold group effects independent of the fit. So the exact finite-sample statement is category (b) in spirit, but it rests on a textbook quadratic-form identity, not on a named theorem. Atanasov et al. do not contain it as stated. Their model is Gaussian features with Kronecker covariance Sigma times K, Gaussian noise with covariance sigma^2 K', a deterministic linear teacher, and an uncorrelated test point. Their Theorem IV.3 allows K different from K', and its variance term has a factor df2_{K,K'} = tr[K K' (K+kappa)^-2]/T, which is the population analogue of L_Z. The note's design B (features with a group mean shift of variance 0.5 per coordinate, group-effect noise) is the case K = I + 0.5 ZZ', K' = sigma_e^2 I + tau^2 ZZ'. So in that Gaussian design the over-claim is a corollary of Theorem IV.3 together with the anchor's bound. Their result is about risk of a single estimator, not about a lower bound on R^2, and it carries no data-dependent L_Z, no estimator of tau^2/sigma_e^2 and no CLT. The stop therefore over-states the cover by calling it 'inside' Atanasov et al.; it covers the Gaussian-design mechanism only.

2. What is genuinely new, and how large.
(i) A counterexample showing the anchor's Theorem 2 fails under clustered noise when features carry the shift, with the exact bias formula. This is correct and checkable, and it is an erratum-sized contribution (design B, over-claim up to 0.14, does not shrink with G). (ii) A valid cluster bound. The min(L, L_Z) version needs no variance ratio but is very conservative (gap -0.19 to -0.36). A tight version needs a variance-component estimate and a CLT at rate sqrt(G) for the group part, with a quadratic-form variance estimator under block dependence. This is a real but routine step (cluster-robust variance of a quadratic form), a lemma-sized addition to the anchor's Appendix D, which I could not check. (iii) The unequal-size case: the subagent's group-mean attempt was uninformative and is untested, so nothing can be claimed.

3. The spread finding.
The 2.2 to 2.5 ratio (CCRCC semi-synthetic) and 1.4 to 1.7 (Gaussian) compare the sd of the bound with the sd of an iid twin. The anchor's own estimated standard error, its sigma-hat for R^2, was never computed, so the claim that the iid SE understates the spread is a statement about a proxy, not about the published procedure. By itself this is a robustness caveat for empirical use with few periods, not a publishable point; the mean-gap evidence on real embeddings is also negative (the bound does not over-claim on average at 0.384 shift). The finance transfer (serial correlation or heteroskedastic returns noise, small T) is the more natural direction, but the same one-line tr(M Sigma) algebra applies, so it is incremental too.

4. Strongest surviving claim.
Under dependent noise the LLG bound is invalid exactly by tau^2 (L - L_Z) minus the bias term, over-claiming whenever the features carry the dependence; min(L, L_Z) is a valid conservative fix and a variance-component version is tight; Gaussian-design confirmation follows from Theorem IV.3 of Atanasov et al. Evidence: simulation and CCRCC semi-synthetic only, no ground truth on real data.

5. Venue.
A short econometrics or finance-methods note or comment (Economics Letters level, or a working-paper response to the anchor), or a section inside a larger paper. Not NeurIPS, ICML or a top statistics journal. Stop confirmed.
