# F1 RRRIDGE, feasibility note (round 9, newtopic, interval 2)

## Verdict
Stop. In the rank-one isotropic case the ridge reduced-rank estimator does have a detection threshold strictly below the one for PCA of the outcomes, and the theory matches simulation. But every step of the derivation is an application of existing machinery (Marchenko-Pastur calculus for the ridge hat matrix, deterministic equivalents for a separable noise matrix, and rank-one outlier and overlap formulas of the Benaych-Georges and Nadakuditi type). The nuisance-factor variant is a second spike in the same formulas. I judge the limits to follow from published results by routine steps, which is the first branch of the plan's stop rule. The second branch, no threshold different from PCA, did not trigger. The verdict is provisional because several sources were read only as abstracts or snippets.

Absence is weak evidence.

## Derivation, Sigma = I, r = 1
Let $B=\sqrt{\theta}\,uv^\top$ with unit vectors, $X$ with iid N(0,1) entries, $E$ with variance one (theta is the signal to noise ratio), $\gamma=p/n$, $c=q/n$. Let $S=X^\top X/n$ with Marchenko-Pastur law $\mu_\gamma$. The ridge hat matrix is $H=X(X^\top X+n\lambda I)^{-1}X^\top$ with eigenvalues $h(s)=s/(s+\lambda)$ in the left singular basis of $X$. The fitted matrix is
$$F=HY=a\,v^\top+HW,\qquad a=\sqrt{\theta}\,HXu .$$
Conditional on $X$ this is a rank-one signal plus noise whose left covariance is $H^2$. Because $V^\top u$ is uniform, $a$ is delocalised in the eigenbasis of $H$ with weights proportional to $s\,h(s)^2$, and its energy is
$$\|a\|^2=n\,\theta\, m_a,\qquad m_a=\int \frac{s^3}{(s+\lambda)^2}\,d\mu_\gamma(s).$$
Write $t=h^2$, let $\nu$ be the law of the n eigenvalues of $XX^\top/n$ (so $\nu=\gamma\mu_\gamma$ on $s>0$ plus an atom $(1-\gamma)_+$ at zero), and rescale singular values squared by $n$, calling the rescaled variable $\zeta$. The deterministic equivalent for $ZZ^\top$, $Z=HW$, uses the scalar $\delta(\zeta)$ solving
$$\delta=\int \frac{t}{c\,t/(1+\delta)-\zeta}\,d\nu(s),$$
taken on the branch continued from $\zeta=\infty$. The outlier equation is that the product of the two resolvent quadratic forms equals the inverse signal energy,
$$\theta\, m_a\,\zeta\,A(\zeta)\,B(\zeta)=1,$$
$$A=\frac{\int s\,t\,(\zeta-c\,t/(1+\delta))^{-1}d\mu_\gamma}{m_a},\qquad B=\frac1c\Big[\int\frac{d\nu}{\zeta-c\,t/(1+\delta)}+\frac{c-1}{\zeta}\Big].$$
The cross term between the left and right resolvents vanishes at leading order because $v$ is delocalised for the right-invariant noise, which is the step I did not prove and took from the cited local-law literature. The detection threshold is the value of $\theta$ at which this equation has its root at the right edge $\zeta_+$ of the noise spectrum,
$$\theta_c(\gamma,c,\lambda)=\big[m_a\,\zeta_+A(\zeta_+)B(\zeta_+)\big]^{-1}.$$
Above it, with $D(x)=\phi_a(x)\phi_v(x)$ the product of the two resolvent forms in $x=\sqrt{\zeta}$, the squared alignment of the leading right singular vector is
$$|\langle\hat v,v\rangle|^2=\frac{-2\,c\,\phi_v(\rho)}{s^2\,D'(\rho)}$$
at the outlier $\rho$. The factor $c$ is a normalisation I fixed by requiring the formula to reproduce the closed forms in the iid case (see below); I did not derive it independently. For PCA of Y alone the same machinery gives the spiked covariance results
$$\theta_c^{\rm PCA}=\sqrt{c},\qquad |\langle\hat v,v\rangle|^2_{\rm PCA}=\frac{1-c/\theta^2}{1+c/\theta}.$$
Two limits serve as checks. For $\gamma<1$ and $\lambda\to0$, $H$ is the projection on the column space of $X$ and the equation gives $\theta_c=\sqrt{\gamma c}$, which is Kargin's OLS threshold in this notation (from the interval 1 record, not re-read). For $\gamma>1$ and $\lambda\to0$, $H=I$, so ridge-RRR equals PCA of Y and the code returns $\theta_c=0.448$ against $\sqrt{c}=0.447$ for $c=0.2$.

Evaluated numerically (f_RRRIDGE_threshold_grid.csv), the ratio of the ridge-RRR threshold to the PCA threshold at the best $\lambda$ on the grid is 0.58 at $\gamma=0.5$, 0.71 at $\gamma=1$, 0.82 at $\gamma=2$, 0.90 at $\gamma=4$ and 0.96 at $\gamma=10$ (c = 0.1). The ratio is below one for interior $\lambda$ at every $\gamma$ tried, tends to $\sqrt\gamma$ or to one as $\lambda\to0$, and for $\gamma=10$ exceeds one by about one percent at $\lambda=100$. The gain therefore decays as the number of features per sample grows. I did not find a closed form for the optimal $\lambda$ or threshold.

## Nuisance factor
Take $E=\sqrt{\psi}\,g\,\xi^\top+W$ with $g=Gf$ constant within donors and $\xi\perp v$. If $G$ is independent of $X$, then $F=a v^\top+\sqrt{\psi}\,Hg\,\xi^\top+HW$ has a second spike whose left vector is delocalised with weights $t$ and energy $n\psi\int t\,d\nu$. Ridge shrinks it by the average of $t$, while PCA of Y keeps all of it. The two spikes obey separate outlier equations, so the nuisance factor does not move the threshold of the predictable spike. It changes which singular vector is on top. A corollary-level consequence is that there are $(\theta,\psi)$ with $\psi>\theta$ where PCA returns the nuisance direction and ridge-RRR returns $v$. If the donor effect correlates with $X$, which is the realistic case in the project data, $Hg$ is no longer delocalised and this derivation does not apply.

## Simulation
f_RRRIDGE_sim_vs_theory.csv, $\lambda=0.1$, $n\in\{500,1000,2000\}$ with 20, 12 and 4 replicates, $\gamma\in\{0.5,1,2\}$, $c\in\{0.1,0.3\}$, five values of $\theta$, seeds fixed in the script. Where $\theta\ge0.8$ or $\theta\ge1.5\sqrt c$, the largest absolute gap between simulated and theoretical alignment is 0.048 at $n=500$, 0.012 at $n=1000$ and 0.035 at $n=2000$, with Monte Carlo standard errors of about 0.01. The $n=2000$ cells have only four replicates, so their gaps are noisy. Near the threshold finite-n smoothing dominates, for example 0.436 simulated against 0.420 predicted at $\gamma=0.5$, $\theta=0.3$, and one cell at $\gamma=2$, $\theta=0.3$ shows 0.204 against 0.042 (four replicates, below the ridge threshold of 0.41). The theoretical ridge-RRR alignment exceeds the PCA alignment in 87 percent of the 90 cells (in the other 12 cells both limits are zero, since theta is below both thresholds). The nuisance check (f_RRRIDGE_nuisance_sim.csv, 10 replicates, $n=1000$, 20 donors) agrees in the cleanly separated case: at $\gamma=0.5$, $\theta=1$, $\psi=1.5$ the predicted outliers are 1.449 for $v$ against 1.184 for the nuisance under ridge, and ridge-RRR top overlap with $v$ is 0.73, while PCA picks the nuisance (overlap 0.80). In two cases ($\gamma=1$) the predicted outliers are within 0.02 of each other and the simulated top vector mixes the two, as expected at a near tie.

## Data check
Not run. The isotropic theory does not describe embeddings with a fast-decaying spectrum, and a check with whitened embeddings would test the whitening more than the theory. The predictions file was published before any run, and all three are scored not tested.

## Stop rule and the plan's prediction
The rule says to stop if the limits follow directly from published results. They do, by routine steps, so I recommend stop. The plan's F1 prediction is scored partly held. Its first half (a threshold strictly below PCA's, depending on gamma and lambda) held in the isotropic case. Its second half (unregularised version in print, ridge not) is consistent with what I found but rests on a thin search.

## What a paper would need
A rigorous proof for the non-invariant case, including the vanishing cross term, a general covariance $\Sigma$ with the spike aligned to its eigenbasis, an unbiased estimator of the threshold and of $(\lambda,r)$ from data, and the correlated-donor case. A fresh reading of Ding (2017), Ding and Yang (2021), Romanov et al. (2025) and Bing, Ning and Xu (2022) is the first step, since any of them may already contain the equation above.
