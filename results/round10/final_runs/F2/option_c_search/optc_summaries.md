## Pathology (frame 0907eef7-a0c0-4d0c-9261-7d9f437cf409)

Queries 32, sources opened 32, not retrieved 7.

**Summary.** The literature offers standard label-free H&E pixel transforms: Ruifrok-Johnston colour deconvolution, and Macenko and Vahadane stain separation, which give a per-pixel haematoxylin amount. ST tools derive spot-level counts from them by watershed (Squidpy) or k-means (VistoSeg), and SpaGCN uses mean RGB per spot. H-OD rises qualitatively in nucleus-dense tissue. I found no study measuring per-patch or per-spot agreement between such proxies (H-OD, H-area fraction, thresholded counts, GLCM texture) and deep segmentations (CellViT, HoVer-Net, StarDist) or pathologist cellularity. The evidence that does exist is unfavourable. Classical instance segmentation is far worse on H&E (AJI 0.12-0.27 vs 0.51 for a CNN), with merge or split errors depending on method. A multi-site study found automated nuclear counts tracking haematoxylin intensity in a dark-staining lab (r=0.82), with lab counts spanning 152-621 against true 129-249. That is site-dependent bias, which matters for centre-held-out designs. Deep segmenters also disagree (kidney 0.67-0.76; HoVer-Net vs StarDist mIoU 0.76), and pathologist cellularity is noisy (ICC about 0.89 between pathologists; 38% misclassification near 20%). Among resources, HEST-1k documents CellViT for every slide plus 10x DAPI-based nucleus segmentation for every Xenium sample, which is a non-expression nuclear area. I found no label-free nucleus features for legacy ST or Visium. Linear probes of FM embeddings recover nuclear size, shape and colour (PLUTO; segmentation-labelled). But embeddings strongly encode medical centre and stain (centre predictable at 93-99%; slide-wise clustering), so a top PC is more likely read as stain or centre than as nuclear morphology. My own observation, not from the literature: a covariate built from the same embedding that feeds the ridge head would not be independent of the predictor. I found no validated, label-free, centre-robust substitute for CellViT nuclear area or neoplastic share, which points to a genuine gap. This is provisional given the search breadth.

## Survey (frame d24387cf-ee0b-4127-a924-d18f82c9ed88)

Queries 23, sources opened 27, not retrieved 14.

**Summary.** Q2: Both literatures define the estimand from fixed unit values; the predictor may use any covariate, including those defining the domain or regressor. In PPI the regressor X also feeds f(X). DSL fits g-hat(Q,X) on the same X it regresses on. StratPPI strata are any pre-fixed partition of the input space, and PPI++ handles a group odds ratio with Z observed for all units. Design-unbiasedness of the difference estimator (PPI = difference estimator, PPI++ ~ GREG) needs only fixed predictions (or cross-fitting). No source flags this as a problem. Named cautions: (i) a pooled rectifier biases a domain contrast by the difference in domain-specific mean prediction errors (Mozer 2026), so domain contrasts need per-domain rectification; (ii) sample-fitted classifiers (endogenous post-stratification) are covered only as an estimation device for a fixed estimand, with asymptotic design consistency; a domain defined by a classifier fitted on labelled outcomes would make the estimand sample-dependent. Q3: The census parameter (Binder 1983 via secondary sources; Skinner; Rao) is the solution of population estimating equations in the recorded values. A covariate computed deterministically from the image, without labels, for every unit gives a different but exactly defined finite-population parameter. Design consistency and linearization variance are unaffected. What changes is interpretation relative to a 'true' covariate: attenuation (Fuller), misclassification bias for domains (Lohr 2011), and bias from structured ML proxy error (Proctor et al.). Recovering the true-covariate parameter needs gold-standard covariate values on a probability subsample (Kluger et al.; three-phase design of Sang et al.). Domain mean differences, slopes and correlations are ratio-type estimators under any covariate, so they are only approximately unbiased (asymptotic), with or without derived covariates. Not found: anything treating two-stage cluster sampling with a derived regressor jointly or image-derived morphology covariates. Cluster PPI (Broska & Howes 2026) is model-based and asymptotic.

## Econometrics (frame 4fdd907f-4a4f-44ab-89ad-fe7d61f81fbe)

Queries 52, sources opened 34, not retrieved 11.

**Summary.** Predicted OUTCOMES with observed covariates are the setting of PPI, cross-PPI, recalibrated PPI, postpi, DSL and Rambachan et al. Predicted COVARIATES are treated by Fong-Tyler, Battaglia et al., Kluger et al., Allon et al., Duan-Pelger, EnsembleIV, Canen-Enamorado, Li, Proctor et al. and Ludwig et al. (a) Every covariate-side source defines the target on the latent or reference-measured variable (Ludwig's f*). The plug-in coefficient is a different parameter, the projection on the proxy: Canen-Enamorado write it as reliability-attenuated, and Kluger et al. say naive use targets the wrong parameter. No source adopts the proxy-defined parameter as the estimand. Doing so is coherent under the projection-parameter and finite-population literature (Buja et al.; Vansteelandt-Dukes; Abadie et al.) if the proxy is a fixed, label-free function of each unit's data built outside the analysis sample (Battaglia: a pre-trained model means conditioning on its training sample; Li). If it is fitted on the analysis donors, Pagan/Murphy-Topel variance terms return. (b) I found no source on ML-generated covariates with few clusters. Kluger et al.'s cluster bootstrap comes without theory; DSL, PPI and Battaglia use iid asymptotics. Few-cluster tools (Cameron-Gelbach-Miller, Ibragimov-Müller, Canay-Romano-Shaikh, MacKinnon-Nielsen-Webb, Wang-Zhang) take regressors as observed and need cluster estimates centred on the target. They therefore carry over to a proxy-defined parameter, but not to a latent target, where the plug-in centre is shifted. (c) Against a latent target the proxy changes validity: bias of either sign under nonclassical error, correct CI width with the wrong centre. Against a proxy target it changes interpretation only. Mixing constructions across samples makes a donor-weighted estimand a mixture of different parameters. Every remedy uses a validation subsample measured with the reference procedure, and for a covariate that needs no expression labels. Open gap: no finite-sample or few-cluster theory for generated covariates.
