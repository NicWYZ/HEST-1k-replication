## B. Section S1, motivation

### S1.1 D2. The accuracy ceiling is low and the encoder spread small; the shipped head has no intercept

1. **Result.** Nine-task average Pearson runs from 0.3252 (ResNet50) to 0.4229 (H-Optimus-1), a spread of 0.0977 (`results/summary/results_encoder.csv`, column `avg_paper9`, head `pca_ridge`; the spread recomputed as $0.4229 - 0.3252$). The no-intercept head gives a pooled fold-median $R^2$ of $-0.953196$, and the level and scale oracle reaches $0.099708$ (`results/round2/R1b_heads/r1b_ladder_pooled.csv`, columns `faithful` and `oracle level and optimal scale`).
2. **Method applied.** The HEST-Benchmark protocol (PCA to 256 components, ridge, per-gene Pearson over patient-stratified folds). The $R^2$ ladder is the decomposition of the mean squared error skill score into squared correlation, conditional bias and unconditional bias, $R^2 = r^2 - (r - s_{\hat y}/s_y)^2 - (\bar{\hat y} - \bar y)^2/s_y^2$, which `round2_r1b_ladder.py` writes as $R^2 = 2r\rho - \rho^2 - b^2/s_y^2$ with $\rho = s_{\hat y}/s_y$. Its ceiling $r^2$ is the oracle rung.
3. **Sources.** HEST (full text, section 5.2 states "PCA-reduced embeddings (with n=256 factors) followed by Ridge regression" and Pearson as the metric, Table 1 caption gives the standard deviation over folds). Murphy88 (not opened; the decomposition $\text{MSESS} = r^2 - [\text{conditional bias}]^2 - [\text{bias}/\sigma_o]^2$ read on a secondary page).
4. **Where the record found it.** HEST is cited by the replication from round 1 (`docs/round01/i01/exec/HEST_replication_handoff.md`). Murphy 1988 does not appear in the record; the ladder's algebra is derived in the script's docstring with no citation.
5. **Implementation.** `code/scripts/aggregate_results.py` (function `enc_avg`) and `code/scripts/build_summary_tables.py` for the benchmark; `code/scripts/round2_r1b_heads.py` (`fit_heads`, `per_gene`) for the three heads; `code/scripts/round2_r1b_ladder.py` (`identity_check`, `ladder`).
6. **Validated.** D1, the replication of the live leaderboard over 108 cells (`results/summary/discrepancy_table.csv`, per the findings survey). The ladder script carries an identity check of the decomposition (`identity_check`).
7. **Missing for the paper.** Murphy88 read from the original (equations and section). The no-intercept fact cites HEST section 5.2 for the head but HEST does not state `fit_intercept=False`; the paper needs the HEST-Benchmark code reference (repository file and commit) for that claim. The paper's pooled $R^2$ figure needs its scope stated (it pools 12 encoders, 10 tasks and 52200 rows, `r1b_ladder_pooled.csv`, columns `n_encoders`, `n_tasks`, `n_rows`).
8. **Gap.** None. The decomposition is classical.

### S1.2 D3. Split design costs more than encoder choice

1. **Result.** The random-minus-patient gap averages 0.1586 over ten tasks, with a same-patient other-slide term of 0.1357 and a novel-slide term of 0.0148 (`results/round2/R3_splits/r3_decomposition_terms.csv`, column `mean`). The ratio to the encoder spread is $0.1586/0.0977 = 1.62$, recomputed.
2. **Method applied.** Grouped (blocked) cross-validation, with hold-out at the spot, spatial block, slide and patient levels, and a within-patient metric so that pooling does not move the score. This is block cross-validation in the ecological literature, preserved-site cross-validation in computational pathology, and the "non-independence between train and test" leakage type in machine learning.
3. **Sources.** Roberts17 (abstract; recommends block cross-validation "wherever dependence structures exist"). Howard21 (full text; preserved-site cross-validation keeps "each site … isolated to a single fold", and across 58 features "the average decrease in AUROC between standard and preserved-site cross-validation was 0.069"). Kapoor22 (abstract; eight leakage types, listed only in the PDF). HEST (section 5.1, patient-stratified $k$-fold "to avoid train/test patient-level data leakage"). Chan25 (full text; four folds with "samples from the same patient belonging in the same fold"). DipZhang26 (abstract; moving from random spot splits to study holdouts lowers the score by a factor of about 1.3 to 6.7 over 360 Visium slides, patient splits "buy almost nothing", and pooled correlations let a per-slide mean baseline recover 59 to 99% of the score). AkramDip26 (abstract; leave-one-patient-out over 62 patients).
4. **Where the record found it.** Howard21 is in round 7's federated review (`docs/round07/tracks/fedlearn/round07_fedlearn_literature_review.md`, item P355, read in full there). Roberts17, Kapoor22, DipZhang26 and AkramDip26 do not appear in the record. The pooling artefact is stated in the script's docstring as the record's own observation.
5. **Implementation.** `code/scripts/split_decomposition.py` (`load_task`, `fit`, `score`, `block_test`), extended by `code/scripts/round2_split_v4.py` (buffered and size-matched arms).
6. **Validated.** The decomposition table gives a second draw (`mean_draw2`, `draw_spread` 0.0 to 0.001 in `r3_decomposition_terms.csv`). The same-patient and novel-slide terms rest on 9 cells in 3 tasks (column `n_cells`, `n_tasks`), the others on 30 cells in 10 tasks.
7. **Missing for the paper.** The 2026 workshop papers (DipZhang26, AkramDip26) overlap D3 directly and must be read in full before the paper states its split result. DipZhang26's claim that patient splits buy almost nothing seems to differ from D3's same-patient term of 0.1357, and the difference may come from DipZhang26's observation that most of their slides come from a single donor; that needs their full text. Kapoor22's taxonomy needs its PDF for the leakage type the paper names. D3's same-patient term rests on 3 tasks, and the root README's limitation list should be checked for which tasks these are before the number is quoted.
8. **Gap.** None. Blocked and grouped cross-validation is classical; the decomposition into terms is an application.

### S1.3 D9. The donor carries the variance

1. **Result.** On CCRCC the donor-clustered standard error of $\theta_3$ is 28.99 to 67.55 times the spot i.i.d. one over the unmerged donor sets (`results/round3/B1_ppi/b1b2_report_numbers.csv`, keys `se_ratio_cluster_over_iid_theta3_CCRCC_{donor,spot}_nL{6,8,12}`, median over 50 genes, arm resnet50). The record's "29 to 68" covers these six rows only; the INT4 and INT24 merged rows give 22.43 to 50.26 (keys `..._CCRCC_merged_...`).
2. **Method applied.** The design effect of cluster sampling, $\text{deff} = 1 + (m - 1)\rho$, in survey sampling, and the Moulton factor $1 + \rho_x \rho_u (\bar N_g - 1)$ in econometrics. The genomics name is pseudoreplication. The derivation in `docs/reference/methods_and_state_of_play.md`, section 8, gives $n_{\text{eff}} = nm/\{1 + (m - 1)\rho\} \to n/\rho$.
3. **Sources.** KBL05 (full text in part; section B.2 equation 18 gives $D^2(\bar y_{cl}) = 1 + (b - 1)\rho$, and paragraph 5 credits Kish (1965) with the term). Kish65 (not opened). Moulton90 (abstract). CM15 (section II.B.1 gives $\tau_k \simeq 1 + \rho_{x_k}\rho_u(\bar N_g - 1)$ and section IV.B credits Kish for the design effect). Zimmerman21 (abstract; cells from one individual act as pseudoreplicates and inflate type I error). Squair21 (full text; single-cell methods "systematically underestimate the variance of gene expression" when they ignore variation between replicates, and pseudobulks aggregate cells within a biological replicate).
4. **Where the record found it.** The design effect is derived in `docs/reference/methods_and_state_of_play.md`, section 8, without a citation. Round 8's positioning note uses the term and cites Hagesæther and Zhang (2009) (`docs/round08/i01/positioning/round08_positioning_note.md`, section 3.4). Squair21 is cited in `docs/round05/00_prep/deck2/deck2_literature_check.md`. Kish65, Moulton90, KBL05 and Zimmerman21 do not appear in the record.
5. **Implementation.** `code/scripts/round3_b1_ppi.py` (`cell_spot` and `cell_donor` for the two variances, `report_numbers`).
6. **Validated.** The B1 acceptance checks in the same file, among them that the cluster and i.i.d. variances agree when every donor contributes one spot (key `acceptance_3_one_spot_per_donor_cluster_eq_iid`, 34 of 34 passed, worst value $1.42 \times 10^{-12}$), and the anchor to round 2's predictions (12 of 12 cells, maximum difference $1.14 \times 10^{-11}$). The size of the ratio is consistent with the derivation's $\sqrt{1 + (m - 1)\rho}$ for $m$ in the thousands.
7. **Missing for the paper.** Kish65 (chapter on cluster sampling) and Moulton90 should be read in their originals if cited; KBL05 is a citable secondary source. The paper should state the range with the merged donor set or say it is excluded. The slide-offset statement (about half a standard deviation with both signs) is in `docs/round03/i03/exec/round3_final_report.md`, section 6.2, "The recentred oracle", as a mean $|b_s|/s_y$ of 0.5245 on unit-calibrated slides, "reproducing A2". That paragraph names no result file, and "both signs" is not stated there, so the number and the sign claim need reading back from round 3's A2 or A4 result files before use.
8. **Gap.** None.

### S1.4 B1. Spot-level intervals do not cover and donor cluster-robust intervals do

1. **Result.** Over 200 relabellings, the spot i.i.d. interval covers 0.0440, the donor cluster-robust interval with a $t_{G-1}$ reference 0.9129, and a percentile donor bootstrap 0.7111 (`results/round3/final_report/final_report_numbers.csv`, rows `b2_classical_iid_coverage_pooled` 0.04403, `b2_classical_cluster_coverage_pooled` 0.91295, `b2_classical_boot_coverage_pooled` 0.71108).
2. **Method applied.** The cluster-robust (sandwich) variance with the small-sample factor $G/(G - 1)$ and $t_{G-1}$ critical values; in survey sampling, the ultimate-cluster variance estimator of a two-stage sample with primary units drawn with replacement. The comparator is the percentile pairs cluster bootstrap.
3. **Sources.** LZ86 (full text; section 2, the estimator and its variance "are consistent given only a correct specification of the regression", with Theorem 1 asymptotics as the number of subjects $K \to \infty$). CM15 (section VI intro, "One should at a minimum use T(G-1) critical values"; section II.C, the factor $c = G/(G - 1)$; section VI.A, "no clear-cut definition of 'few'", and the CGM08 rejection rates of 0.068, 0.081, 0.118 and 0.208 for $G$ of 30, 20, 10 and 5 at nominal 0.05; section II.F says the pairs cluster bootstrap variance is "no better asymptotically than the CRVE" and points to VI.C for few-cluster pitfalls, which was not reached). BCH11 (abstract; with the number of clusters fixed and cluster sizes growing, the cluster $t$ statistic follows a $t$ distribution with the number of clusters playing the role of the sample size). CGM08 (abstract; standard tests "can over-reject … with few (five to thirty) clusters"). KF74 (not opened).
4. **Where the record found it.** CM15 and Liang and Zeger are cited in `docs/reference/methods_and_state_of_play.md` (around line 119) and in round 3 and round 4 plans; CGM08 in `docs/round04/i01/ppi/round4_ppi_track.md`; Bell and McCaffrey (2002) as the survey origin of CR2 in round 8's note, section 3.9. BCH11 and KF74 do not appear in the record.
5. **Implementation.** `code/scripts/round3_b1_ppi.py` (`cell_donor`, `intervals`, `_boot_spot`); `code/scripts/round3_b2_semisynthetic.py` for the 200 relabellings; `code/scripts/round3_final_report_numbers.py` (`b2_by_setting`) for the pooled numbers.
6. **Validated.** The pooled numbers are recomputed from committed CSVs by `round3_final_report_numbers.py`; the B1 acceptance rows above. Per setting, CCRCC donor design gives cluster coverage 0.8976, 0.9063 and 0.9376 at $n_L$ of 6, 8 and 12 (`final_report_numbers.csv`, rows `b2_classical_cluster_coverage__CCRCC__donor__nL*`).
7. **Missing for the paper.** CM15 section VI.C for the pairs bootstrap statement, and BCH11's full text for the conditions under which $t_{G-1}$ holds (they require growing cluster sizes and homogeneity conditions that the paper should check against skewed donor contributions, B8). A survey-sampling source for the ultimate-cluster estimator (Wolter's or Särndal, Swensson and Wretman's chapter) is not opened here and is on the L2 list for S4.
8. **Gap.** None.

### S1.5 A4. HCP is valid once $K + 1 \ge 1/\alpha$ and costs width

1. **Result.** At $K = 10$ on CCRCC, HCP covers 0.9692 at 1.9465 times the pooled quantile's mean width, and the pooled quantile covers 0.8644; at $K = 20$ the width ratio is 1.3604 with HCP coverage 0.9392 (`results/round4/conformal/C3_real/c3_report_numbers.csv`, prediction C3.1, scope `CCRCC alpha=0.1 K=10` and `K=20`). Below $K = 10$ HCP has finite share 0.0 at $K$ of 4, 6 and 8 (same file).
2. **Method applied.** Hierarchical conformal prediction, the split conformal quantile of a weighted empirical distribution giving each calibration group total weight $1/(K + 1)$ and a point mass $1/(K + 1)$ at $+\infty$; the comparator is split conformal on pooled spots.
3. **Sources.** LBW (full text in part; section 2.2 equation 6 defines the weights $1/\{(K_1 + 1)N_k\}$ with the mass at $+\infty$, and Theorem 1 gives coverage at least $1 - \alpha$ under hierarchical exchangeability and at most $1 - \alpha + 2/(K_1 + 1)$ when scores are distinct). DWR (abstract; CDF pooling, single and repeated subsampling for two-layer data). Lei18 (abstract; split conformal). TISSUE (full text of the preprint; conformal intervals for spatial expression imputed from a single-cell reference, not from histology, with multiple imputation for downstream tests). TIDEST (abstract; reconstructed expression treated as measured gives invalid discoveries). WML20 (preprint; "uncorrected post-prediction inference will frequently have deflated standard errors, bias, and inflated false positive rates").
4. **Where the record found it.** LBW and DWR in round 8's positioning note, section 7 table, and in the round 4 and round 5 conformal plans. TISSUE, TIDEST and WML20 in `docs/round00/literature_landscape.md` (the record's reading level there is not stated).
5. **Implementation.** `code/scripts/round3_a4b_hcp.py` (`run_spec`, reusing `weighted_quantile` from `round3_a3_weighted.py`), and `code/scripts/round4_conf_c3.py` (`o0_rows`, `wq_cols`, `k_table`) for the $K$ map.
6. **Validated.** `round3_a4b_hcp.py` asserts that its donor grouping agrees row by row with the task definition and mirrors A1's enumeration (docstring). Round 4 reproduced GHCP's released code (A7). The finiteness pattern follows from LBW's equation 6. The set is the whole line when $(1 - \alpha)(K + 1) > K$, which for $\alpha = 0.1$ holds for $K \le 8$ and fails at $K = 9$, consistent with the finite share of 0.0 at $K \le 8$ (derived here from the definition, not stated in LBW).
7. **Missing for the paper.** The record calls HCP "conservative". LBW calls its guarantee tight and bounds over-coverage by $2/(K_1 + 1)$, which is $2/11 = 0.18$ at $K = 10$ (vacuous above 1) and $2/21 = 0.095$ at $K = 20$, where the observed 0.9392 lies inside $[0.90, 0.995]$. The paper should describe the over-coverage as the finite-$K$ slack of Theorem 1 rather than as conservativeness, and should cite LBW's published version if one exists (v4 of August 2025 lists none). DWR's full text is needed if its repeated subsampling is named as an alternative. Lei18's journal version is `[verify]`.
8. **Gap.** None for the method. TISSUE and TIDEST are the nearest uncertainty work on predicted spatial expression and neither treats histology-predicted expression or the donor as the unit (from TISSUE's methods and TIDEST's abstract); that is a positioning point for S1, not a gap.

### S2.4 D11. Library-size normalization halves the factor on CCRCC and does not rescue the predictions; the scale question D5

1. **Result.** On CCRCC the factor's share falls from 0.650 to 0.338 under $\log(1 + 10^4 y/n)$, with $n$ the spot's total count; median skewness of donor means falls from 2.053 to 1.416; leave-one-donor-out median $R^2$ of the prediction moves from $-0.066$ to $-0.073$ (`results/round9/norm/norm_summary.csv`). On INDIANA_KIDNEY the share goes from 0.313 to 0.243 and on LUNG_XENIUM from 0.343 to 0.320 (same file). Log library size correlates with PC1 at 0.802, 0.929 and 0.942 (column `abs_r_loglib_pc1_raw`). Every estimand of rounds 3 to 9 is on $\log(1 + y)$ of unnormalized counts.
2. **Method applied.** Global-scaling (library-size) normalization followed by the shifted logarithm, the default in scanpy and Seurat, set against the unnormalized $\log(1 + y)$ that HEST-Benchmark uses as its target.
3. **Sources and conventions for D5.** The L0 scope asks for the conventions on the expression scale an inference is made about. What the sources opened here say, by source.
   - HEST (full text, section 3.4). Counts are raw ("No additional normalization was conducted, and we let users explore various normalization strategies"); section 5.2 predicts "the log1p-normalized expression of the top 50 highly variable genes", which the record and FedCard read as $\log(1 + y)$ of raw counts. This is the scale of every earlier estimand.
   - FedCard (full text, item 5). The corpus offers `lognorm` $= \log(1 + 10^4 x/n)$ with $n$ the spot total over its own panel, `log1p` as HEST-Benchmark's target, and `raw`.
   - Chan25 (full text). Histology-to-expression methods differ. HisToGene divides by total UMI, scales and logs; DeepSpaCE uses sctransform; ST-Net and DeepPT have no normalization step described. Because "some models were trained on normalised/augmented counts", the benchmark chose scale-free metrics.
   - Gindra25 (section 3.3). Normalization "applied when necessary (based on the encoder requirements)"; no fixed scale.
   - AEH23 (full text). The shifted logarithm $\log(y/s + y_0)$ with size factor $s$, followed by PCA, "performs as well or better than the more sophisticated alternatives"; they recommend $y_0 = 1/(4\alpha)$ from the overdispersion and note that CPM scaling implies an unrealistically large overdispersion.
   - Lun18 (full text). Log-transforming size-factor-normalized counts with a pseudocount creates "a spurious difference on the log-scale" between groups with different size factors for genes that are not differentially expressed; the remedy is a larger pseudocount computed from the size-factor range, and validation with count models.
   - Booeshaghi (full text). The standard proportional fitting then $\log(1 + \cdot)$ "retained substantial depth correlation in many datasets"; an extra proportional fitting after the log removes it.
   - HS19 and the Seurat vignette. Depth stays a confounder after log-normalization; Pearson residuals of a regularised negative binomial model are the alternative scale.
   - Bhuva24 (full text). In spatial data library size is associated with tissue structure, "library size normalization should not be performed prior to spatial domain identification", and is allowed for cross-sample comparison; the paper recommends no general scale.
   - SpaNorm (abstract). Models library size and biology jointly so that normalization keeps spatial biology.
   - Atta24 (abstract). For imaging panels, normalization by detected gene counts per cell distorts expression in a region- or cell-type-specific way when the panel over-represents some cell types, and cell area or volume normalization does not; they recommend "non-gene count-based normalization approaches when feasible". This bears on LUNG_XENIUM and every corpus Xenium task, where FedCard's `lognorm` divides by the panel total.
   - Salas25 (preprint in part). Xenium matrices were "log-transformed and normalized" with standard single-cell workflows; no specific scale recommended.
   - AH10 (full text). The median-of-ratios size factor, a depth estimate that is robust to a few highly expressed genes.
   - Squair21 (full text) and Crowell20 (vignette, secondary). Donor-level (pseudobulk) inference sums raw counts per replicate and tests on counts or log counts per million (edgeR logCPM into limma-trend in Squair21); muscat defaults to "the sum of raw counts" and uses log-normalized values "for visualization only".
4. **Where the record found it.** None of the D5 sources above is cited in the record except HEST and Squair21 (deck 2 check). Decision D5 is in `CLAUDE.md`'s state block and was settled on 7 November to be decided by testing with both scales carried (L0 scope, section 2).
5. **Implementation.** `code/scripts/round9_c2_normalized_summaries.py` (`main`), Longleaf job b4d4780e (`docs/round09/00_prep/round09_normalization_check.md`).
6. **Validated.** Raw counts were recovered from $\log(1 + y)$ exactly for CCRCC and INDIANA_KIDNEY (`results/round9/norm/c2_checks.csv`, `frac_integer_counts` 1.0) and for 0.99999954 of LUNG_XENIUM entries (same file), every spot matched to its library size (`frac_spots_with_lib` 1.0). The scored predictions file says "1.0 on every task", which the CSV shows is a rounding of LUNG_XENIUM's value. Predictions N1 to N4 were written before the run and scored (N2 held, the others refuted).
7. **Missing for the paper.** For D5 the literature gives no single convention for the scale of an inference about donor means. The candidate scales it names are (a) $\log(1 + y)$ of raw counts (HEST's target), (b) the shifted log of size-factor-normalized counts with a stated pseudocount (AEH23, Lun18), (c) the donor pseudobulk on counts or logCPM (Squair21, Crowell20), and (d) Pearson residuals (HS19). For targeted Xenium panels, cell-area normalization (Atta24) is the alternative to the panel total, and pseudo-spots have no cell areas (HEST section 3.2), so that alternative may not be available on the corpus's Xenium tasks. The mean of $\log(1 + y)$ over spots and the log of the donor's mean expression are different estimands, and Lun18's bias applies to the first whenever spot depths differ between donors. Full texts of Atta24, SpaNorm and HS19 are needed if the paper cites them for anything beyond their abstracts.
8. **Gap.** None found as a method. The choice of estimand scale is a decision (D5), and the sources above are the candidates.
