## Row S6-1. Same-cluster tuning of the coefficient (B5, first part)

1. **Result.** B5. Tuning $\lambda$ on the labelled clusters it is applied to under-covers at few clusters while giving the narrowest intervals, and cross-fitting restores nominal coverage. In round 4's superpopulation simulation at $n_L = 4$ the median coverage is 0.8735 under rule (a), 0.856 under rule (b), 0.902 under rule (c) and 0.89775 for the classical estimator, all with CR1 and $t_{n_L - 1}$, over 432 cells each. The median PPI-to-classical width ratios for $r > 0$ at $n_L = 4$ are 0.7365 (a), 0.6055 (b) and 1.009 (c) (`results/round4/ppi/Q1_estimator/q1_report_numbers.csv`, rows `Q1.1|super|*|CR1_t|GL4|cov_median` and `S|super|*|CR1_t|GL4|r>0|width_ratio_median`; the rule (c) width from `docs/round04/i01/ppi/round4_ppi_Q1_report.md`, section 6.1).
2. **Existing method.** Single-sample PPI++ (power tuning on the same labelled data) against split-sample and cross-fit PPI++, in the non-asymptotic analysis of Mani, Xu, Lipton and Oberst. The general device is cross-fitting of a nuisance coefficient (Zrnic and Candès's cross-prediction for the predictor itself). The ridge-shrunk coefficient of Eyre and Madras is an alternative fix for few labels.
3. **Sources.**
   - Mani, Xu, Lipton and Oberst, "No Free Lunch: Non-Asymptotic Analysis of Prediction-Powered Inference", arXiv:2505.20178 (v1 26 May 2025, v2 24 June 2026), published in Proceedings of the 43rd ICML, PMLR 306, 86296 to 86335, 2026. Read level: full text of v1 for sections 3, 4.1 to 4.4 and 5; arXiv abstract page and PMLR landing page for v2 and the venue. Their Proposition 4.5 gives the single-sample estimator a finite-sample bias, their Proposition 4.6 shows that treating $\hat\lambda$ as fixed gives a variance estimate always below the classical one, so "it can produce overly optimistic variance estimates" (section 4.4), and section 5 reports that the single-sample intervals' tighter widths "come at the cost of inadequate coverage" while the cross-fit intervals match the classical coverage. Theorem 4.1 and Corollary 4.1 give the cross-fit estimator's MSE as half the split-sample MSE plus a non-negative term of order $1/n^2$. The Gaussian break-even condition is $|\rho_{fy}| > 1/\sqrt{c\,n - 2}$ with $c = 2$ for single-sample and $c = 1$ for cross-fit, $n$ the fold size (Proposition 4.1). Independent units only, no clusters.
   - Angelopoulos, Duchi and Zrnic, "PPI++: Efficient Prediction-Powered Inference", arXiv:2311.01453v2 (26 March 2024). Read level: introduction and sections 2.1 to 2.2 only; the body of section 6 (power tuning) did not come through the fetch. It introduces the plug-in $\hat\lambda$ and justifies the interval asymptotically.
   - Eyre and Madras, "Regression for the Mean: Auto-Evaluation and Inference with Few Labels through Post-hoc Regression", arXiv:2411.12665v2, ICML 2025 (PMLR 267). Read level: abstract and sections 2.1, 4.1 to 4.3 through the HTML version. With scarce labels PPI++ "can perform even worse than classical inference", the estimated $\lambda$ has not converged (section 4.1, footnote 1), and a ridge penalty shrinks $\lambda$ toward zero (section 4.2).
   - Song, Kluger, Parikh and Gu, "Demystifying Prediction Powered Inference", arXiv:2601.20819v1. Read level: full text for sections 2.3.1, 4.1 to 4.3 and 5.2. Section 4.3 says the tuning parameters are estimated "often with sample splitting or cross-fitting". Its double-dipping result (section 5.2, Table 2) concerns reuse of labels to train the predictor, not tuning of $\lambda$, and is a neighbouring caution.
   - Zrnic and Candès, "Cross-Prediction-Powered Inference", arXiv:2309.16598. Not opened in this pass; round 8 read sections 3, 4 and 6 (`docs/round08/i01/positioning/round08_positioning_note.md`, section 1.2 table).
4. **Where the record found it.** Mani et al. first in the deck-2 literature check of 4 October (`docs/round05/00_prep/deck2/deck2_literature_check.md`, lines on the break-even rule), then sections 3, 4.2 and 4.3 read in round 8 (`docs/round08/i01/positioning/round08_positioning_note.md`, section 1.2 table and section 3.11, graded close for C1). Eyre and Madras in the deck-2 check and round 8's note section 3.9. Song et al. is listed in round 4's originality check (`docs/round04/00_prep/round4_originality_check.md`, its sources line). PPI++ is the base method throughout.
5. **Implementation.** `code/scripts/round4_ppi_estimator.py`, `lambda_rule` (rules `none`, `a_b1`, `b_cluster`, `c_crossfit`, and `c_crossfit_design`), with `_cluster_lambda_donor`, `_cluster_lambda_spot`, `_crossfit_halves`; the simulation in `code/scripts/round4_ppi_q1_sim.py`. Round 5's cross-fitted estimator is `code/scripts/round5_ppi_estimator.py`.
6. **What the record validated.** Q1's acceptance checks (`results/round4/ppi/Q1_estimator/q1_acceptance.csv`; Q1 report section 3), with check 5 failing for rule (c) on $\lambda$'s location at $r = 0$ (largest cell median 0.382) because each half's value is clipped at 0. ## Row S6-2. Cluster bootstraps at few clusters (B5, second part)

1. **Result.** B5. No bootstrap is competitive at few labelled clusters. At $n_L = 4$ in round 4's superpopulation simulation, rule (c), the median coverage at nominal 0.90 is 0.760 for the percentile cluster bootstrap, 0.75875 for BCa and 0.85175 for bootstrap-$t$, against 0.902 for CR1 with $t_{n_L - 1}$ (`results/round4/ppi/Q1_estimator/q1_report_numbers.csv`, rows `Q1.2|super|c_crossfit|boot_pct|GL4|cov_median`, `boot_bca`, `boot_t`, `CR1_t`). On the skewed spot-weighted $\theta_3$ at $\rho = 0.1$ the wild cluster bootstrap-$t$ covers 0.75675 with Mammen weights and 0.83125 with Rademacher weights at $n_L = 6$ for the classical estimator (`results/round4/ppi/Q1_estimator/q1b/q1b_report_numbers.csv`, rows `Q1b.4|none|wild_t_*|GL6|rho0.1|cov_median`), below CR1 (`docs/round04/i02/ppi/round4_ppi_Q3_report.md`, Q1b.4). Round 7 adds Kluger et al.'s cluster bootstrap, row S6-8.
2. **Existing method.** The pairs (cluster) bootstrap with percentile and BCa intervals, the bootstrap-$t$, and the wild cluster bootstrap, as compared for few clusters in econometrics. The percentile interval's small-sample narrowness for a mean. CR1 with $t_{G - 1}$ as the baseline small-sample practice.
3. **Sources.**
   - Cameron, Gelbach and Miller, "Bootstrap-Based Improvements for Inference with Clustered Errors", Review of Economics and Statistics 90(3), 414 to 427, 2008, DOI 10.1162/rest.90.3.414. Read level: full text of the NBER technical working paper t0344 (2007), abstract and sections 3 to 5 and 7. Monte Carlo at $G$ of 5 to 30; standard tests over-reject "with few (5-30) clusters" (abstract); bootstrap-se and BCa give no asymptotic refinement and BCa "in general leads to greater over-rejection than when CRVE is used" (section 5.1); the wild cluster bootstrap-$t$ with the null imposed has rejection rates near 0.05 "even for G = 6" (section 5.1); without a bootstrap, at least a $T$ reference with $G$ or fewer degrees of freedom (section 7). The published REStat version was not opened.
   - MacKinnon, Nielsen and Webb, "Cluster-Robust Inference: A Guide to Empirical Practice", Journal of Econometrics 232(2), 272 to 299, 2023, DOI 10.1016/j.jeconom.2022.04.001 (citation read from the RePEc record). Read level: full text of arXiv:2205.03285v1 through section 5.3. CV1 with $t(G - 1)$ is "often not nearly conservative enough, especially when G is small" (section 4.2); there is "no magic number for G" (section 4.3); CV3 is the cluster jackknife (section 2.2, eq. 18); the pairs cluster bootstrap is not recommended for linear regression because bootstrap sample sizes vary (section 5.2); the wild cluster restricted bootstrap is recommended in most cases (section 1), with Webb's six-point weights when $G$ is small (section 5.3). Sections 6 to 9 were not reached.
   - MacKinnon and Webb, "Wild Bootstrap Inference for Wildly Different Cluster Sizes", Journal of Applied Econometrics 32(2), 233 to 254, 2017. Read level: full text of Queen's working paper 1314 to about two thirds. CRVE with $t(G - 1)$ over-rejects with unequal cluster sizes, "even with 100 unbalanced clusters" (section 1); the restricted wild bootstrap under-rejects and the unrestricted one over-rejects when very few clusters are treated (section 6).
   - Cameron and Miller, "A Practitioner's Guide to Cluster-Robust Inference", Journal of Human Resources 50(2), 317 to 372, 2015, DOI 10.3368/jhr.50.2.317. Read level: section VI.A and the start of VI.B from the eScholarship copy; the wild bootstrap and pairs-bootstrap pitfalls subsections did not come through. At a minimum $T(G - 1)$ critical values with the factor $c = \frac{G}{G - 1}\frac{N - 1}{N - K}$, and "Even with both of these adjustments, Wald tests generally over-reject" (VI.A).
   - Hesterberg, "What Teachers Should Know About the Bootstrap: Resampling in the Undergraduate Statistics Curriculum", The American Statistician 69(4), 371 to 386, 2015 (venue from the RePEc record), arXiv:1411.5279. Read level: full text, sections 2.3, 3.2 and 5.2 to 5.3. Bootstrap distributions of the mean are too narrow by $\sqrt{(n - 1)/n}$ (section 3.2) and the percentile interval "suffers the same narrowness and more" in small samples (section 5.2). This is the elementary mechanism behind the percentile interval's 0.760 at four clusters.
   - Gafarov and Ura, "Refined Cluster Robust Inference", arXiv:2603.24786v2 (4 June 2026). Read level: abstract and introduction through the HTML version. A closed-form Edgeworth-corrected critical value from the estimated score skewness and kurtosis, with size control "with as few as 10 clusters", better than the wild cluster bootstrap under skewed scores. Bears on the skewed donor contributions behind round 4's spot-weighted $\theta_3$ failures and B8.
   - Mashreghi, Haziza and Léger, "A survey of bootstrap methods in finite population sampling", Statistics Surveys 10, 2016, DOI 10.1214/16-SS113. Not opened (Project Euclid returned a bot check). The survey-sampling side of the bootstrap (Rao and Wu's rescaling bootstrap for without-replacement designs) is therefore not read in this pass.
4. **Where the record found it.** Cameron, Gelbach and Miller is cited in round 4's Q1 decisions (`docs/round04/i02/ppi/round4_ppi_Q1_decisions.md`, addition 3) as the source of the wild cluster bootstrap-$t$. Cameron and Miller and MacKinnon, Nielsen and Webb are cited in the header of `code/scripts/round4_ppi_estimator.py` and in round 8's note section 3.9 (C9). Gafarov and Ura is cited in round 9's fewcluster brief (`docs/round09/i01/fewcluster/round09_fewcluster_brief.md`). MacKinnon and Webb (2017) and Hesterberg were not found in the record.
5. **Implementation.** `code/scripts/round4_ppi_estimator.py`, `bootstrap` (percentile, bootstrap-$t$ and BCa on resampled labelled donors), `bootstrap_textbook`, `_jackknife`, `draw_counts`; the wild bootstrap-$t$ of addition 3 in `code/scripts/round4_ppi_q1b_sim.py`.

8. **Gap.** None. Under-coverage of percentile and BCa cluster bootstraps at few clusters is in print (Cameron, Gelbach and Miller 2008; MacKinnon, Nielsen and Webb 2023, section 5.2), with the elementary mechanism in Hesterberg (2015).

## Row S6-3. The finite-population factor for the design target, and what it does to a superpopulation interval (B5, third part)

1. **Result.** B5. The finite-population factor is needed for the design target and is wrong for the superpopulation target. On the design target at $n_L = 12$ of $G = 24$ with the textbook form and rule (c), median coverage is 0.89325 with the factor and 0.97075 without it (`results/round4/ppi/Q1_estimator/q1_report_numbers.csv`, rows `Q1.3|design|textbook|textbook_t|fpc1|c_crossfit|GL12|cov_median` and `fpc0`). Applied against the superpopulation target, rule (a) with the factor falls from a median 0.84575 at $n_L = 4$ to 0.7685 at $n_L = 20$ (`results/round4/ppi/Q1_estimator/q1_coverage_by_G.csv`, rows `super,complement,ppi,a_b1,CR1_t,True`).
2. **Existing method.** The finite-population correction $1 - n/G$ for simple random sampling of clusters without replacement, and the design-based account of cluster adjustments, in which conventional cluster-robust standard errors are conservative when a non-negligible share of the population's clusters is sampled.
3. **Sources.**
   - Abadie, Athey, Imbens and Wooldridge, "When Should You Adjust Standard Errors for Clustering?", arXiv:1710.02926v4 (20 September 2022), published in the Quarterly Journal of Economics, 2023 (`[verify]` volume and pages; the publisher record was behind a verification page). Read level: full text for sections 1 to 4 and 7. Conventional cluster standard errors "can be severely inflated" when a non-negligible share of population clusters is sampled (abstract); "Clustered standard errors are conservative in general" (section 3.3) and heavily biased when the sampled fraction is large (section 4).
   - Abadie, Athey, Imbens and Wooldridge, "Sampling-Based versus Design-Based Uncertainty in Regression Analysis", Econometrica 88(1), 265 to 296, 2020, DOI 10.3982/ECTA12675. Read level: abstract only (RePEc record).
   - Cochran (1977), *Sampling Techniques*, chapters 2 and 9 for the correction. Not opened (no open copy); it is on the L2 list of the brief.
4. **Where the record found it.** The correction is classical in round 8's note (C1, section 3.2). Abadie et al. were not found in the record.
5. **Implementation.** `code/scripts/round4_ppi_estimator.py`, `fpc_factor`, `variances`, `var_textbook`, `textbook_intervals`; the linearised design-target interval `textbook_t|fpc|lin` in `code/scripts/round4_ppi_q2_masking.py`.
6. **What the record validated.** Q1 report sections 6.1 and 6.2. Round 7 found Broska and Howes's interval, which has no factor, conservative for the design target as $n_L/G$ grows, by less than the factor $\rho_{\mathrm{BH}}$ suggests (`docs/round07/i02/ppi/round07_ppi_S2_decisions.md`, item 3), which is the Abadie et al. mechanism in the PPI setting.

## Row S6-5. Spot-weighted estimands and the permuted predictor (B6, second part)

1. **Result.** B6. On spot-weighted estimands a permuted predictor appears to help, because any predictor that is constant across spots reproduces the between-donor variance of the design-weight sums. On CCRCC $\theta_3$ at $n_L$ of 6 to 16 the permuted predictor takes a median $\hat\lambda$ of 0.856 to 0.915 and an empirical variance ratio of 0.285 to 0.397 against the classical estimator, with coverage 0.855 to 0.8675 (`results/round4/ppi/Q5a/q5a_spot_weighted_permuted.csv`, rows `CCRCC,theta3`). The record marks every spot-weighted PPI row as a nuisance row (`docs/round04/i03/ppi/round4_ppi_final_report.md`, section 10.2).
2. **Existing method.** Two classical facts. (i) In cluster sampling with unequal cluster sizes, the unbiased estimator of the mean per element has a variance driven by the variation of the cluster totals, which an estimator using cluster size as auxiliary (the ratio-to-size estimator) removes. A predictor that is constant within each cluster acts as such an auxiliary. (ii) The choice between an individual-weighted and a cluster-weighted estimand under informative cluster size.
3. **Sources.**
   - Cochran (1977), chapter 9 (cluster sampling with unequal sizes), and Lohr, *Sampling: Design and Analysis*, chapter 5. Not opened (no open copies).
   - Ridgeway (course notes), "Cluster Sampling: unequal one-stage", PUBHBIO 7225, Ohio State (`https://randridge.github.io/PUBHBIO7225/PUBHBIO7225_L12_unequal1stageClus.html`). Read level: full page. Shows the unbiased estimator's variance through the spread of PSU totals and the ratio estimator's through $t_i - M_i \hat y_r$, with a standard error falling from 103.8 to 27.9 in its example. Course notes, not a citable source for the paper, kept only as a pointer to the textbook result.
   - Seaman, Pavlou and Copas, "Review of methods for handling confounding by cluster and informative cluster size in clustered data", Statistics in Medicine 33, 5371 to 5387, 2014, DOI 10.1002/sim.6277. Read level: full text, sections 4 and 5, from the UCL Discovery copy. Section 4 separates "inference about a typical member of a typical cluster" from inference about all members, to which clusters contribute in proportion to their size; section 5 says the choice "will be driven by the choice of estimand".
   - Blette, Chen, Kahan, Forbes, Harhay and Li, "Evaluating informative cluster size in cluster randomized trials", arXiv:2510.01127v1. Read level: abstract and introduction. Individual-average and cluster-average estimands differ under informative cluster size. It cites Kahan et al. (2023) on estimands in cluster randomised trials, which was not opened.
4. **Where the record found it.** Round 3 named the effect the covariance-estimand effect, and round 4's Q5 decisions read it as a finding about estimand choice (`docs/round04/i04/ppi/round4_ppi_Q5_decisions.md`, item 1). No textbook source is cited for it in the record. Seaman et al. and the informative-cluster-size literature were not found in the record.
5. **Implementation.** The permuted arm of `code/scripts/round4_ppi_q2_masking.py` (`load`, `summarise`), the Q5a table in `results/round4/ppi/Q5a/` (scripts listed in its `PROVENANCE.md`); populations `donor` and `spot` throughout `code/scripts/round4_ppi_estimator.py` (`donor_values`, `_cluster_lambda_spot`).

## Row S6-7. Broska and Howes's interval at few clusters, and its small-sample correction `bh_t` (B12, first part)

1. **Result.** B12. Broska and Howes's cluster-robust PPI++ interval as published (normal quantile, divisor $n_L$) covers the design target at a median 0.70425 on tissue and 0.717 on census at $n_L = 4$. The corrected form `bh_t`, with the labelled term multiplied by $n_L/(n_L - 1)$ and a $t_{n_L - 1}$ quantile, covers 0.8845 and 0.8825 (`results/round7/ppi/S5_numbers/run1/s5_bh_vs_tuned.csv`, rows `design,tissue,4,bh`, `design,census,4,bh`, `bh_t`). From $n_L = 8$ `bh_t` over-covers the design target and is wider than the tuned interval, since it has no finite-population factor (`docs/round07/i04/ppi/round07_ppi_final_decisions.md`, section 2, item 6). The widening at $n_L = 4$ is bounded by $\frac{t_{3,0.95}}{z_{0.95}}\sqrt{4/3} \approx 1.65$ (`docs/round07/i02/ppi/round07_ppi_S2_decisions.md`, item 4).
2. **Existing method.** Cluster-robust PPI++ (Broska and Howes), with the classical small-sample practice of CR1-type scaling by $G/(G - 1)$ and a $t_{G - 1}$ reference (Cameron and Miller 2015; Cameron, Gelbach and Miller 2008), whose survey origin for few PSUs is bias-reduced linearisation with Satterthwaite degrees of freedom (Bell and McCaffrey 2002), with the Bell and McCaffrey degrees of freedom recommended routinely by Imbens and Kolesár (2016) and generalised by Pustejovsky and Tipton. The group-estimates $t$-test of Ibragimov and Müller is a further classical few-cluster option.
3. **Sources.**
   - Broska and Howes, "Cluster-Robust Prediction-Powered Inference", arXiv:2610.09601v1 (7 October 2026). Read level: sections 2.2 to 2.4, 3.1 to 3.3, 4 and appendix A.2 through the ResearchGate copy (publication 415374328); the abstract also through the deeplearn.org mirror. The arXiv page itself was not reachable from here. The interval uses $z_{1-\alpha/2}$ (section 2.3, Remark 1); validity is asymptotic as $G \to \infty$ with i.i.d. clusters (section 2.3, Theorem 1; appendix A.2, Assumption 1); with few labelled clusters both their interval (88%) and labels-only (90%) under-cover at nominal 95% (section 3.2); and section 4 says "our variance estimator has no small-sample correction" and names small-sample corrections and the wild cluster bootstrap as future work. No finite-population correction; clusters drawn with replacement from an artificial population of $G = 2{,}266$ broadcasts (section 3.1). Power tuning $\hat\lambda = \hat\Sigma_{12}/\hat\Sigma_{22}$ (section 2.4, eq. 12).
   - Bell and McCaffrey, "Bias reduction in standard errors for linear regression with multi-stage samples", Survey Methodology 28(2), 169 to 181, 2002. Read level: full text from Statistics Canada, sections 3, 4, 6 and 8. Linearisation is biased low with few PSUs; BRL adjusts residuals by $(I - H_{ii})^{-1/2}$ (section 3); Satterthwaite degrees of freedom "can be less than two" (section 4); simulation-based critical values when they fall below about 5 (section 8).
   - Imbens and Kolesár, "Robust Standard Errors in Small Samples: Some Practical Advice", Review of Economics and Statistics 98(4), 701 to 712, 2016 (venue from the RePEc record), NBER WP 18478. Read level: full text of the March 2016 draft, abstract and section 4. "We recommend researchers routinely calculate the Bell-McCaffrey degrees-of-freedom adjustment" (abstract); $t(S - 1)$ improves on the normal but still under-covers (section 4).
   - Pustejovsky and Tipton, "Small sample methods for cluster-robust variance estimation and hypothesis testing in fixed effects models", arXiv:1601.01981v2. Read level: abstract and summary of recommendations. CR2 with Satterthwaite degrees of freedom, applied routinely. Published in the Journal of Business and Economic Statistics, 2018 (`[verify]`, not read from a record).
   - Ibragimov and Müller, "t-Statistic Based Correlation and Heterogeneity Robust Inference" (LMU-hosted copy). Read level: abstract and introduction. Estimate within each of $q \ge 2$ groups and use the $t$-statistic of the $q$ estimates with $q - 1$ degrees of freedom, conservative at level up to $2\Phi(-\sqrt{3}) = 0.08326$ (Theorem 1). Venue not confirmed (`[verify]`, Journal of Business and Economic Statistics, 2010).
   - Cameron and Miller (2015) and Cameron, Gelbach and Miller (2008), as in row S6-2.
4. **Where the record found it.** Broska and Howes in round 7's overlap check of 13 October (`docs/round07/00_prep/round07_overlap_check.md`), read in full by the oversight chat in round 7 (`docs/round07/oversight/round07_ppi_positioning.md`; round 8's note, section 3.11 table, "in full in round 7"). The correction as C9 in round 8's note, section 3.9, with Bell and McCaffrey, Kennel and Valliant (2019), Jang and Eltinge (2009), Imbens and Kolesár and Pustejovsky and Tipton read there from abstracts by a subagent. Ibragimov and Müller in round 9's plan, direction scan and draft (`docs/round09/tracks/fewcluster/paper_draft_v0.md`).
5. **Implementation.** `code/scripts/round7_ppi_weighting.py`, `bh` (the published interval, eq. as theory section 2.1) and `bh_direct`; `code/scripts/round7_ppi_s2_comparators.py`, `bh3`, `bh3_parts` (the split $T_L + T_U$ of the published $\widehat{\mathrm{se}}^2$) and `bh_variants`, which forms `bh` ($T_L + T_U$, normal), `bh_t` ($T_L\,n/(n - 1) + T_U$, $t_{n - 1}$), `bh_t_n2` (the same variance, $t_{n - 2}$) and `bh_t_fpc` (`bh_t` times $1 - n_L/G$, $t_{n - 1}$), with `rho_bh` for the ratio $\rho_{\mathrm{BH}} = \frac{1 - (1 - f)R^2}{(1 - f)(1 - R^2)}$, $f = n_L/G$. The S5 table by `code/scripts/round7_ppi_s5_numbers.py`.
6. **What the record validated.** Round 7's S2 report and S2b run (`docs/round07/i01/ppi/round07_ppi_S2_report.md`, sections 6.3 and 6.4; `results/round7/ppi/_i01_numbers_r3/s2_report_numbers.csv`), the S5 matched table, and the final decisions (section 2, items 6 and 7; section 4, R7-5).

Every result of S6 rests on an existing method found in print, and no gap is recorded. The paper's limits section can cite Mani et al. (2026, ICML) for same-sample tuning, Cameron, Gelbach and Miller (2008) and MacKinnon, Nielsen and Webb (2023) for few-cluster bootstraps, Abadie et al.
