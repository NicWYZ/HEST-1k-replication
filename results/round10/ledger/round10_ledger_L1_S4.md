## 0. One code finding that bears on B10, E and the open line in `CLAUDE.md`

The findings survey's B10 says round 5's variance "pools residuals fitted with different coefficients and so grows with the level of the predictions", widening round 5's interval by 1.085 to 20.3 times. The scripts do not support that reading for the intervals rounds 5 and 7 reported. This is a code reading by this subagent and has not been run.

1. Round 4's original design-target variance, `var_textbook` and `textbook_two_stage(..., lin=False)` in `code/scripts/round4_ppi_estimator.py` and `code/scripts/round4_ppi_q2_masking.py`, takes the sample variance of $e_g = \bar z_g - \lambda_{(g)}\bar f_g$ across both halves. That variance moves with the level of $\bar f$ when $\lambda_A \ne \lambda_B$.
2. Round 4's Q5a closing memo found and fixed exactly this defect on 3 October (`docs/round04/i05/ppi/round4_ppi_Q5a_closing.md`, section 2). The fix adds $(\lambda_{(g)} - c_U)\bar F$ to each contribution, so that $e_g = \bar z_g - \lambda_{(g)}(\bar f_g - \bar F) - c_U\bar F$. Under a shift $f \mapsto f + a$ every $e_g$ moves by the same $-c_U a$, so the sample variance does not change. The corrected interval is named `textbook_t|fpc|lin`. Its effect is in `results/round4/ppi/Q5a/q5a_design_variance_before_after.csv`. For rule `c_crossfit_design`, donor-weighted $\theta_3$ at $n_L = 8$ on ACS_STATES with the package predictor, the ratio of estimated to empirical variance is 10.270601 before and 0.851860 after (read from the file).
3. Round 5's `design_whole_clusters` (`code/scripts/round5_ppi_estimator.py`) has `lin=True` as its default, and round 5's real-task driver calls `textbook_two_stage(..., lin=True)` (`code/scripts/round5_ppi_e3_twolevel.py`, lines 458 to 459). Every reported regime-A interval of rounds 5 and 7 is named `textbook_t|fpc|lin` or `textbook_t|fpc|lin|xf` (`round5_ppi_e1_merge.py`, `round5_ppi_e4b_merge.py`, `round7_ppi_s2_comparators.py`, `round7_ppi_s5_numbers.py`). No round 5 or round 7 script calls `var_textbook` or `textbook_intervals`.
4. Round 9's `code/scripts/round9_fewcluster_williams.py` builds its arm `R5` from `eA = zA - lB * fA` and `eB = zB - lA * fB` pooled across halves, with no $(\lambda_{(g)} - c')\bar F$ term. Its `R5` variance is therefore round 4's pre-Q5a variance plus round 5's cross-fitting term, not round 5's `textbook_t|fpc|lin|xf`. Its `W_plain` arm, which uses the same pooled variance without the cross-fitting term, has a coverage within 0.002 of `R5` on every task at $n = 8$ (0.871500 against 0.873075 on CCRCC, 0.905300 against 0.904800 on INDIANA_KIDNEY, 0.939350 against 0.939150 on LUNG_XENIUM and 0.942500 against 0.942500 on ACS_STATES, `results/round9/fewcluster/williams/i34_williams_summary_hest.csv` and `i34_williams_summary_acs.csv`), which is what this reading predicts.

So the widening factors of B10 describe a variance that round 4 had already withdrawn, and by the code the intervals reported in rounds 5 to 7 do not carry the level dependence. They still pool residuals across halves around one mean, which differs from round 9's within-half pooling by a level-free between-half term. The final runs should confirm this by running round 9's comparison once more with the `lin` term in the `R5` arm. This bears on the `CLAUDE.md` line "Whether the tuned intervals of rounds 5 to 7 carry round 5's variance inflation is not checked" and on the frozen documents that state B10 (round 9's Williams note, section 2 item 3, and the findings survey, B10 and section 3.2).

## 1. What the three named scripts implement

**`code/scripts/round4_ppi_estimator.py`** (header lines 2 to 68; module md5 recorded by round 5 as `d79e69aa65800b9d24de556e019a0c64`). The header names the estimator as the survey difference estimator and, in its PPI++ form, the GREG estimator, citing Mozer, Särndal, Swensson and Wretman (1992, chapter 8) and Breidt and Opsomer (2017), and names the variances as Liang and Zeger's sandwich with Cameron and Miller (2015), MacKinnon, Nielsen and Webb (2023) and Bell and McCaffrey (2002). It works on per-donor sufficient statistics. Its functions are these.
- `lambda_rule` with rules `none` (classical), `a_b1` (round 3's), `b_cluster` (tuned on the CR1 variance, clipped to $[0, 1]$, zero when $G_L < 4$), `c_crossfit` (halves, each half's coefficient applied to the other, $c_U$ their weighted average), `d1_pretest` and `d2_pretest` (dropped at Q3), and `c_crossfit_design`, the design-target rule kept by rounds 5 to 9 (each half's least-squares slope of the donor contributions with intercept, clipped to $[0, 1]$, applied to the other half, zero when $n_L < 6$; helpers `_design_lambda`, `_crossfit_halves`).
- `estimate`, the complement form $c_U\,\overline{f}_U + \overline{(z - \lambda f)}_L$, with per-donor influence pieces.
- `variances`, giving `iid_z`, `CR1_t` (factor $G/(G-k)$, $t_{G_L - k}$), `CR2_bm` (the $(1 - w_g)^{-1}$ leverage adjustment of a mean with Bell and McCaffrey's Satterthwaite degrees of freedom, `df_term_bm`), Welch-Satterthwaite combinations, and each with the finite-population factor $1 - G_L/G$ on the labelled term (`fpc_factor`).
- `design_exact_var`, a linearised SRSWOR design variance of the complement form, flagged in its docstring as a proposal and not a component the source names.
- `estimate_textbook` and `var_textbook`, the design-target difference estimator over the whole population, $\hat\theta = c_U\bar F + \frac{1}{n_L}\sum_{g \in L}(\bar z_g - \lambda_{(g)}\bar f_g)$ with $(1 - n_L/G)s_e^2/n_L$ and $t_{n_L - 1}$, without the `lin` term (section 0).
- `bootstrap` and `bootstrap_textbook` (percentile, studentised, BCa with a donor jackknife), `t_interval`, `all_intervals`, `textbook_intervals`.

**`code/scripts/round5_ppi_estimator.py`** (header lines 1 to 16). Imports round 4's module unmodified.
- `design_whole_clusters`, the design-target difference estimator with every unit of a labelled cluster labelled, donor- or spot-weighted, with the `lin` term on by default and the variance $(1 - n_L/G)s_e^2/n_L$, $t_{n_L - 1}$. With `xf=True` it adds `crossfit_extra`, $\frac{n_L}{G}\,\overline{(\lambda_{(g)} - c_U)^2}_L\,s_f^2/n_L$, round 5's cross-fitting term, donor-weighted only. Together these are round 5's interval `textbook_t|fpc|lin|xf`, the estimator round 8 identified with Williams' $T_1 - T_2$.
- `oracle_lambda`, the fixed population least-squares coefficient; `population_rectifier_var`, the exact design variance $(1 - n_L/G)S_r^2/n_L$ at a fixed coefficient; `contributions_textbook`.
- `johnson_interval`, Johnson's (1978) skewness-adjusted $t$ applied to the contributions, an E1 diagnostic.

**`code/scripts/round9_fewcluster_williams.py`** (header lines 1 to 19). Whole clusters labelled, even $n \in \{4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 16\}$, 20,000 draws per population, seed 20261027. One function, `run_pop`, computes seven arms on the same draws. `R5` is round 5's point estimate with the pooled variance of section 0 item 4 plus the cross-fitting term, $t_{n-1}$. `W_plain`, `W_r5var` and `W_within` are Williams' $T_1 - (1 - n/G)T_2$ with three variances, the last pooled within halves, $(1 - f)s_w^2/n$ with $t_{n-2}$. `FOLD` is the fold-wise form, the unlabelled clusters split at random between the folds, with $\sum_j (N_j/G)^2(1 - n_j/N_j)s^2_{e,S_j}/n_j$ and $t_{n-2}$. `GLOB` is the global least-squares slope with $(1 - f)\,\text{RSS}/\{(n - 2)n\}$ and $t_{n-2}$. `FIX1` is the fixed coefficient 1 with $(1 - f)s^2_{z - f}/n$ and $t_{n-1}$. The coefficient rule in the split arms is round 5's (clipped to $[0, 1]$, zero below 6). Inputs are the ACS state table and `c0_cluster_summaries.csv.gz` (uni_v2 donor means). It is a design simulation over the observed cluster values, not a rerun of round 5's real-task pipeline.

### B9. Round 5's cross-fitted estimator is Williams' (1961) exactly unbiased split-sample regression estimator without his factor $1 - n/G$

1. **Result.** B9. For even $n_L$, $\hat\theta_{\text{R5}} = T_1 - T_2$ and $\hat\theta_{\text{W}} = T_1 - (1 - n/G)T_2$, and round 5's design bias is $\{(n/G)/(1 - n/G)\}\,\text{Cov}(c', \bar f_L)$.
2. **Method.** Exactly unbiased split-sample regression estimator under SRSWOR (Williams 1961; Mickey 1959); design-unbiased learning by subsampling and Rao-Blackwellisation (Sanguiao Sande and Zhang 2020); conditional cross-fitting (Lu et al. 2025; An et al. 2026).
3. **Sources.**

This subagent read the full text from an OCR scan on SciSpace (redirected from typeset.io), sections 1 to 7 and references, with the equations only partly legible. The CUNY Academic Works PDF (record `hc_pubs/52`) was blocked by the proxy (403). Legible points are that eq. (2) is $\bar y + \bar b(\mu_x - \bar x)$ plus a covariance correction weighted by $(1 - n/N)$, "valid for any defined form of the coefficient b"; eq. (13) is a variance estimator with a $k(k-1)$ denominator under the assumption of "a negligible n/N (or k/s) and a fixed set of uncorrelated groups"; section 6 warns it "can be subject to high sampling error, particularly for small" $k$; section 3 gives the optimum number of groups $\sqrt{n/3}$ for least-squares slopes under a normal linear model; section 5 covers two-stage sampling of equal-sized primaries. Round 8 read it in full from the CUNY copy with eqs. (1) and (2) checked against the page image.
   - Mickey, "Some finite population unbiased ratio and regression estimators", JASA 54(287), 594 to 612, 1959, not opened (JSTOR, paywalled). The RAND version P-1348 (1958) has an abstract page, opened, with no PDF link ("Web Only"). The abstract says the paper "provides nonnegative unbiased estimators of estimator variance for a subclass of these estimators" under random sampling without replacement. Worthley (1967, Kansas State M.S. report, `core.ac.uk/download/33363059.pdf`, opened, OCR garbled) describes Mickey's class as $\bar y - a(Z_m)(\bar x - \bar X)$ with $a$ a function of the first $m$ units, averaged over orderings and subsequences, and says "He states an unbiased, non-negative estimator of the variance", built from the squared deviations of the terms $R(m_{j-1}, m_j)$ around their average, for integers $0 < m_0 < \dots < m_k = n$; the formula itself is illegible (secondary).
   - Sanguiao Sande and Zhang, "Design-unbiased statistical learning in survey sampling", arXiv:2003.11423v1, 25 March 2020, full text (sections 1 to 5 from the PDF). Read for E below.
   - Lu, Shi, Liu and Ding, arXiv:2508.15664v1, and An, Dagdoug, Haziza and Tillé, arXiv:2609.10904v1, as in B5.
4. **Where found.** Round 8 positioning note, section 3.2 (C1), 19 October.
5. **Implementation.** Round 5's estimator is `round4_ppi_estimator.lambda_rule('c_crossfit_design')` with `round5_ppi_estimator.design_whole_clusters(..., lin=True, xf=True)`. Neither is in the estimator modules.

## 4. Question R7-6. A fixed against a fitted coefficient at few clusters

What the sources opened in this pass say, without a recommendation.
- **A coefficient fixed in advance keeps a difference estimator design-unbiased, and one fitted on the same sample does not.** Middleton and Aronow, "Unbiased estimation of the average treatment effect in cluster-randomized experiments", Statistics, Politics, and Policy 6(1-2), 39 to 75, 2015, DOI 10.1515/spp-2013-0002, full text (author PDF). Section 5.1, "So long as k is fixed before analysis, this strategy does not lead to bias"; footnote 8 and Appendix B, "estimating k from the same data set can lead to bias"; section 5.3 recommends estimating $k$ from auxiliary data; section 6.5 reports that with weakly predictive covariates "the estimated values of k's tend to miss their mark by a large extent". Aronow and Middleton (2013), full text, section 5.1, fix $\theta_i$ in advance or fit it on units whose assignment is independent of unit $i$, following Williams (1961).
- **A fitted coefficient costs variance at small $n$, and below a correlation threshold it loses to the classical estimator.** Mani et al. (2025), Proposition 4.1 and Corollary 4.1, for i.i.d. Gaussian data, PPI++ beats the classical estimator only when $\lvert\rho\rvert > 1/\sqrt{cn - 2}$, $c = 1$ single-sample and $c = 1/2$ cross-fit; with independent pseudo-labels cross-fit PPI++ has variance $\frac{\sigma_y^2}{n}(1 + \frac{2}{n - 2})$ (Proposition 4.4); at $n = 20$ "no dominant method" (Figure 1). The paper gives no exact variance for the fixed $\lambda = 1$ form and does not recommend fixed over estimated. Eyre and Madras, arXiv:2411.12665v2, abstract only, "the PPI++ method can perform even worse than classical inference" with few labels.
- **With few clusters an estimated covariate coefficient costs degrees of freedom.** Deke, "Design and analysis considerations for cluster randomized controlled trials that have a small number of clusters", Evaluation Review 40(5), 2016, abstract only (publisher summary on mathematica.org), on trials of 10 or fewer clusters, says the benefit of covariate adjustment depends on whether precision gained outweighs degrees of freedom lost. Moerbeek and van Schie, BMC Medical Research Methodology 16, 79, 2016, DOI 10.1186/s12874-016-0182-7, full text summary, use $n_2 - 3$ degrees of freedom for the adjusted model against $n_2 - 2$.
- **Williams' own result on the number of groups.** For least-squares slopes under a normal linear model the optimum number of groups is $\sqrt{n/3}$, which at $n$ of 6 to 16 is about 1.4 to 2.3, so two groups is the smallest permissible choice in that range (Williams 1961, section 3, garbled OCR; this subagent's arithmetic).
- **Survey-sampling textbook treatment.** Cochran (1977), chapter 7, compares the regression estimator with a preassigned coefficient (the difference estimator, exactly unbiased with variance $(1 - f)S^2_{y - b_0 x}/n$) and the estimated one (bias of order $1/n$). Not opened in this pass; `[verify]`.
- **The record's own evidence.** Round 9 found `FIX1` with the smallest or near-smallest root mean squared error at $n \le 10$ on every task (`docs/round09/i03/fewcluster/round09_fewcluster_williams.md`, section 2 item 6), and the `GLOB` arm biased (fraction of populations with bias below three Monte Carlo SE 0.18 on CCRCC at $n = 8$, `i34_williams_summary_hest.csv`, column `frac_bias_z_lt3`, read from the file).

What the sources do not give is a comparison of a fixed $\lambda = 1$ against a cross-fitted, clipped coefficient under SRSWOR of 4 to 16 clusters with the design target. That comparison is round 9's own simulation. It is a question for the final runs, not a literature gap, since each piece (fixed-coefficient difference estimator, its exact variance, the cost of estimating the coefficient) is classical or in print.
