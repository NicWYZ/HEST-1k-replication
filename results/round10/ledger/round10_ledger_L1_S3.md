### A1. Spot-level split conformal under-covers for a new donor, and coverage follows the calibration design, not the encoder

- **Existing method.** Split (inductive) conformal prediction with the absolute residual score, and the locally weighted (`scaled`) score. The under-coverage is the known consequence of calibration and test points not being exchangeable.
- **Sources.** Lei, G'Sell, Rinaldo, Tibshirani and Wasserman, "Distribution-Free Predictive Inference for Regression", JASA 2018 (venue as the record cites it), arXiv:1604.04173 v2. Abstract only here; the record read the full text and searched it on 7 October (`docs/round05/tracks/conformal/round5_conf_theory.md`, reading log). The abstract names split conformal and bands of "locally varying length". Papadopoulos et al. (2002), the origin of split conformal as GHCP cites it, not opened (not searched for). Barber, Candès, Ramdas and Tibshirani, "Conformal prediction beyond exchangeability", arXiv:2202.13415 v5 (Annals of Statistics 2023 as the record cites it), abstract only; the abstract does not show the form of the coverage-loss bound.
- **Where the record found them.** `docs/round03/tracks/exec/round3_execution_plan.md` (reading list, lines 753 to 757); `docs/round03/00_prep/round3_oversight_handoff.md`, item 4.
- **Implementation.** `code/scripts/round3_a0_harness.py` (`build_fold_specs`, `choose_calibration`, `conformal_quantile`, `interval_metrics`, `run_one`); tables by `code/scripts/round3_a1_tables.py`.
- **What the record validated.** With the absolute score at 0.90, coverage is 0.8937 (random), 0.8585 (slide_out), 0.8024 (donor) and 0.7862 (patient), over 132 cells, 12 encoders and 11 tasks for the first, second and fourth designs and 36 cells and 3 tasks for slide_out (`results/round3/A1_coverage/a1_coverage_by_design.csv`). The stage report rounds these to 0.894, 0.859, 0.802 and 0.786 (`docs/round03/i01/exec/round3_A1_stage_report.md`, coverage table). The ordering by design is the same for every encoder (survey, A1).
- **Missing for the paper.** The split conformal source read in full and cited by section (Lei et al. 2018 section 2; Vovk, Gammerman and Shafer 2005 for the guarantee). A citable statement of the coverage loss under non-exchangeability (Barber et al. 2023, theorem to be read at L2). The 12-encoder result predates the corpus extension and is on the benchmark tasks, so the paper must say which tasks it rests on. Rerun only if the final runs change the head.
- **Gap.** None. The method and the reason for its failure are in print; the measurement is the paper's.

### A3. Feature-based weighted conformal cannot repair donor shift here

Bhattacharyya and Barber, "Group-Weighted Conformal Prediction", arXiv:2401.17452 v4 (15 April 2025), abstract only, the group-level special case where weights are known by group. Kish's effective sample size not opened (classical; the record does not cite a source for it).

### A4. HCP is valid once $K + 1 \ge 1/\alpha$ and conservative

- **Existing method.** Hierarchical conformal prediction (HCP), the weighted quantile that gives each calibration group mass $1/(K+1)$ spread over its spots and the test group an atom at $+\infty$.
- **Sources.** Lee, Barber and Willett (as in A2), the method's source. Dunn, Wasserman and Ramdas (as in A2), the predecessor, whose repeated subsampling they recommend when a guarantee is wanted (abstract, read here). GHCP's section 1.3 places both, read here (see A6).
- **Where the record found them.** `docs/round03/i02/exec/round3_A1_decisions.md`, section 3, which transcribes Theorem 1 (coverage at least $1 - \alpha$ for any $K \ge 1$, upper bound $1 - \alpha + 2/(K+1)$ with distinct scores) and the finiteness condition $1/(K+1) \le \alpha$.
- **Implementation.** `code/scripts/round3_a4b_hcp.py` (through `round3_a3_weighted.weighted_quantile` and `w3_weights`); `code/scripts/round4_conf_sim.py` (`hcp_q`, `m_hcp`); `code/scripts/round4_conf_c3.py` (`o0_rows`); checked against the released code by `code/scripts/round4_conf_c1_codepath.py`.
- **What the record validated.** At $K = 10$ on CCRCC, HCP covers 0.9692 against the pooled quantile's 0.8644, at 1.9465 times the pooled width, falling to 1.3604 at $K = 20$, means over encoders at $\alpha = 0.1$ (`results/round4/conformal/C3_real/c3_report_numbers.csv`, rows C3.1). The C3 anchor reproduces round 3's `a4b_hcp_K10__<enc>.csv` (`docs/round04/i02/conformal/round4_conf_final_report.md`, section 3).
- **Missing for the paper.** Theorem 1 and the HCP definition cited by equation number from the published version. The corpus tasks at the donor level need their own HCP rows (L0 scope, section 3).
- **Gap.** None.

### Institution axis. Leave-one-center-out calibration on the corpus tasks

- **Existing method.** Two methods apply as they stand. HCP with centers as the groups (Lee, Barber and Willett), which treats centers as exchangeable and is finite only when the number of calibration centers $K_c$ satisfies $K_c + 1 \ge 1/\alpha$. Leave-one-center-out evaluation, the standard held-out-site design, which the federated track already uses (`docs/round07/tracks/fedlearn/round07_fedlearn_plan.md`, line 85). Donor-level HCP pooled across centers is the A1 analogue and inherits its failure if centers shift. Parametric routes from small area estimation borrow across areas under a working model (Bersson and Hoff; Chatterjee, Lahiri and Li).
- **Sources.** Lee, Barber and Willett (as in A2); its section 1.1 setup is two-level, with hospitals and patients among its examples. Dunn, Wasserman and Ramdas (as in A2), two-level. Duchi et al. (as in A9), multi-environment, abstract only. Lu, Yu, Karimireddy, Jordan and Raskar, "Federated Conformal Predictors for Distributed Uncertainty Quantification", ICML 2023, arXiv:2305.17564 v2, abstract only; partial exchangeability across clients, and the abstract does not say whether coverage is for a new client. Liu, Levis, Normand and Han, "Multi-Source Conformal Inference Under Distribution Shift", arXiv:2405.09331 v1, abstract only; target population with several possibly biased sources. Shahid, "When Average Calibration Fails: Site-Conditional Federated Conformal Risk Control", arXiv:2606.20115 v3, abstract only; site-conditional control for the 20 existing institutions of FeTS-2022, not a new site. Bersson and Hoff, "Optimal Conformal Prediction for Small Areas", arXiv:2204.08122 v1, abstract only; an area-level conformal region using a working model's posterior predictive as the score. Chatterjee, Lahiri and Li, "Parametric bootstrap approximation to the distribution of EBLUP and related prediction intervals in linear mixed models", arXiv:0806.2931, DOI 10.1214/07-AOS512, abstract only.
- **Implementation.** None in this repository for conformal calibration by center. The federated project's leave-one-center-out code is in `NicWYZ/ST-Fed-Learn` and round 7's `code/scripts/round7_fedlearn_v2.py` (V2, "within against leave one center out", `docs/round07/i02/fedlearn/round07_fedlearn_final_report.md`, line 78), which scores point predictions, not sets.
- **What the record validated.** Nothing yet. D7 records that the benchmark subset of HEST-1k has no institution axis.
- **What the existing condition implies on the candidate tasks.** The L0 scope lists centers per task as kidney Visium 6, lung Xenium 4, brain Visium 9, bowel Visium 10, breast Xenium 5 and breast Visium 9 (`docs/round10/i01/ledger/round10_ledger_L0_scope.md`, section 3). Holding one center out leaves $K_c$ equal to the count minus one, so $K_c + 1$ equals the center count, and HCP over centers at $\alpha = 0.1$ is finite only where there are at least 10 centers. That is bowel Visium alone, at exactly 10. At $\alpha = 0.2$ the condition is 5 centers, met by every listed task except lung Xenium. This is arithmetic on Lee, Barber and Willett's condition, and A8's impossibility (Tibshirani, Barber and Ramdas) says no valid method does better at the center level without assumptions.
- **Missing for the paper.** The final-runs data stage's confirmed center and donor counts from the registry. A full read of Lee, Barber and Willett's appendix C and Duchi et al. to see whether a three-level (center, donor, spot) construction is stated. Federated conformal (Lu et al.) read in full for whether its guarantee covers a new client.
- **Gap.** Not established. A distribution-free prediction set for a spot of a new center that uses the nesting of donors within centers was not found in four searches (`round10_ledger_L1_S3_queries.csv`, the two three-level queries, the multi-site query and the pathology multi-center query), and the federated, multi-source and small-area hits are either for existing sites, assume a working model, or do not say. Because existing methods do apply (center-level HCP, donor-level HCP as a comparator, parametric small-area intervals), and because the searches ran through a summarising tool with abstracts only, this is recorded as a not-found form, not a gap. Searches that failed to find it are logged with survey sampling and econometrics terms as well as machine learning.

## The GHCP full-text check that round 8 asked for

Round 8 asked whether GHCP compares its sets with the full conformal set built from the target group's own residuals, and what baselines it uses (`docs/round08/i01/positioning/round08_positioning_note.md`, section 3.10). From the HTML v1, read through the fetch tool in two passes.

1. **Baselines.** Section 3.1.1 compares GHCP ($\eta = 0.5$) with HCP. Section 3.1.2 compares GHCP with and without within-group training, with HCP as a baseline (Table 4). Section 3.2, on ACS PUMS, compares GHCP, HCP and Std-CP. Appendix D.1 compares GHCP with a random forest against GHCP with the Bayes-optimal predictor. ## Question P. What the literature says about prediction sets and inference in one paper

Collected for the L3 recommendation; nothing is decided here.

- **TISSUE** (Sun, Ma, Navarro Negredo, Brunet and Zou, Nature Methods 21, 444 to 454, 2024, DOI 10.1038/s41592-024-02184-y). Full text (fetch) of the authors' PDF, first 100000 of 109313 characters. It is a spatial transcriptomics paper that gives conformal prediction intervals for predicted expression ("TISSUE prediction intervals for predicted gene expression") and then uses them for downstream inference, a multiple-imputation hypothesis test for differential expression ("Uncertainty-aware hypothesis testing with TISSUE"), clustering and classification, in one paper.
