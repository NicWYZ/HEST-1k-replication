## 1. What changes for L3

1. **Question E. Mickey (1959) supplies the exact variance, and Williams' form is a member of Mickey's class.** Mickey's estimator $t_\alpha$ (his equations 2.2 to 2.5, p. 596) fits any coefficient on the first $\alpha$ draws, keeps those draws as observed, and estimates the mean of the rest of the population by the difference estimator on the remaining $n - \alpha$ draws. It is exactly unbiased under simple random sampling without replacement because $E\{t_\alpha \mid Z_\alpha\} = \mu_y$ (2.6). Section 4 (pp. 600 to 601) gives, for a nested chain of $k$ such pieces, the average (4.1) with the variance estimator
$$
s^2(\bar t) = \frac{1}{k(k - 1)}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\big[t(\alpha_j, \alpha_{j+1}) - \bar t\big]^2 \quad (4.2),
$$
exact and nonnegative, with $k - 1$ degrees of freedom. For an average over $m$ orderings of the draws it gives (4.3) with
$$
s^2(T_m) = \frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}s^2_{\gamma_i} - \frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\big(\bar t_{\gamma_i} - T_m\big)^2 \quad (4.4),
$$
exact, and not said to be nonnegative.
   - Williams' $k = 2$ estimator, as round 8's note writes it (section 3.2), is Mickey's $t_{n/2}$ averaged over the two orders of the halves. After algebra that is skipped, the correction term of (2.2) averages to $(1 - n/N)\frac{1}{4}(b_A - b_B)(\bar x_A - \bar x_B)$. Sanguiao Sande and Zhang's single split, as round 8 and L1 describe it, is $t_\alpha$ with $Z_\alpha = s_1$.
   - **The oversight chat's reading of section 4, `[proposed]`.** Since $\text{var}(t_\alpha) = E\{\text{var}(t_\alpha \mid Z_\alpha)\}$ by (2.6), the classical estimator $s^2_A = \{(N - \alpha)/N\}^2\{1 - (n - \alpha)/(N - \alpha)\}\,s^2_{e,B}/(n - \alpha)$ on the residuals $e = y - b_A x$ of the second half is unbiased for $\text{var}(t_\alpha)$ when $n - \alpha \ge 2$. Mickey's (4.5) and (4.6) need only that each ordering's estimator has the same distribution, so with the two orders of the halves
$$
\hat V\big(\hat\theta_{\text{Williams}}\big) = \tfrac{1}{2}\big(s^2_A + s^2_B\big) - \tfrac{1}{4}\big(t_A - t_B\big)^2
$$
is exactly unbiased for the variance of Williams' $k = 2$ form at any $n/G$. This is the piece round 8's note (section 3.2) and round 9's Williams step (`docs/round09/i03/fewcluster/round09_fewcluster_williams.md`, section 3) recorded as not yet written. It applies Mickey's section 4 and is not a new result. Mickey does not display it, though, so it is the oversight chat's reading until Nicolas has seen it, and until Mickey's 1960 corrigendum has been read.
   - **Check.** An exact enumeration over every sample of $n = 4, 6, 8$ and every split into halves, on two toy populations ($N = 10$ nonlinear, $N = 12$ skewed), with the unclipped slope and round 5's rule, found every identity to hold to rounding. Williams' form equalled the average of the two $t_{n/2}$, both were unbiased, $E(\hat V)/\text{var} = 1.0$, and Mickey's chain with $k = 2$ was unbiased with $E\{s^2(\bar t)\}/\text{var}(\bar t) = 1.0$ (`checks/mickey_williams_check.py` and `checks/mickey_williams_check_output.txt`, 38 seconds in the cloud workspace). The same output shows the weakness. $\hat V$ was negative in a fraction of samples from 0.0013 (nonlinear population, round 5's rule, $n = 8$) to 0.336 (skewed population, unclipped slope, $n = 4$).
   - **What the textbooks add.** Cochran cites Mickey twice: section 6.15 (book p. 175), for the ratio form (6.76), noting that it coincides with Hartley-Ross at $n = 2$ (p. 177); and section 7.11 (p. 203), which names Mickey and Williams as unbiased regression-type estimators "not yet extensively tried" and reports Rao (1969) finding Mickey's usually inferior. He gives no variance estimator for either. Särndal et al. cite Mickey once (section 7.3.1, p. 251, ratio estimators) and never Williams. Neither book fits a coefficient on part of the sample and applies it to the rest. None of the PPI papers read (Broska and Howes, Kluger et al., Chen et al.) gives an exactly unbiased cross-fitted form or a variance valid when $n/G$ is not small.
   - **For a coefficient fixed in advance nothing changes.** Cochran's Theorem 7.1 (eqs. 7.3, 7.4, book p. 190) and Särndal et al.'s Result 6.3.1 (eqs. 6.3.4 to 6.3.6, pp. 222 to 223) give the difference estimator with a preassigned coefficient, exactly unbiased under any relation between $y$ and $x$, with exact variance and an exactly unbiased variance estimator (Cochran 7.8).
2. **Prediction P10.2, first half: held.** The masterplan predicted that the ledger would find a published variance estimator for the exactly unbiased split-sample form under simple random sampling without replacement. Mickey (1959), section 4, gives one for his chain subclass and for averages over orderings. The second half (no published interval with a finite-sample coverage guarantee for the design target at 4 to 16 clusters without a restriction on the population) is consistent with every gated source read here: every condition found is asymptotic. It is scored at L3 after the open sources.
3. **Question V. No source read gives a bound on one cluster's share of the variance.**
   - Bickel and Freedman (1984), Theorem 3 (p. 473), covers stratified sampling without replacement under (5) $2 \le n_i \le N_i - 1$ and the Lindeberg condition (6) on the units. Under uniformly bounded third moments they note that (6) follows from $\max_i v_i/\sqrt{\rho_i} \to 0$, with the "variance weight" $v_i^2$ (which corresponds to round 9's share $v_h$, not $v_i$) and the "effective sample size" $\rho_i = n_i(N_i - 1)/(N_i - n_i)$. In their words the normal approximation holds if, for each stratum, the variance weight contribution is small or the stratum is heavily sampled. The proof's Case 3 (p. 480) allows a bounded number of strata whose share does not shrink if they are heavily sampled. So the condition reduces to "the largest share tends to zero" only when every stratum's sample stays bounded, which is regime B. L1's reading of it as an asymptotic form of $v_h \le v_0$, "unless the stratum is nearly a census", needs that qualification. Condition (5) excludes take-all strata. The V reader flags, without resolving, that the printed $\rho_i$ admits a single stratum with $N - n$ small and fixed, which p. 474 says should not be expected to be normal; "heavily sampled" should not be read as "nearly a census".
   - Krewski and Rao (1981) is further from V than L1 says. Primary units are drawn with replacement, the limit is $L \to \infty$ strata with $n_h = O(1)$, and condition C3, $\max_h W_h = O(L^{-1})$, bounds each stratum's population share $W_h = M_h/M$, not its share of the variance. Their small-$L$ results (section 4) are model-based and about bias and stability, not coverage.
   - Cochran section 2.15 (pp. 39 to 44) gives Hájek's condition in Lindeberg form, the one-sided misses under positive skew, the rule $n > 25G_1^2$ (2.69), and the advice to enumerate extreme units completely. He gives no numeric bound on a stratum's share.
   - The ledger's search for V continues with the open sources (Hidiroglou 1986 and Bloznelis 1999 among them) before L3.
4. **B13. The classical impossibility results do not cover a finite population of $G$ clusters sampled without replacement.**
   - Bahadur and Savage (1956) need independent, possibly sequential, draws from a convex class of distributions in which every mean occurs. L1's "Corollary III" is Corollary 3, p. 1117. Their proof moves probability mass that tends to zero (eq. 13). With $G$ equally weighted clusters the smallest mass that can be moved is $1/G$.
   - Gleser and Hwang (1987), Theorem 1, needs parametric densities with common support and a limit of densities (1.5), and concludes only that the diameter is infinite with positive probability. Its full title ends "in errors-in-variables and related models".
   - Read plainly, the two papers support crediting round 9 with the proof technique and the impossibility for infinite families. They do not contain round 9's finite-population lemma or Theorems 1 to 3. Round 9's description of its lemma as Bahadur and Savage's total-variation form "with total variation $n/G$" is an analogy, since round 9's bound is $1 - \pi^k P_F(N \le k)$. Whether Theorems 1 to 3 are claimed is still Nicolas's decision (O5), and hard rule 5 still applies.
5. **R7-6 (fixed against fitted coefficient).**
   - Cochran section 7.3 to 7.7: with a fitted $b$ the variance $\frac{1-f}{n}S_y^2(1 - \rho^2)$ holds only for large samples (7.25), against the exact minimum at the population coefficient (Theorem 7.2, 7.11). The exact cost of a misplaced fixed coefficient is (7.17), the fitted-coefficient bias is of order $1/n$ with leading term (7.38), Rao (1969) found the regression estimate's mean square error over the ratio estimate's to average 1.15, 1.36 and 1.51 at $n$ of 12, 8 and 6 (p. 197), and Table 7.1 (p. 198) shows the large-sample formula (7.25) underestimating the variance by 38%, 34% and 28% at $n$ of 6, 8 and 12, and the sample estimate (7.29) by 48%, 42% and 33%.
   - Särndal et al. section 6.8 gives the optimal coefficient (Montanari) and its sample estimate, but no bias, variance or variance estimator for the estimated coefficient. Their only statement on that cost is the general one for the regression estimator, that bias and variance are of order $1/n$ (Remarks 6.4.2(b) and 6.6.2). Exercise 6.4 (p. 243) shows that under simple random sampling a fixed coefficient beats the $\pi$ estimator when it lies between 0 and twice the optimum.
   - Kluger et al. (section 4.5) say the untuned estimator is best avoided, in their setting of many labelled units.
   - Shao and Wang (2014), Theorem 2 and eq. (3.2), with $H \to \infty$ strata and $n_h$ bounded: no general winner between a pooled slope and slopes per stratum, and a slope per stratum needs $n_h > 2$. This bears on regime B by analogy only, and says nothing about a coefficient fitted per half.
   - Nothing read compares a fixed coefficient with a fitted one at 4 to 16 clusters under simple random sampling without replacement. That comparison remains round 9's simulation and the final runs', as L1 S4 section 4 said.
6. **The formulas the paper would display, with their textbook sources.**
   - **Exact and design-based.** The difference estimator with a preassigned coefficient, as in item 1. For clusters and two stages, apply Särndal et al.'s Result 4.2.1 (p. 128; simple random cluster sampling (4.2.10) to (4.2.12), p. 129) or Result 4.3.1 (p. 137, (4.3.8) to (4.3.14)) to $D_k = y_k - y_k^0$. Equivalently, apply Cochran's Theorems 10.1 and 10.2 (eqs. 10.8, 10.14, 10.15, 10.21, pp. 277 to 279) for equal sizes and (11.22), (11.24) (p. 303) for unequal sizes. Cochran's notes on Theorem 10.2 cover both of the project's regimes: $f_2 = 1$ is one-stage sampling of whole clusters (regime A), and $f_1 = 1$ is proportional stratification with the clusters as strata (regime B). Neither book prints the two-stage difference estimator with an auxiliary variable as one formula, so the paper states the combination and cites both pieces.
   - **Approximate only.** Särndal et al.'s chapter 8 estimators (Results 8.4.1, 8.9.1 and 8.9.2) all estimate coefficients and have Taylor-approximation variances with g-weighted estimators. The authors warn the approximations may be crude in small samples (p. 333). So L1 S4 B2's "chapter 8 is the one to read" should point to sections 6.3, 4.2 and 4.3 for the exact formula. The ratio-to-size versions (Cochran 11.27, 11.30; 9A.2, 9A.4) are approximate too.
   - **Ultimate-cluster variance.** Särndal et al. do not use the term. With primary units drawn with replacement, (4.5.3) is exactly unbiased (Result 4.5.1, p. 151). Without replacement, $V^\ast$ (4.3.17) underestimates by $\sum V_i$ (4.3.18), is exact when clusters are observed whole, and had relative bias −0.017 in the book's example (p. 140). $V^{\ast\ast}$ (4.6.2) overestimates exactly when sampling without replacement beats sampling with replacement (p. 154).
7. **Form C is the combined regression estimator, not the separate one.** The record's form C is a pooled within-cluster slope with each cluster's own intercept (header of `code/scripts/round5_ppi_twolevel.py`, lines 8 and 23 to 24). In Cochran that is the combined regression estimate (7.50) with the pooled within-stratum slope (p. 202). In Shao and Wang it is $\hat Y_{CS}$, and in Särndal et al. it is not the separate estimator (7.8.13), which has a slope per group. L1 S6 row B6 item 2 names it "the separate (within-stratum) regression estimator with a pooled slope", which is the wrong name; the paper should call it the combined regression estimator with stratum (cluster) means. Cochran 7.11 (p. 203) says the separate estimate is more exposed to bias and to error in the coefficients when strata are small.
8. **The PPI papers.**
   - Broska and Howes (arXiv:2610.09601 v1, 7 October 2026): one $\lambda$ and no cluster intercept (cluster fixed effects are named as future work), clusters as i.i.d. draws with Bernoulli labelling, a variance divided by $G$ with a normal quantile, no finite-population factor, and section 4 states that the variance estimator has "no small-sample correction" (confirmed against the text). The record's `bh_t` and `bh_t_fpc` are the project's own corrections, which bears on R7-5. L1 S6 item 7(iii) overstates their section 4, which names only small-sample corrections in general and the wild cluster bootstrap.
   - Kluger et al. (arXiv:2501.18577 v3, 15 November 2025), Appendix B.1: Bernoulli labelling of clusters, resampling of all clusters with replacement, weights $\xi/\pi$ and $(1 - \xi)/(1 - \pi)$, a percentile interval, and $\hat\Omega$ chosen once from the bootstrap draws. This matches the record's description of their Algorithm 5. The under-coverage they tie to clustering is the classical interval's at about 50 labelled clusters.
   - Chen, Zhou, Peng and Wang, "Balanced Active Inference", NeurIPS 2025 (the record's Chen et al.): theory asymptotic in units, with a vanishing-dependence assumption (Assumption 4), coefficient fixed at 1, and predictor trained on separate data. Their Table 1 shows the cube-active design under-covering on 2 of 11 datasets (0.8025 and 0.8700 at nominal 0.9), which bears on R7-7. The variance estimators are in their Supplement S2, which is not in the file.
   - Fuller (2009, Biometrika, DOI 10.1093/biomet/asp042): Theorem 1 is for the regression estimator, not `rej_t`'s simple mean, and is asymptotic ($o(n_N^{-1})$). His Monte Carlo stops at $n = 20$ and about 90% rejection, against the project's 8 clusters at 99%. His eq. (16), that the Horvitz-Thompson variance with the basic design's probabilities stays consistent after rejection, bears on E4's simulated inclusion probabilities. He advises stratifying first and rejecting second, which bears on R7-7.

## 3. Corrections to L1

1. **`williams.pdf` is not Williams (1961).** The identifier L1 S4 gave for Williams, DOI 10.2307/2527991 `[verify]`, is Gates and Beard, "Rapid chi-square test of significance for three-part ratios", Biometrics 17(2), 261 to 266, 1961, the article before Williams' in the same issue, and that is the file supplied. Williams, "Generating unbiased ratio and regression estimators", Biometrics 17(2), 267 to 274, 1961, still has to be read from a clean copy, so his equations (1), (2), (13) and the section 3 ratio quoted in L1 S4 stay as round 8 read them and unchecked. Cochran's reference list (p. 411) prints the entry as "Williams, W. H. (1963). The precision of some unbiased regression estimators. Biometrika, 17, 267-274", which mixes Williams' 1961 volume and pages with another year, title and journal, so Williams is not to be cited through Cochran.
2. **Shao and Wang's DOI** is 10.5705/ss.2012.064, not .074 (printed on the article). The issue number is not printed and stays `[verify]`.
3. **Form C's name** (S6 B6 item 2): the combined regression estimator, not the separate one (section 1, item 7).
4. **Bickel and Freedman's condition** (S5 section 3.4): stated with the qualification of section 1, item 3.
5. **Krewski and Rao's C3** bounds a stratum's population share, not its variance share; L1's paraphrase (through Yung and Rao) is not to be attributed to them.
6. **Gleser and Hwang were read in the original in round 9.** L1 S5.3 item 4 says no round 9 check read them in the original. NOV9 read all 12 pages as images (`results/round9/fewcluster/novelty/novelty_NOV9_structured.json`; `docs/round09/i02/fewcluster/round09_fewcluster_sources_and_median.md`, line 7). The record contradicts itself there, since `round09_fewcluster_tuned_novelty.md`, line 9, says abstracts.
7. **Sanguiao Sande and Zhang's Monte Carlo variance** as L1 S4 transcribed it (a subtracted term with $1/\{K(K - 1)\}$) does not match Mickey's (4.4) for the variance of the average over orderings. Which is right for which target (the average or the full Rao-Blackwellisation) is checked when their paper is reopened.
8. **Section pointers.** Ratio-to-size is Cochran section 9A.1 and 11.8 (eqs. 9A.2, 9A.4), not chapter 9. The regression estimators for strata are Cochran sections 7.9 to 7.11, not 7.10 alone. The exact two-stage formula is Särndal et al. sections 6.3, 4.2 and 4.3, not chapter 8. Neyman (1934) is printed in Cochran's list as pp. 558 to 606, against L1's 558 to 625 (probably the printed discussion). The ratio-estimator Example 6.8.1 and (7.8.10), p. 273, give the $1 - r^2$ form for B4. S5's "chapter 9 `[verify]`" in Särndal et al. is section 9.4 (Result 9.4.1, p. 351).
9. **Bibliographic confirmations.** Bickel and Freedman 12(2), 470 to 482; Krewski and Rao 9(5), 1010 to 1019; Mickey JASA 54(287), 594 to 612; Cassel, Särndal and Wretman (1976), Biometrika 63, 615 to 620 (Särndal et al. p. 667); Hájek (1960), Publ. Math. Inst. Hungarian Acad. Sci. 5, 361 to 374; Rao (1973), Biometrika 60, 125 to 133; Hartley and Ross (1954), Nature 174, 270 to 271 (Cochran's list). The `[verify]` marks on these can come off.
