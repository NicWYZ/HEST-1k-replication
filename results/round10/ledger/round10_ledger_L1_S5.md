### S5.1 (B8) Under-coverage of the design interval at few donors, and skewed donor contributions

1. **Result.** Round 4's corrected design interval for CCRCC $\theta_3$ covers 0.840 to 0.8825 at $n_L \ge 6$ (donor population, design target; read back from `results/round4/ppi/Q5a/q5a_design_variance_before_after.csv`, column `coverage_median_after`; the survey rounds this to 0.840 to 0.883), and the Spearman correlation of a gene's coverage with $|\text{skewness}|$ of its donor contributions is $-0.811$ with median $|\text{skewness}|$ 1.758 for `hoptimus0` (read back from `results/round5/ppi/E1_interval/e1_real_coverage_vs_skewness.csv`; the survey's 1.76).
2. **Existing method.** The design-based $t$ interval for the mean of cluster values under simple random sampling of clusters without replacement, with the finite-population factor $1 - n/G$. That its normal approximation needs the largest squared deviation to be negligible is Hájek's (1960) condition for the finite-population central limit theorem; the finite-sample counterpart is the Berry-Esseen bound for the finite-population Student statistic.
3. **Sources.**
   - Li and Ding (2017), "General forms of finite population central limit theorems with applications to causal inference", JASA 112(520), 1759 to 1769, DOI 10.1080/01621459.2017.1295865, arXiv:1610.04821. Full text (summary), section 2.1 (Theorem 1, attributed to Hájek 1960, condition (4)) and appendix A.1. With $m_N = \max_i (y_i - \bar y)^2$ and $v_N = \sum_i (y_i - \bar y)^2/(N - 1)$ the condition is
     $$\frac{1}{\min(n, N - n)}\,\frac{m_N}{v_N} \to 0, \qquad \frac{m_N}{v_N} = (N - 1)\,\kappa(y),$$
     so it is stated in the same max-share $\kappa$ that Theorem 1 of round 9 bounds (the identity on the right is arithmetic on the two definitions).
   - Hájek (1960), Publ. Math. Inst. Hungar. Acad. Sci. 5. Not opened (no online copy found; statement taken from Li and Ding).
   - Bloznelis (1999), "A Berry-Esseen bound for finite population Student's statistic", Annals of Probability 27(4), 2089 to 2108, DOI 10.1214/aop/1022874830. Not opened (Project Euclid bot wall). Round 9's NOV1 and NOV11 read the abstract.
   - Cochran (1977), *Sampling Techniques*, 3rd ed., chapters 2 and 9 for the interval. Table of contents only (Wiley).
4. **Where the record found it.** Hájek and Bloznelis in round 9's novelty map (`results/round9/fewcluster/novelty/novelty_NOV1_WORSTCASE_candidates.csv`, `novelty_NOV3_IMPOSSIBILITY_candidates.csv`, `novelty_NOV11_CRIT_candidates.csv`), and in the frontier note's literature section (`docs/round09/i01/fewcluster/round09_fewcluster_frontier.md`, section 6). 5. **Implementation.** `code/scripts/round4_ppi_estimator.py` (`estimate_textbook`, `var_textbook`, the `textbook_t|fpc|lin` interval), `code/scripts/round5_ppi_estimator.py` (`design_whole_clusters`), and `code/scripts/round5_ppi_e1_diag.py` (`--moments` writes the skewness of the $G$ contributions to `e1_contribution_moments.csv`; the merge writes `e1_real_coverage_vs_skewness.csv`).

### S5.3 (B13) The frontier, Theorems 1 to 3, and the required number of clusters

1. **Result.** Under simple random sampling of $n$ of $G$ clusters, a location and scale equivariant interval is valid over all populations with residual max-share
   $$\kappa(e) = \frac{\max_g (e_g - \bar e)^2}{\sum_h (e_h - \bar e)^2} \le \kappa_0$$
   with a finite critical value exactly when $\kappa_0 < \kappa_2(k^\ast - 1)$, where $\kappa_2(k) = (1 - k/G)/k$ and $k^\ast$ is the smallest $k$ with $\pi(k) = \{\binom{G-k}{n} + \binom{k}{n}\}/\binom{G}{n} \le \alpha$. At $\alpha = 0.10$ the frontier `kappa_sup` is 0.158333 at 8 of 24 ($k^\ast = 6$) and 0.071301 at 8 of 51 ($k^\ast = 12$) (read back from `results/round9/fewcluster/feasibility/s3_frontier_exact.csv`). Theorem 2 (coefficient fitted on the sample) gives a frontier about half as large, with median $\kappa^\dagger$ 0.0907, 0.0860 and 0.1484 on CCRCC, INDIANA_KIDNEY and LUNG_XENIUM against median residual max-shares 0.533, 0.336 and 0.312, and a share beyond of 1.000, 1.000 and 0.997 (read back from `results/round9/fewcluster/exact/i26_real_exact_summary.csv`). Theorem 3 is the stratified product form. At $G = 24$ a scale-free guarantee needs 13 labelled clusters at $\Lambda = 3$ and 17 at $\Lambda = 4$ (read back from `results/round9/fewcluster/feasibility/s3_required_n.csv`, rows $G = 24$, $\alpha = 0.1$).
2. **Existing methods it rests on.** (a) The nonexistence of bounded valid confidence procedures over convex classes without restriction (Bahadur and Savage), and its forms for confidence sets of finite expected diameter (Gleser and Hwang) and for locally almost unidentified parameters (Dufour). (b) The iid threshold $n < 1 + \log_2(1/\alpha)$ below which the $t$ interval is unbounded at the symmetric two-point law (Hendriks, IJzerman-Boon and Klaassen; Lanke for the median). (c) The finite-sample leverage condition for size control of heteroskedasticity-robust tests (Pötscher and Preinerstorfer), the closest finite-sample analogue of a max-share condition. (d) The asymptotic max-share condition (Hájek 1960 via Li and Ding, S5.1) and its stratified form (Bickel and Freedman 1984, section 3 of this document).
3. **Sources.**
   - Bahadur and Savage (1956), "The nonexistence of certain statistical procedures in nonparametric problems", Annals of Mathematical Statistics 27(4), 1115 to 1122, DOI 10.1214/aoms/1177728077. Original not opened (Project Euclid). Lecture notes restating it opened (C. Chen, December 2023, `catherineycchen.github.io/data/Nonexistence_Nonparametric.pdf`), Corollary III says that over a convex class in which every real mean is attained, an interval bounded below with probability one under some member has infimum coverage zero. Secondary, and not a citable source.
   - Gleser and Hwang (1987), Annals of Statistics 15(4), 1351 to 1362, DOI 10.1214/aos/1176350597. Not opened (Project Euclid bot wall).
   - Dufour (1997), "Some impossibility theorems in econometrics with applications to structural and dynamic models", Econometrica 65(6), 1365 to 1388, DOI 10.2307/2171740. Full text (summary) of the author's posted version (compiled February 2000, stating the Econometrica reference), section 3, Theorem 3.6 and Corollary 3.7. A valid confidence set for a locally almost unidentified parameter is unbounded with positive probability.
   - Hendriks, IJzerman-Boon and Klaassen (2006), "Student's t-statistic under unimodal densities", Austrian Journal of Statistics 35(2 and 3), 131 to 141, DOI 10.17713/ajs.v35i2&3.361. Full text (summary), sections 1 to 8 named. The two-point law $P(X = \pm 1) = 1/2$ gives $P(T_n = \infty) = 2^{1-n}$, so no bounded valid $t$ interval exists when $n < 1 - \log\alpha/\log 2$; the uniform law is least favourable among symmetric unimodal laws to second order.
   - Lanke (1974), "Interval estimation of a median", Scandinavian Journal of Statistics 1, 28 to 32 (pages from the reference list of Paul and Kuchibhotla). Not opened here; read in full by round 9's NOV9 (`docs/round09/i02/fewcluster/round09_fewcluster_sources_and_median.md`, section 1).
   - Paul and Kuchibhotla, "Inference for median and a generalization of HulC", arXiv:2403.06357. Full text (summary), Remark 1 and the conjecture. Lanke's stated threshold $\log_2(2/\alpha)$ is false without shift invariance and his proof gives $\log_2(1/\alpha)$.
   - Pötscher and Preinerstorfer, "A necessary and sufficient condition for size controllability of heteroskedasticity robust test statistics", arXiv:2412.17470v4 (the record gives Econometric Theory 2026, `[verify]`). Full text (summary), Theorem 2.2. Size control by a finite critical value holds if and only if $h_{ii} < 1$ for every $i$ with $R(X^\top X)^{-1}x_i^\top \ne 0$, with $h_{ii}$ the hat-matrix diagonal, under its Assumption 1. The condition depends on the design matrix only and is checkable from it (the paper points to the `hrt` R package). Gaussian regression, no clusters, no finite population.

### S5.4 (B14) Certified critical values below the frontier

2. **Existing method.** The Chebyshev and Markov moment-duality bound on the probability that a quadratic in a self-normalised sum is positive (a quadratic majorant), applied with the exact moments of sampling without replacement, and the cone geometry of the $t$ statistic, $t^2 = (n - 1)u/(n - u)$ with $u = (\sum y)^2/\sum y^2$.
3. **Sources.**

For $X$ with mean 0 and variance 1 and $0 < b \le a$, $P(X \notin (-a, b)) \le 1 \wedge \{4 + (a - b)^2\}/(a + b)^2$, motivated by bounding the probability that $X^2 + AX + B \ge 0$ in self-normalised sums. No journal reference on the page.
   - Jonsson (2011), "On the heavy-tailedness of Student's t-statistic", Bernoulli 17(1), 276 to 289, DOI 10.3150/10-BEJ262, arXiv:1102.2072. Full text (summary), Lemmas 3.1 and 3.3. Lemma 3.1 gives $T_n^2 > x$ iff $U_n > nx/(n + x - 1)$, which inverts to the identity above; Lemma 3.3 bounds $|x_i - x_1|$ when $n - u_n < h^2$.
   - The nine sources of the NOV12 list (S5.3, item 7). Not opened (paywalled; the record has UNC proxy links).
4. **Where the record found them.** NOV12 (`results/round9/fewcluster/novelty/novelty_NOV12_CERT_candidates.csv`; `docs/round09/i03/fewcluster/round09_fewcluster_nov12.md`). Pinelis read in full and Jonsson in sections by that check.

## 3. Question V. Conditions that bound one cluster's share of the design variance

The question, from round 9's Theorem 3 and its box class, is whether it is defensible to assume that no stratum (cluster, in regime B) carries more than $v_0$ of the design variance $V = \sum_h W_h^2(1 - n_h/N_h)S_h^2/n_h$, with $v_h = V_h/V \le v_0$ (`docs/round09/i03/fewcluster/round09_fewcluster_stratified.md`, lines 29 and 101 to 115). Nicolas decided V is settled by the literature and by testing (L0 scope, section 2). This section collects what the literature says, by the form the condition takes, and does not decide it.

### 3.1 Asymptotic negligibility conditions (stated, not tested)

1. **Hájek (1960), via Li and Ding (2017).** For simple random sampling without replacement the normal limit holds under $(N - 1)\kappa(y)/\min(n, N - n) \to 0$ (S5.1). This is a bound on the max-share of a single unit, asymptotic, and for one stage. Read level is full text (summary) of Li and Ding, sections 2.1 and A.1. Hájek not opened.
2. **Bickel and Freedman (1984), "Asymptotic normality and the bootstrap in stratified sampling", Annals of Statistics 12(2), 470 to 482 `[verify]` pages, DOI 10.1214/aos/1176346500.** Full text (summary) of Berkeley Technical Report 29 (January 1984), sections 1 to 4, Theorems 1 to 5. For sampling without replacement within strata (their case (b), conditions (5) and (6)), a Lindeberg condition stated with variance weights $v_i$ that sum to one; the summary reports that normality holds if for each stratum the variance-weight contribution is small or the stratum is heavily sampled, which corresponds to $\max_h v_h \to 0$ unless the stratum is nearly a census. This is the asymptotic form of the box class's $v_h \le v_0$, and the closest survey-sampling statement of it found. The extracted formulas were garbled, so the exact condition needs the published text.
3. **Krewski and Rao (1981), "Inference from stratified samples: properties of the linearization, jackknife and balanced repeated replication methods", Annals of Statistics 9(5), 1010 to 1019 `[verify]` pages, DOI 10.1214/aos/1176345580.** Not opened (Project Euclid bot wall after the DOI redirect). Its conditions are quoted, in the Yung and Rao form C1 to C3 (a Liapunov moment condition, a bound on the largest weighted second-stage contribution, and convergence of $n\,\text{Var}$), in Rubin-Bleuer and Schiopu Kratina (2005), "On the two-phase framework for joint model and design-based inference", Annals of Statistics 33(6), 2789 to 2810, arXiv:math/0603078, full text (summary), Example 6.1 and appendix. That paper states no explicit condition that no primary unit contributes a non-negligible share of the variance.
4. **Ohlsson (1989), "Asymptotic normality for two-stage sampling from a finite population", Probability Theory and Related Fields (Zeitschrift für Wahrscheinlichkeitstheorie) 81, DOI 10.1007/BF00340058.** Metadata and a review only. General conditions for two-stage asymptotic normality through a martingale central limit theorem. The conditions themselves were not seen.
5. **Bloznelis (1999).** Berry-Esseen for the finite-population Student statistic. Not opened. A finite-sample bound, but with an unspecified constant (round 9's NOV11 and NOV12 rows), so it does not give a usable $v_0$.
6. **Econometrics, cluster-robust inference with many clusters.**
   - Djogbenou, MacKinnon and Nielsen (2019), "Asymptotic theory and wild bootstrap inference with clustered errors", Journal of Econometrics 212(2), 393 to 412. Full text (summary) of CREATES Research Paper 2019-05, Assumption 3, $\mu_N^{(2+\lambda)/(2+2\lambda)}\sup_g N_g/N \to 0$, which "rules out the possibility that one cluster is proportional to the entire sample"; simpler sufficient forms (11) to (13).
   - Hansen and Lee (2019), "Asymptotic theory for clustered samples", Journal of Econometrics, DOI 10.1016/j.jeconom.2019.02.001, arXiv:1902.01497. Abstract only (allows heterogeneous and unbounded cluster sizes; the exact condition not seen).
   - Carter, Schnepel and Steigerwald (2017), "Asymptotic behavior of a t-test robust to cluster heterogeneity", Review of Economics and Statistics 99(4), 698 to 709. Full text (summary) of the authors' February 2016 version, sections 2 and 3, Assumption 2. Requires $G \to \infty$ and $E[\gamma]/G \to 0$, which holds if the largest cluster is a vanishing share of the sample; cites Rogers' conjecture that a normal approximation is adequate when $\max n_g/n < 0.05$.
   - Chiang, Sasaki and Wang, "Genuinely robust inference for clustered data", arXiv:2308.10138 (v8). Full text (summary), section 1 and Theorem 1 (section 3.2). The cluster-robust $t$ is asymptotically normal if and only if the largest cluster's score is ignorable, formalised as a tail index $\alpha = 2$ of the cluster score; $\max_g N_g^2/N \to 0$ is sufficient, not necessary.

### 3.2 Finite-sample conditions on the design (stated and checkable)

1. **Pötscher and Preinerstorfer, arXiv:2412.17470 (S5.3).** A necessary and sufficient condition for a finite size-controlling critical value, $h_{ii} < 1$ on the relevant indices, checkable from the design matrix alone. Gaussian regression; it controls whether any finite critical value exists, the same yes-or-no question as Theorem 1, but in a leverage form and without a finite population.
2. **Bell and McCaffrey (2002), "Bias reduction in standard errors for linear regression with multi-stage samples", Survey Methodology 28(2), 169 to 181.** Full text (summary). The primary-unit leverage block $H_{ii} = X_i(X^\top X)^{-1}X_i^\top$ is used to inflate residuals, $A_i = (I - H_{ii})^{-1/2}$; bias is worst when a coefficient depends mainly on a few primary units; the adjustment fails when an eigenvalue of $H_{ii}$ equals 1; a Satterthwaite degrees of freedom $f = 2/\text{cv}^2$ computed from $X$ alone is recommended over $n - 1$.
3. **Hagemann, "Permutation inference with a finite number of heterogeneous clusters", arXiv:1907.01049 (v2, February 2023; journal `[verify]`).** Full text (summary), section 2, Theorem 2.1, eqs. (2.3) to (2.6), Table 2, appendix B. No bound on heterogeneity is assumed. The level is adjusted to $\bar\alpha$, the largest level whose worst-case rejection probability over all variance configurations of approximately normal cluster estimates is at most $\alpha$ (for example $\bar\alpha = 0.0428$ at $\alpha = 0.10$ with four treated and four control clusters). Appendix B allows restricted variance sets, which would raise $\bar\alpha$; the paper recommends no procedure for choosing or reporting a bound.
4. **Ibragimov and Müller (2016), "Inference with few heterogeneous clusters", Review of Economics and Statistics 98(1), 83 to 96 (DOI 10.1162/REST_a_00545 `[verify]`).** Abstract only. Its predecessor (Ibragimov and Müller 2010) builds on Bakirov and Székely's (2006) result that the $t$ test is conservative at levels below about 0.083 under independent normal estimates with arbitrary variances (from round 9's NOV11 row, not read here). That route assumes normal cluster estimates instead of bounding a share.

### 3.3 How a share condition is tested or reported in practice

1. **Effective number of clusters.** $G^\ast = G/(1 + \gamma)$ with $\gamma$ the squared coefficient of variation of cluster variance components, and a feasible $G^\ast_A$ computed with a working covariance of ones; size distortion rises sharply below about 10 (Carter, Schnepel and Steigerwald, section 3). There is an R package `effClust` (CRAN reference manual in the search results, not opened).
2. **Cluster leverage, partial leverage and influence.** MacKinnon, Nielsen and Webb (2023), "Leverage, influence, and the jackknife in clustered regression models: reliable inference using summclust", Stata Journal 23(4), 942 to 982, DOI 10.1177/1536867X231212433, arXiv:2205.03288 (v3). Abstract only. Cluster-level measures that identify designs in which cluster-robust inference is likely to be unreliable.
3. **A formal test.** Chiang, Sasaki and Wang test $H_0\colon \alpha = 2$ for the cluster score's tail index with the likelihood ratio test of Sasaki and Wang (Stata `testout`), and reject it for 24 of 31 AER and Econometrica articles (their Table 3, per the summary).
4. **Survey sampling, unit influence on the design variance.** Beaumont, Haziza and Ruiz-Gazen (2013), "A unified approach to robust estimation in finite population sampling", Biometrika 100(3), 555 to 569. Full text (summary) of the author manuscript, sections 1 to 3 and the appendix. A unit's conditional bias $B_i^1 = E_P(\hat\theta_{HT} \mid I_i = 1) - \theta$ is its contribution to the sampling error, with $\text{var}_P(\hat\theta_{HT}) = \sum_i B_i^1 y_i$ (approximately for stratified simple random and high-entropy designs, their eqs. 14 and 15); influential sample units are detected by large estimated conditional biases (eqs. 21 to 23); units with $\pi_i = 1$ have zero conditional bias. This is the survey-sampling statement of a unit's share of the design variance and the sample-based way to detect a dominant unit. It is a robust-estimation device, not a validity condition.
5. **The design response, a take-all stratum.** Hidiroglou and Lavallée (1988) and the Insee guide (S5.11). The survey practice when a few units would dominate the variance is to take them with certainty rather than to assume them away. The Insee guide gives no numeric share threshold. No source found states a numeric variance-share rule for certainty primary units (log row 53).
6. **Survey influence diagnostics for regression.** Li and Valliant (2011), "Linear regression influence diagnostics for unclustered survey data", Journal of Official Statistics 27(1), 99 to 119. Full text (summary). Survey-weighted leverages, DFBETAS, DFFITS and Cook's distance for unclustered designs; clustered versions are named as future work.

### 3.4 What the search did not find

No source in survey sampling, econometrics or the machine-learning side states a finite-sample bound on one stratum's or cluster's share of the design variance as the assumption under which a few-cluster or stratified interval is valid, or tells a user how to choose $v_0$. Every condition found is asymptotic (Hájek; Bickel and Freedman; Krewski and Rao; Djogbenou et al.; Carter et al.; Chiang et al.), a leverage condition in a Gaussian model (Pötscher and Preinerstorfer), a worst case over all heterogeneity under normality (Hagemann; Ibragimov and Müller), or a diagnostic without a validity guarantee ($G^\ast$, summclust, conditional bias). The queries that failed are log rows 53 and 54 and, for the asymptotic literature, the queries of rows 16 to 31. This agrees with round 9's verdict that the stratified frontier and its box form were not found (`round09_fewcluster_stratified.md`, line 157). It is recorded as a finding of the search, not as a gap claim; whether V is defensible is Nicolas's decision with David, informed by the final runs, which carry regime B's numbers with and without the bound (L0 scope, section 2).
