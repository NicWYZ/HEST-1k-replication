## 1. The four sources

### 1.1 Zhang, Sanguiao-Sande and Lee (2025)

"Design-Based Predictive Inference", Journal of Official Statistics 41(1), 404 to 432, DOI 10.1177/0282423X241277719 (printed on the article). Read in full, with formulas checked on printed pp. 406 to 407, 410 to 419, 427 and 429 to 430.

1. **The Monte Carlo estimator and its divisor.** Section 2.3.2 (p. 415) averages the subsample-trained prediction estimator over $T$ splits and gives, as eq. (9), an MSE estimator whose dispersion term is $\frac{1}{T}\sum_t\{\hat Y_1^{*(t)} - \tilde Y^{RB}\}^2$. The divisor is $1/T$, the form of Mickey's (4.4). It targets the MSE of the Monte Carlo average actually computed. This confirms L2 part 2, section 2.1, item 1, from the primary source.
2. **What it is an estimator of.** It is an MSE estimator for prediction estimators, which are biased in general (eq. 6, p. 412). The theorems cover prediction estimators of the form (1), not Mickey's $t_\alpha$, whose correction uses the test part's labels. Section 2.2 (p. 413) gives the conditional variance of the "hypothetical" estimator on $s_1$, which is the ingredient of the project's $s_A^2$, but not the combination over two halves.
3. **How the splits are drawn.** The paper does not say the $T$ splits are drawn independently. Only its printed closed form $\text{MSE}(\tilde Y^{RB}) = \text{MSE}(\hat Y^{RB}) + E_p V_q/T$ (p. 415) needs that. It names $T$-fold cross-validation, a random partition of $s$ with each part taken in turn as the test set, as an admissible sub-design for Theorems 1 and 2 (p. 410 and p. 411), but writes no estimator for the average over the folds of one partition.
4. **Exactness and designs.** Theorems 1 and 2 are exact at any sampling fraction for any design with known conditional inclusion probabilities (p. 411 to 413). For SRSWOR of the sample and of the training part, $\pi_{2i} = n_2/(N - n_1)$ exactly (p. 414 to 415). A multistage design must subsample whole primary units (p. 411). Strata are subsampled within, with at least 2 test units per stratum (p. 426).
5. **Negative values.** Not discussed. The Monte Carlo coefficient of variation of the MSE estimator is 3.48 at 2 test units in its illustration, against 0.31 at 20 (Table 1, p. 417).
6. **Citations.** It cites Mickey (1959) and Hartley and Ross (1954) (p. 406) as design-unbiased ratio and regression estimators, and Sanguiao-Sande and Zhang as Sankhya A 83, 714 to 744. It does not cite Williams (1961), Cassel, Särndal and Wretman (1976) or any PPI paper.
7. **For the project.** With a predictor fixed in advance its design-unbiased estimator reduces to a stratified difference estimator (p. 428). The reader's own derivation, that (9) stays unbiased for splits that are not independent as long as each has the right marginal law, is checked and generalised in section 2.

### 1.2 Breidt and Opsomer (2017)

"Model-Assisted Survey Estimation with Modern Prediction Techniques", Statistical Science 32(2), 190 to 205, DOI 10.1214/16-STS589 (printed). Read in full, with formulas checked on pp. 191 to 196, 198 and 202.

1. **The difference estimator.** Eq. (4) (p. 192) with a method $m(\cdot)$ that does not depend on the sample is exactly design-unbiased whatever the quality of the method, with exact variance (5) and an unbiased variance estimator under a measurable design (p. 192, unnumbered). The paper does not mention PPI, which postdates it. That PPI with an externally trained predictor and a fixed coefficient is this estimator is Mozer's identity (arXiv:2603.19160) and the reader's reading, and Breidt and Opsomer are cited for the estimator's exactness.
2. **With a fitted model.** The model-assisted estimator (7) and its plug-in variance (8) (p. 194) are only asymptotically unbiased and consistent. Eq. (9) splits (7) into the exactly unbiased but infeasible $\text{DIFF}(y, m_N)$ and a remainder. The asymptotic conditions D1 to D4 (p. 193) let the sampling fraction tend to a constant in $(0, 1)$ but need the sample size to grow, and normal intervals are said to be right in moderate to large samples (p. 193).
3. **Not in the paper.** Sample splitting, cross-fitting and leave-one-out are not discussed, and no exactly unbiased estimator with a fitted model is given. Model calibration (Wu and Sitter 2001, section 8.1, p. 198) is the nearest object to PPI with a fitted coefficient. Cluster and two-stage designs and few strata are not treated. It cites Cassel, Särndal and Wretman (1976) (p. 195) and not Mickey, Williams or Hartley and Ross.

### 1.3 Bloznelis (1999)

"A Berry-Esseen bound for finite population Student's statistic", Annals of Probability 27(4), 2089 to 2108 (no DOI printed; L1 S5 gives 10.1214/aop/1022874830). Read in full, with pp. 2089 to 2098 and 2105 to 2108 checked as images and pp. 2099 to 2104 read as text. The paper writes $N$ for the sample and $n$ for the population.

1. **The bounds.** Theorem 1.1 (eq. 1.1, p. 2089) bounds the Kolmogorov distance between the finite-population Studentized mean and the normal by $\frac{c}{\sqrt q}\frac{\beta_3}{\sqrt N\sigma^3}$, with $q = 1 - N/n$. Theorem 1.2 (eq. 1.2, p. 2090) is a truncated version at level $\sigma\sqrt{npq}$. In every theorem $c$ is an absolute constant whose existence is proved and whose value is never given, so neither bound can be evaluated. This confirms round 9's record.
2. **Conditions.** Only $N < n$ and a population with positive variance. No condition on the largest unit. The Erdős-Rényi condition (1.3), a Lindeberg-type condition, is assumed only for Corollary 1.3, the asymptotic normality of the Studentized mean.
3. **The reader's reading in shares** (not stated in the paper). With $v_i$ unit $i$'s share of the centred sum of squares, the right side of (1.1) equals $\frac{c}{\sqrt{pq}}\sum_i v_i^{3/2} \le c\sqrt{v_{\max}/(pq)}$, and its smallest possible value is $c/\sqrt{Nq}$, which is $0.30c$ to $0.65c$ for the population and sample sizes of the project. So the natural scale for a small share is $pq$, and even at its best the bound is far above the 0.05 a 90% interval needs unless $c$ is much below 1.
4. **Not covered.** Regime B (stratified sampling) and any Edgeworth or skewness term. It does not cite Bickel and Freedman (1984). It points to Höglund (1978) and Rao and Zhao (1994) as earlier bounds; whether either has an explicit constant was not checked.

### 1.4 Mashreghi, Haziza and Léger (2016)

"A survey of bootstrap methods in finite population sampling", Statistics Surveys 10, 1 to 52, DOI 10.1214/16-SS113 (printed). Read in full, sections 5 and 6 quickly, with formulas checked on pp. 10, 13 to 15, 17 to 20, 25 to 27, 29 to 31 and 34. It is a review with no simulation of its own and reports no coverage numbers.

1. **What reproduces the finite-population factor.** For SRSWOR, Sitter's (1992a) pseudo-population bootstrap, Rao and Wu's (1988) rescaling bootstrap, Sitter's (1992b) mirror-match bootstrap and the bootstrap-weight methods of Rao, Wu and Yue (1992) and the generalised bootstrap reproduce $N^2(1 - f)s^2/n$ exactly (Tables 2, 4 and 5, pp. 18, 27 and 30). Stratified designs are resampled within each stratum (pp. 19 and 27). For multistage designs with a large first-stage fraction the with-replacement approximation is not adequate (p. 34), and Funaoka et al. (2006) is the method named.
2. **The naive with-replacement bootstrap.** Its variance is $\frac{n - 1}{n(1 - f)}$ times the unbiased one (eq. 3.1, p. 10; Table 4, p. 27). The ratio is above 1 when $f > 1/n$ and below 1 when $f < 1/n$. At 4 labelled clusters it is below 1 on every task with more than 16 clusters (about 0.90 on CCRCC, 0.89 on INDIANA_KIDNEY and 0.81 on ACS by state, by this arithmetic), so there a design-based bootstrap would widen the interval. At 8 or more labelled clusters it is above 1 on the four tasks, and a design-based bootstrap would narrow it. Round 4's design-target rows (`results/round4/ppi/Q1_estimator/q1_report_numbers.csv`, rows `S|design|textbook|boot_pct|fpc0|...`) show the percentile bootstrap covering 0.7655 at 4 labelled clusters and 0.94425 at 12 for the classical estimator. That pattern fits a variance too small at small $n$ and too large at large $n$, together with the percentile interval's small-sample narrowness (Hesterberg 2015, row S6-2). This is the oversight chat's reading, `[proposed]`, and it holds for linear estimators.
3. **Intervals.** Normal, bootstrap-$t$ and percentile intervals are discussed, with no BCa (pp. 19 to 21, 27 to 28 and 33). The tuning constant of a direct method changes the interval but not the variance (pp. 27 to 28), and methods that match two moments may not support percentile or bootstrap-$t$ intervals (p. 33). Nothing is said about few primary units.
4. **Citations.** Rao and Wu (1988) throughout. Not Mickey, Williams, Hartley and Ross, Cassel et al., or PPI.

## 2. The variance of an average of split-sample estimators, checked again

**The identity.** Let $t_1, \dots, t_K$ be estimators with the same mean $\mu$, and $s_1^2, \dots, s_K^2$ estimators with $E(s_k^2) = \text{Var}(t_k)$. Write $\bar t = \frac{1}{K}\sum_k t_k$. Then, skipping the expansion of the square,
$$
E\Big[\frac{1}{K}\sum_k (t_k - \bar t)^2\Big] = \frac{1}{K}\sum_k \text{Var}(t_k) - \text{Var}(\bar t),
$$
so that
$$
\hat V(\bar t) = \frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^K s_k^2 - \frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^K (t_k - \bar t)^2
$$
is exactly unbiased for $\text{Var}(\bar t)$. Nothing is assumed about the joint law of the $t_k$, so the pieces may come from complementary halves, from unequal halves, or from the $K$ folds of one partition. Each $t_k$ here is Mickey's $t_\alpha$ with the coefficient fitted on the labelled units outside fold $k$, which is exactly unbiased by Mickey's (2.6), and $s_k^2$ is the classical SRSWOR variance of the difference estimator on fold $k$'s residuals, unbiased for $\text{Var}(t_k)$ when fold $k$ has at least 2 units. With $K = 2$ and equal halves this is the `[proposed]` estimator of part 1, $\tfrac{1}{2}(s_A^2 + s_B^2) - \tfrac{1}{4}(t_A - t_B)^2$, which therefore rests on this identity and not on Mickey's equal-distribution argument (4.5) and (4.6). The same form is Mickey's (4.4) and Zhang, Sanguiao-Sande and Lee's (9). Neither states it for the folds of one partition. The identity is elementary and is not claimed as a result. It is the oversight chat's reading, `[proposed]`, until Nicolas has seen it.

**The check.** `checks/l3_split_variance_check.py` enumerates every sample and every assignment to folds on three toy populations (the two of part 1 and one with a single outlying unit), with the unclipped slope and round 5's rule, at $n$ of 6 to 9, two folds (equal and unequal) and three folds. It ran in 6 minutes in the cloud workspace, the same kind of exact enumeration on toy populations as part 1's check, with no data and no cluster. The ledger brief says the track has no compute, so this is recorded as a departure for Nicolas to confirm at L3. The output is `checks/l3_split_variance_check_output.txt`. Page and table numbers quoted from the four papers in section 1 are outside the repository and are listed in `.verify-exceptions-round10-ledger`.

1. **Unbiasedness.** The average $\bar t$ is unbiased and $E(\hat V)/\text{Var}(\bar t) = 1.0$ in all 30 settings, odd $n$ and three folds included.
2. **Negative values.** $\hat V$ is negative in a fraction from 0.0 to 0.217641 of the enumerated cases, the largest with one outlying unit, the unclipped slope and $n = 6$.
3. **The conservative estimator.** $\frac{1}{K}\sum_k s_k^2$ is nonnegative and, by Cauchy-Schwarz, never below $\text{Var}(\bar t)$ in expectation. Its expectation is 1.101126 to 1.883294 times $\text{Var}(\bar t)$.
4. **Round 9's within-half plug-in.** $(1 - n/N)s_w^2/n$, with the residuals pooled within folds, has expectation 0.990693 to 1.329553 times $\text{Var}(\bar t)$, and 0.994496 to 1.038315 under round 5's clipped rule. It is not exact, but on these populations it is within 0.01 of unbiased or above.

**Effect.** The variance of the two-fold form needs no source beyond Mickey (1959) for each piece, the classical SRSWOR variance for each $s_k^2$, and the identity. Odd $n$ is covered by unequal folds, which removes the gap round 8 noted for Williams' equal groups. The decision on which variance the final runs report is in the decisions document.
