CHEATSHEET: predicting 50-gene expression (log1p counts) from 224x224 H&E patches (0.5 um/px, ~112 um)
Source: one human skin melanoma section (10x Xenium aggregated to spots), 814 training spots.
The test patches come from the same section (other regions), so stain and scanner are the same.

=== 1. THE BIG PICTURE ===
Three gene programs explain about 80% of the variance. Work out which tissue the patch shows, then use its profile.
 K = keratinocyte/epidermis program: KRT5 KRT16 KRT17 DSP SBSN S100A14 SERPINB5 MAL2 AQP3 COL17A1
     (also DMKN DSC1 KRTDAP SLPI TP63 MIR205HG LY6D RHOV FGFBP1 CAPNS2 AHNAK2 CRABP2 DEFB1 SERPINB2 LAMB3)
 M = melanoma tumour + stroma program: VIM POSTN MGP TYRP1 HAPLN1 (in this tissue, melanoma cells themselves
     express all five very highly: VIM~6.3, POSTN~6.4, MGP~5.9, TYRP1~5.0, HAPLN1~3.8)
 I = immune/vascular program: LYZ CD52 ITM2A WDFY4 LCK GZMK BIRC3 IDO1 (+CCL19 CCL22 CD1B MMRN1 VWF SELE)
Score definition (training): K = mean of the 10 K genes, M = mean of the 5 M genes, I = mean of the 8 I genes.
Training distribution: K median 1.2 (range 0-7.3; >3 means epidermis), M median 5.1 (0-6.3), I median 1.2 (0-4).
Any gene ~= b0 + bK*K + bM*M + bI*I, clipped at 0 (coefficients in the table in section 4).

Training class frequencies (expect roughly similar in test):
 TUM (melanoma nests)                      51%
 TUMimm (tumour + stroma/immune/vessels)   13%
 EPIviab (viable epidermis, K>3, M>=3)      8%
 EPIcorn (upper epidermis/cornified edge)   6%
 ACELL (acellular pink: keratin plug, hyalinised collagen; few nuclei)   13%
 EMPTY (mostly glass, white fraction >0.75)  9%

=== 2. VISUAL GUIDE (what each class looks like) ===
TUM: sheets/nests of large epithelioid melanoma cells. Abundant pale pink-lilac cytoplasm, round-oval vesicular
  nuclei with visible nucleoli, fairly evenly spaced (about 40-60 nuclei per patch). Thin fibrovascular septa
  between the nests. Lavender-purple overall. This is the default for any cellular purple patch.
  Typical scores: K~1.2 M~5.6 I~1.3.
TUMimm: tumour plus more stroma: pink fibrous septa, spindle cells, scattered small dark round lymphocytes,
  vessels or red cells, looser or oedematous areas. Nests may be smaller. LYZ, CD52, ITM2A, WDFY4, GZMK, LCK
  and BIRC3 go up (LYZ ~5.2) and HAPLN1 goes down a little. Scores: K~1.2 M~5.0 I~2.4.
  NOTE: immune content is hard to see. Most tumour patches have I~1.0-1.6. If you see clear lymphocyte
  aggregates, use I~2.5-3.5. Otherwise use I~1.3-1.6, or 1.8 if stroma is prominent.
EPIviab: stratified squamous epithelium. Keratinocytes are polygonal with dense, more eosinophilic (pinker,
  more saturated) cytoplasm and intercellular bridges, and they lie in layers. The basal layer has crowded
  darker, smaller, elongated nuclei along a basement membrane. It often borders a surface or keratin layer.
  The patch is pinker and more saturated than tumour. Mixed epidermis and tumour is common (melanoma
  invades the epidermis), so M stays moderate (~3.9). Scores: K~5.8 M~3.9 I~1.5.
  Basal-heavy patches: raise KRT5, COL17A1, TP63, MIR205HG, LAMB3. Suprabasal/granular: raise KRTDAP, SBSN,
  SLPI, DEFB1, SERPINB2, DSC1, KRT2, DMKN.
EPIcorn: the surface of the epidermis. Flattened keratinocytes with thin elongated nuclei parallel to the
  surface, compact pink lamellar keratin and parakeratosis, usually beside white background. Strong
  suprabasal/keratin genes (KRT16 6.9, KRT17 6.5, SBSN 6.1, SLPI 5.5, DMKN 5.5, KRTDAP 4.9) but low
  melanoma genes (M~1.9). Scores: K~5.5 M~1.9 I~0.35.
ACELL: homogeneous glassy bright-pink material (thick keratin plugs or hyalinised collagen), loose keratin
  flakes or ribbons with almost no viable nuclei, and dermal collagen bundles with a few spindle nuclei.
  Nearly everything is ~0-1.4. KRT16/KRT17 ~2 (keratin debris), DSP 1.4, MGP/VIM/POSTN ~1.3.
  Caution: pink lamellar keratin with flattened nuclei that is directly attached to epithelium is EPIcorn
  (high K). Detached acellular keratin or glassy slabs are ACELL (low).
EMPTY: mostly white glass. All genes ~0-1.5 (KRT16/KRT17 ~1.5, DSP 1.2, VIM/POSTN/MGP ~1.0).

Partial tissue: if a patch is a fraction w white (background) and the rest is class X, predict roughly
  (1 - 0.5*w)*X + 0.5*w*EMPTY. Log counts fall off sub-linearly: a tumour patch that is 45% white still has
  M~4.7 against 5.7 for a full patch.
Mixtures (e.g. epidermis next to a tumour nest): average the class profiles in proportion to area. If you are
  unsure between two classes, predicting the weighted average minimises squared error.

=== 3. TIPS ===
- Purple, cellular, large round nuclei with nucleoli = melanoma, so VIM/POSTN/MGP ~6, TYRP1 ~5, HAPLN1 ~3.5-4.
  These five genes are the most predictable (r~0.9 from images). Get them right first.
- The hardest call is epidermis vs tumour. Look for layering, a surface, pinker cytoplasm with bridges, and
  basal palisading. Few patches (~14%) are epidermis; do not over-call it.
- Vessels with red cells inside tumour: VWF ~3.3 (against 2.0). Other vascular genes barely change.
- Clusters of small dark round nuclei (lymphocytes or inflammatory cells): use TUMimm (LYZ, CD52, ITM2A,
  WDFY4 and GZMK up).
- Low-variance genes (HBD, TFF3, SELE, MYH11, CCL19, CCL22, CD1B, IL1B, MMRN1) are mostly 0-1.4. Use the
  class mean; they are weakly predictable.
- Spatial structure seen in training: lymphocyte-rich tumour (high I) is in the centre of the tumour mass.
  Keratin/acellular regions lie along the skin surface, with the epidermis between them and the tumour.

=== 5. OPTIONAL PYTHON MODEL (spatial-CV mean per-gene Pearson ~0.50 with no visual judgement) ===
It computes 9 image features, takes inverse-distance-weighted K,M,I from the 5 nearest of 80 prototypes, and
applies the table coefficients. Visual class judgement (section 2) can do better on epidermis; you can blend
both, e.g. average the K,M,I from this model with your visual estimate.
Feature definitions (numpy, skimage, scipy). a = RGB float array in [0,1], shape 224x224x3:
  g=a.mean(2); hsv=rgb2hsv(a); hed=rgb2hed(a)
  white=(g>0.82)&(hsv[...,1]<0.18); t=~white; m = t if t.sum()>50 else all pixels
  white_frac=white.mean()
  grad=mean(hypot(ndi.sobel(g,0),ndi.sobel(g,1)))
  nuc=(g<0.55)&(a[...,2]>=a[...,0]-0.02); lab,n=ndi.label(ndi.binary_opening(nuc,iterations=1));
     nn = number of components with >=20 pixels
  Em=mean(hed[...,1][m]); Hm=mean(hed[...,0][m]); Sm=mean(hsv[...,1][m]); S90=90th percentile of hsv[...,1][m]
  lbp9 = fraction of pixels with value 9 in local_binary_pattern((g*255).astype(uint8),8,1,'uniform')
  pink=((a[...,0]>0.8)&(a[...,1]<0.6)&(a[...,2]>0.65)&(g>0.6)).mean()
Feature order: white grad nn Em Hm Sm S90 lbp9 pink
Standardise z=(f-MU)/SD, take squared Euclidean distance D to each prototype centre, keep the 5 nearest,
weights w=1/(D+0.5) normalised, and [K,M,I] = sum of w*proto_scores.
Then gene = max(0, b0+bK*K+bM*M+bI*I).
