CHEATSHEET: predicting 50 genes from H&E patches of clear cell renal cell carcinoma (ccRCC)
==============================

What the data are
-----------------
- Each patch is 224 x 224 px at 0.5 um/px (112 um across). It is centred on one Visium spot 55 um
  across, so the central ~110 px circle is what was measured; the rim is context.
- Values are log(1 + count) per spot. Counts are low: for most genes the median spot has 0 or 1 count
  (log values 0, 0.69, 1.10, 1.39, 1.61, 1.79, 1.95, 2.08 = 0..7 counts). Many spots are 0 for a gene
  even in tissue where the gene is "on". Per-spot noise is large, so a good prediction is a sensible
  expected level for the tissue type, not an extreme value.
- Training material: resected ccRCC from 7 patients, 500 spots each. They differed a lot in tumour
  morphology, and most of the variation in these genes was between tumour phenotypes, not
  between spots inside one tumour. Deciding which phenotype a patch belongs to matters most.
  Within a phenotype the next most useful judgement is how cellular the spot is (tumour cells vs
  fibrous stroma vs blood).

Gene groups that rise and fall together (A4)
------------------------------
1. Overall "cellularity / transcript richness" axis. Inside every section the strongest shared
   signal is how much living, cell-dense tissue the spot holds. Genes that track it most: LY6E, IFI27,
   LRRC41, CPE, HTRA1, CIB1, FLNB, PXN, SEMA4B, SLC39A14, VMP1, SLC15A4, HSPA1A, CD109, DERL1,
   CHST15, NAMPT, IFI6, TMEM91, CKB. Weaker but same direction: all the others. Thus a patch of dense
   tumour cells should get higher values for nearly every gene than a patch of paucicellular collagen,
   blood lake, oedema, empty lumen or tissue edge from the same tumour.
2. Interferon response: IFI27, IFI6, ISG15, IRF7, PARP10, LY6E (and partly TRBC1). Highest in tumours
   that are heavily inflamed (many lymphocytes, neutrophils and macrophages mixed between the tumour
   cells), and moderate in high-grade tumours. IFI27 and LY6E are also high in ordinary clear-cell
   tumour epithelium and in endothelium, so they are not purely inflammatory.
3. Myeloid / inflammatory: CD14, S100A9 (neutrophils, monocytes), GPNMB (macrophages, foamy
   histiocytes), HMOX1 (macrophages handling haem, haemorrhage with haemosiderin), plus
   CHST15. S100A9 is near zero in most tissue and rises sharply only where neutrophils or
   inflammatory infiltrate are abundant.
4. T cells: TRBC1 (T-cell receptor beta). Highest in lymphocyte-rich tissue: lymphoid aggregates,
   dense lymphocytic infiltrates in stroma or tumour.
5. Classic clear-cell programme (VHL/HIF-driven tumour epithelium): SLC39A14, LRRC41, TMEM91, NOL3,
   SCARB1 (lipid uptake, fits the lipid-laden clear cytoplasm), SLC15A4, PTGS1, AKAP8, IFI27, LY6E,
   EGFL7 and NOTCH3 (the delicate capillary network that wraps every nest; NOTCH3 also pericytes and
   vessel walls). These are clearly higher in clear-cell nests than in stroma.
6. High-grade / dedifferentiated epithelioid programme: CPE, CD109, CKB, SEMA4B, CIB1, FLNB, PXN,
   CHST15, KRTCAP2, DERL1, VMP1. In a tumour with large pleomorphic nuclei, prominent nucleoli and
   abundant amphophilic or glassy cytoplasm these were 2-6 times the level seen elsewhere (CPE most
   of all, often 3-5 in log1p units, i.e. tens of counts).
7. Sarcomatoid / mesenchymal programme: HTRA1 (very strong), NOTCH3, LAPTM4B, YWHAE, BTF3,
   NT5DC2, TFRC and LY6E moderate. HTRA1 is the single most distinctive gene of spindle-cell tumour.
8. Iron uptake / growth: TFRC, NT5DC2, LAPTM4B were high together in an eosinophilic, inflamed,
   high-grade tumour and moderately high in sarcomatoid tumour; low in low-grade clear-cell tumour.
9. Stress genes HSPA1A, GADD45B, NAMPT, TP53INP1 vary with tissue state; HSPA1A is fairly flat
   (0.5-1.1) everywhere and is one of the few genes still near 1 in low-yield clear-cell tumour.
   TP53INP1 and HSPA1A were highest in necrotic/degenerating tumour.

Tissue phenotypes seen in training and their gene levels (A1, A2, A5)
------------------------------
Decide which description the tumour in the patch resembles most. Seven phenotypes were seen;
new tumours will often look like one of them or a blend.

CCa - Classic low-grade clear-cell carcinoma, compact nests. Nests and alveoli of polygonal cells
  with water-clear or finely vacuolated cytoplasm, sharp cell membranes, small round nuclei
  (low grade, nucleoli inconspicuous at this magnification), a thin "chicken-wire" capillary network
  between nests, occasional small slit-like lumens with red cells. Some cells have
  granular pale-pink cytoplasm. Fibrous bands/capsule of dense pink collagen separated
  nodules. This phenotype had the highest SLC39A14 (mean 2.5 in tumour nests, often 3-4), LRRC41
  (~1.8), IFI27 (~1.9), and raised NOL3, SCARB1, PTGS1, PXN, NOTCH3, TMEM91, SLC15A4, AKAP8, HTRA1
  (~1.2). Low S100A9, CD14, GPNMB, NT5DC2, TFRC, CKB.

CCb - Clear-cell carcinoma with lymphocytic infiltrate. Sheets and nests of clear to pale-grey cells
  with small to medium round nuclei, many scattered small dark lymphocytes between tumour cells and
  lymphoid aggregates near fibrous septa; papillary-like fibrovascular cores with oedema and some
  thick-walled vessels in the periphery. Moderate values:
  LY6E ~1.4, LRRC41 ~1.2, IFI27 ~1.1, TMEM91 ~0.9, DERL1 ~0.8, CIB1 ~0.8, HSPA1A ~0.8,
  SLC39A14 only ~0.6. Low NOTCH3, GADD45B, S100A9, interferon genes besides IFI27/LY6E. TRBC1 ~0.5
  in tumour, ~0.8 in lymphocyte-dense patches.

CCc - Clear-cell carcinoma with haemorrhage and hyalinised stroma, low overall yield. Classic clear
  cells (large clear cytoplasm, round dark nuclei, some with small nucleoli), but the tissue is
  broken up by large lakes of fresh red cells, haemorrhagic areas, oedematous or hyalinised pale-pink
  stroma with few cells, and haemosiderin. Nearly every gene was low here, even in good-looking
  tumour nests: HSPA1A ~1.1 and LRRC41 ~1.1 were the only genes near 1; IFI27 ~0.6, LY6E ~0.4,
  SLC39A14 ~0.5, most others 0.1-0.5. Expect about one-third to one-half of CCa levels for the
  same genes. Stroma and blood lakes in this kind of tumour were close to zero for most genes.

FIB - Sclerotic, fibrotic, inflamed tissue with scant tumour. Mostly dense, wavy or hyalinised
  pink collagen, with scattered plump fibroblasts, chronic inflammatory cells (lymphocytes, plasma
  cells), small vessels, haemosiderin, a few atrophic or entrapped tubules and small groups of
  clear tumour cells; also organising haemorrhage and thrombus-like areas. Everything was low
  (mean log1p mostly 0.1-0.6; IFI6 ~0.7, GADD45B ~0.6, HTRA1 ~0.6, IFI27 ~0.55, CPE ~0.55). The
  occasional clear tumour cell groups in such tissue carried the clear-cell programme (SLC39A14
  ~1.7, PTGS1 ~1.7, LRRC41 ~0.5).

EOS - Eosinophilic, high-grade, heavily inflamed carcinoma. Solid and trabecular sheets of cells
  with eosinophilic or granular cytoplasm and medium-large nuclei, broken by many narrow
  slit-like/cleft spaces, mixed with numerous inflammatory cells (neutrophils, lymphocytes,
  macrophages, some foamy cells); little true clear cytoplasm; some areas of looser, oedematous or
  haemorrhagic tissue. Uniform at low power, with no nodular fibrous septa. This phenotype
  had the strongest immune and interferon signals: S100A9 ~1.7, CD14 ~1.5, TRBC1 ~1.8, IFI27
  ~2.2, IFI6 ~1.8, ISG15 ~1.1, IRF7 ~0.7, PARP10 ~0.8, GPNMB ~0.9, plus TFRC ~1.4, NT5DC2 ~1.4,
  LAPTM4B ~1.0, FLNB ~1.1, DNAJB12 ~0.65, GADD45B ~0.95, HNRNPK ~0.8. Low SLC39A14, LRRC41,
  TMEM91, CKB, EGFL7. Looser areas with many neutrophils and red cells had the highest S100A9 (3+).

SARC - Sarcomatoid / spindle-cell carcinoma. Interlacing fascicles of spindle cells with elongated
  or plump oval nuclei in pink fibrillary stroma, alternating with sheets of large epithelioid
  cells with vesicular nuclei, distinct nucleoli and abundant pink cytoplasm; no clear-cell nests.
  Fascicles of collagen-rich spindle stroma and occasional lymphoid collections. HTRA1 dominates:
  ~3.2 in tumour sheets (4-5 in the densest epithelioid sheets), ~2.3 even in the paucicellular
  spindle/collagen areas of the same tumour. Also LY6E ~1.8, NOTCH3 ~0.8, LAPTM4B ~0.8, NT5DC2 ~0.7,
  YWHAE ~0.7, TFRC ~0.65, DERL1 ~0.8, CPE ~1.0. Low LRRC41 (~0.3), SLC39A14 (~0.5), TRBC1 (~0.1),
  S100A9, CD14, CKB, GADD45B.

HG - High-grade (nuclear grade 3-4) epithelioid carcinoma. Large cells with abundant pale
  amphophilic, glassy or partly vacuolated cytoplasm, large vesicular pleomorphic nuclei with
  prominent nucleoli, occasional bizarre or multinucleated cells, thin fibrovascular septa,
  scattered lymphocytes and red cells. Highest overall transcript yield of all.
  CPE ~3.7 (often 4-5), CD109 ~1.9, CIB1 ~1.6, FLNB ~1.6, LRRC41 ~1.6, SEMA4B ~1.4, CKB ~1.4,
  PXN ~1.2, DERL1 ~1.1, CHST15 ~1.1, VMP1 ~1.0, LY6E ~2.2, IFI27 ~1.8, HSPA1A ~1.1, TMEM91 ~0.8,
  SLC15A4 ~0.9. Low HTRA1 (~0.8), SLC39A14 (~0.5), NOTCH3, EGFL7.
  Within this tumour, areas of dyscohesive pleomorphic or spindled cells scattered in pale-pink
  fibrillary/myxoid matrix (sarcomatoid-like, low cell density) were much lower:
  CPE ~1.4 (often 0), CD109 ~0.1, nearly every gene below 0.5. So "large pleomorphic cells in
  sheets" means high CPE, but "isolated pleomorphic cells in loose pink matrix" does not.

Within-tumour adjustments by what is visible in the patch (A3)
------------------------------
Start from the phenotype value, then adjust by the content of the central 55 um spot area:
- Dense viable tumour cells filling the spot: use the phenotype value or slightly above (about
  +0.1 to +0.3 for the cellularity-axis genes in group 1). The most cellular spots of a tumour
  carried most of its high values.
- Fibrous stroma / capsule / septum (pink collagen, few spindle nuclei): total transcript yield was
  about 1 log unit lower than tumour in the same section (range 0.5-1.8). Practically: take
  roughly 40-60% of the tumour value for genes of groups 1, 5 and 6, and expect many zeros. Genes
  that hold up best in stroma: HTRA1 (fibroblast protease; in sarcomatoid tumour stroma ~2.3),
  NOTCH3 (vessel walls, pericytes), CPE, HSPA1A, IFI6, GADD45B, CHST15, RXRA. Genes that collapse
  most in stroma: SLC39A14, LRRC41, TMEM91, NOL3, SCARB1, SLC15A4, PTGS1, SEMA4B, CD109, AKAP8.
- Thick-walled vessel or smooth muscle wall: like stroma but CKB, HTRA1, NOTCH3, RXRA, LAPTM4B
  slightly higher, PXN and SLC39A14 low.
- Lymphoid aggregate or dense lymphocytes (many small round dark nuclei): TRBC1 up clearly (to
  ~1.5-2+ in a big aggregate, against ~0.5 in tumour), with TP53INP1, CD14, EGFL7, GPNMB, ISG15,
  H3F3A, CHST15, S100A9 modestly up; tumour-programme genes (SLC39A14, LRRC41, NOL3, CPE in HG
  tumours) down to roughly half or two-thirds.
- Neutrophil-rich inflammation, abscess-like or loose inflamed tissue: S100A9 high (1.5-3.5), CD14
  up, IFI6/ISG15 up.
- Foamy macrophages, haemosiderin-laden macrophages: GPNMB, CD14, HMOX1 up.
- Haemorrhage / blood lake (sheets of red cells filling most of the spot): total yield lower
  (about -0.3 to -1 log unit), most tumour genes about half; HMOX1, S100A9, EGFL7, STK39, HTRA1
  hold up comparatively and may even be higher (HMOX1 ~0.6 vs ~0.3 in neighbouring clear-cell
  tumour). A patch with only a few red cells in capillaries is not haemorrhage; treat as tumour.
- Empty space (lumen, cyst, tear, tissue edge) in more than half of the patch: scale everything
  down roughly in proportion to how much of the central area lacks tissue.
- Necrosis / degenerating cells with debris (rare): HSPA1A clearly up (~2.5), TP53INP1, NAMPT,
  DNAJB12 up; most structural/tumour genes down (LRRC41, LY6E, FLNB, CKB, CHST15 down 0.7-1.0).
- Mixed patch (half tumour, half stroma): average the two.

How to choose a phenotype from one patch (A1, A6)
------------------------------
- Clear cytoplasm with sharp membranes, small regular nuclei and a fine capillary net: one of
  the CC phenotypes. Default to a value between CCa and CCb. Move toward CCa if the cells form
  crisp alveolar nests with granular pink cytoplasm and almost no inflammation. Move toward CCb
  if lymphocytes are sprinkled through the tumour. Move toward CCc if much of the
  tissue is haemorrhage, red-cell lakes, haemosiderin or hyalinised oedematous stroma. If unsure
  between them, the average of the three CC columns is a safe choice: SLC39A14 ~1.2,
  LRRC41 ~1.4, IFI27 ~1.2, LY6E ~1.0, HSPA1A ~0.8, TMEM91 ~0.75, CPE ~0.7, HTRA1 ~0.7,
  NOL3 ~0.6, SLC15A4 ~0.65, PXN ~0.6, CIB1 ~0.5, SCARB1 ~0.55, PTGS1 ~0.5, and S100A9, CKB,
  NT5DC2, TFRC, IRF7 low (~0.1-0.3).
- Pink/granular (eosinophilic) tumour cells with many inflammatory cells and slit-like spaces:
  EOS. The more neutrophils and lymphocytes, the more S100A9, CD14, TRBC1, IFI27, IFI6, ISG15.
- Spindle cells in fascicles, or plump epithelioid cells in a pink fibrillary background without
  clear cells: SARC (high HTRA1).
- Large pleomorphic nuclei with prominent nucleoli (visible at this magnification as dark dots
  inside open, vesicular nuclei), abundant glassy/amphophilic cytoplasm, cohesive sheets: HG
  (high CPE, CD109, CKB, SEMA4B, CIB1, FLNB).
- Mostly collagen with chronic inflammation, entrapped tubules and few tumour cells: FIB (everything
  low).
- Normal kidney (proximal tubules with brush border and pink granular cytoplasm, glomeruli) was
  essentially absent from training; if seen, treat as a cellular epithelium of moderate yield
  (roughly the ALL column), with clear-cell tumour genes (SLC39A14, NOL3, LRRC41) not elevated.
- Staining varied strongly between sections: some were eosin-heavy and almost uniformly pink with
  faint nuclei, others hematoxylin-heavy and purple-blue. Stain colour is a property of the lab and the
  batch, not of the tumour. Do not predict from colour. Judge cell shape, cytoplasm (clear vs granular vs
  glassy), nuclear size and nucleoli, architecture (nests, sheets, fascicles), and the amount of
  stroma, inflammation and blood.
- Blurry, out-of-focus or folded tissue: judge the tissue type as well as possible and keep
  predictions close to the phenotype mean.

Typical levels and spreads (A7)
----------------------
- Overall spot-level yields differed between tumours by about 1.5 log units (a ~4-fold difference
  in total counts), partly due to tissue type (dense high-grade or inflamed tumour highest;
  haemorrhagic, sclerotic tissue lowest). When a patch looks poor (much blood, oedema,
  hyalinisation, sparse cells), lean low on all genes.
- Typical tumour-patch values for the stronger genes lie between 0.5 and 2.5. Values above 3
  were found mainly for CPE (high-grade sheets), HTRA1 (sarcomatoid), SLC39A14 (classic clear-cell
  nests), S100A9 and CD14 (neutrophil-rich), IFI27 and LY6E (inflamed or high-grade tumour), and
  TRBC1 (lymphoid aggregates).
- Genes with weak and noisy signal everywhere (rarely above 1): BLVRB, BTF3, H3F3A, TM2D3, YWHAE,
  DNAJB12, KRTCAP2, AKAP8, IRF7, TP53INP1, EGFL7. Predict near their overall means (0.2-0.35),
  slightly higher in the most cellular, high-yield tissue and near zero in stroma, blood or
  low-yield tissue.
- Relative order within one tumour, for most genes:
  dense tumour > mixed tumour/stroma > lymphoid > haemorrhage > stroma > empty, for most genes. Use
  the class-specific exceptions above (TRBC1 for lymphocytes, S100A9/CD14 for neutrophils and
  monocytes, HMOX1 for blood and macrophages, HTRA1/NOTCH3 for stroma and vessels).
