CHEATSHEET: PREDICTING 50-GENE EXPRESSION FROM H&E PATCHES OF CLEAR CELL RENAL CELL CARCINOMA (ccRCC)
==============================

What this is based on
---------------------
Visium spatial transcriptomics of ccRCC resection sections from seven patients (500 spots per section, 3500 in all).
Each spot is 55 micrometres across; each patch is 224 x 224 pixels at 0.5 micrometres per pixel, i.e. a 112 x 112
micrometre square centred on the spot, so the spot itself covers roughly the central half of the patch width
(the central circle of about 110 pixels diameter). Tissue at the patch edge contributes less than tissue in the centre.

Values are log(1 + count) per spot for 50 genes, with no normalisation for sequencing depth. Counts are LOW:
for most genes the median spot has 0 counts, and a value of 0.69 means one count, 1.10 two counts, 1.39 three,
1.61 four, 1.79 five, 1.95 six, 2.08 seven, 2.20 eight, 2.30 nine, 2.40 ten, 3.04 twenty, 3.43 thirty, 4.62 one hundred.
So each spot's value is very noisy (Poisson-like sampling of a few molecules). What is predictable from a
patch is the EXPECTED level, i.e. the average value that patches looking like this one tend to have.
Expected (average) values are therefore usually fractional numbers between 0 and 2, even though single spots
show 0, 0.69, 1.10 and so on.

The three things that matter most, in order:
 1. The kind of SECTION / tumour the patch comes from (tumour grade, cytoplasm type, inflammation, how much
    of the section is stroma). Differences between sections are much larger than differences between tissue
    compartments inside one section. Seven section types seen in training are described in detail below.
 2. Whether the patch centre is viable tumour epithelium (higher for almost every gene) or stroma, blood,
    acellular hyaline/oedematous material or pale loose degenerate tissue (lower for almost every gene).
 3. Specific cell types: inflammatory cells (S100A9, CD14, TRBC1, GPNMB, interferon genes), clear tumour cells
    with alveolar capillary network (LRRC41, SLC39A14, TMEM91, SLC15A4, NOL3, SCARB1, PTGS1), high-grade
    tumour (CPE, CD109, CKB, SEMA4B, CIB1, FLNB, LY6E), haemorrhage (HMOX1, HSPA1A, EGFL7 relatively spared),
    fibroblast/vascular-mural cells (HTRA1, NOTCH3, EGFL7).

A general fact: almost all 50 genes rise and fall together with the overall RNA richness of a spot (more
viable, cytoplasm-rich, metabolically active cells = more of everything). The genes that track total RNA most
strongly are LY6E, IFI27, LRRC41, CPE, HTRA1, CIB1, FLNB, PXN, VMP1, SEMA4B, SLC39A14, CD109. So once you decide
a patch is "rich" or "poor", move all genes in the same direction, then adjust the cell-type-specific genes.

What the genes are and where they are made (biology, to reason about new tissue)
------------------------------
Groups of genes that rise and fall together (beyond the shared "RNA richness" effect):

* Interferon / antiviral response: IFI6, ISG15, IFI27, IRF7, PARP10, LY6E (weaker: NAMPT, GADD45B).
  Made by tumour cells, endothelium and immune cells when interferon is around. Highest where tumour is
  infiltrated by many inflammatory cells, and in high-grade tumour. IFI27 is also strongly expressed by ccRCC
  tumour cells and tumour capillaries, which is why it is the highest gene overall, and why it is high in
  almost any viable tumour (typical 1.4-2.2 in tumour areas of RNA-rich sections, 0.5-0.9 in stroma).
  LY6E is the second-highest gene, a broad epithelial/interferon gene; high in all viable tumour.

* Myeloid / inflammatory: CD14 (monocytes/macrophages), S100A9 (neutrophils and monocytes), GPNMB (macrophages,
  especially foamy or lipid-laden ones; also some tumour cells), HMOX1 (macrophages handling haem; rises in
  haemorrhage and haemosiderin areas). TRBC1 (T lymphocytes) travels with this group because T cells and
  myeloid cells infiltrate together. S100A9 is near zero (mean about 0.15-0.35) wherever there are no
  neutrophils, and jumps to about 1.7 in a heavily inflamed tumour. Plain lymphoid aggregates of small round
  lymphocytes raise TRBC1 (by about +0.2 over the section's average) and slightly CD14/ISG15/IRF7, but do NOT
  raise the total; isolated lymphoid aggregates in stroma are actually fairly RNA-poor in this assay.

* Clear-cell tumour module: LRRC41, SLC39A14, TMEM91, SLC15A4, NOL3, SCARB1 (HDL/cholesterol uptake - lipid-rich
  clear cytoplasm), PTGS1, STK39, AKAP8. These are highest in nests of cells with water-clear or finely
  vacuolated cytoplasm surrounded by a delicate "chicken-wire" capillary network, and drop sharply outside
  tumour (LRRC41 about 1.4 in clear tumour vs 0.35 in stroma and 0.2 in blood). SLC39A14 is the most extreme:
  in one classic alveolar clear-cell tumour it averaged 2.4 in tumour nests (97% of spots non-zero), but 0.4-0.6
  in other clear-cell tumours, so treat a SLC39A14 value above 1 as reserved for very typical, well-preserved,
  nested clear-cell carcinoma with a crisp capillary network.

* High-grade / dedifferentiating tumour-cell module: CPE, CD109, CKB, SEMA4B, CIB1, FLNB, PXN, DERL1, CHST15,
  KRTCAP2, VMP1, LY6E. Very high in a tumour with large vesicular nuclei, prominent nucleoli and abundant
  amphophilic-to-clear cytoplasm (ISUP grade 3-4). CPE (carboxypeptidase E, a neuroendocrine/secretory enzyme)
  reached 3.7 on average there (about 40 counts per spot) but is 0.4-1.2 in other tumours. CKB and CD109
  similarly 1.2-1.9 there and 0.1-0.5 elsewhere.

* Proliferation / iron / lysosome: TFRC (transferrin receptor), NT5DC2, LAPTM4B, DNAJB12, HNRNPK. Highest in
  the heavily inflamed eosinophilic tumour (TFRC 1.4, NT5DC2 1.4, LAPTM4B 1.0); NT5DC2 is otherwise near 0.1-0.4.

* Stroma / vessel wall: HTRA1 (fibroblasts and matrix-rich areas, also expressed by spindle-shaped tumour
  cells), NOTCH3 (pericytes and vascular smooth muscle; raised where there are many small vessels or
  muscular vessel walls), EGFL7 (endothelium; slightly raised in blood-filled areas and vascular stroma).
  Note that in this tumour type even fibrous stroma is not much richer in HTRA1/NOTCH3 than the tumour
  nests, because ccRCC nests are themselves full of capillaries and pericytes.

* Stress / immediate-early: HSPA1A (heat shock), GADD45B, NAMPT, H3F3A, TP53INP1. These do not follow tumour
  vs stroma much. HSPA1A is relatively high (about 0.9-1.0) in the haemorrhagic low-grade, high-grade, inflamed and
  dense lymphocyte-rich tumours, lower (about 0.55-0.6) in the classic alveolar clear-cell, spindled and
  stroma-dominated tissue, and keeps its level even in blood-filled and poorly cellular patches
  (HSPA1A is the gene least reduced in haemorrhage; it is even slightly higher there). GADD45B is relatively
  high in normal-looking renal tubules and in fibromuscular stroma-dominated sections (about 0.55-1.1).

* Housekeeping-like, uniformly low: BTF3, YWHAE, TM2D3, BLVRB, AKAP8, KRTCAP2, DNAJB12, H3F3A, CPD, RXRA.
  Predict 0.1-0.5 depending on RNA richness (0.1 in poor sections and acellular areas, 0.4-0.5 in rich tumour).

SEVEN SECTION TYPES SEEN IN TRAINING (the most important part)
==============================
Every training section had a distinct overall character, and the gene profile followed that character much
more than it followed the local compartment. With a new patch, first decide which of these types the tissue
most resembles (look at nuclear size and grade, cytoplasm, how many inflammatory cells, how much stroma),
then start from that type's numbers, then adjust for the compartment at the patch centre (next chapter).
If a new patch fits none of them well, blend the two closest, or use the pooled class table.
The letters are only names for the descriptions here.

TYPE A - "High-grade, large-nucleus tumour" (RNA richest of all; total counts per spot about 5x the poorest types)
  Look: purple/lilac staining. Solid sheets and nests of large polygonal tumour cells with LARGE round-oval
  vesicular nuclei, open chromatin and conspicuous nucleoli (ISUP grade 3-4); nuclei often 2-3x the size of a
  lymphocyte nucleus. Cytoplasm abundant, pale amphophilic to lightly eosinophilic, partly clear or vacuolated.
  Thin fibrovascular septa with a few red cells. Some areas are looser and paler, with spindled or
  degenerate-looking cells, oedema and scattered extravasated red cells. Little stroma overall, few lymphocytes.
  Expression: highest CPE (3.3 section average; 3.7 in solid tumour areas, 1.9 in pale loose areas),
  CD109 1.5 (1.9 in tumour), CKB 1.2 (1.3-1.6), SEMA4B 1.2 (1.4-1.5), CIB1 1.3, FLNB 1.4, LY6E 1.9 (2.2 in
  tumour), IFI27 1.6, LRRC41 1.3, PXN 1.0, DERL1 0.9, CHST15 0.9, VMP1 0.9, KRTCAP2 0.5 (highest of all types),
  SLC15A4 0.75. Low: S100A9 0.34, TRBC1 0.36, NT5DC2 0.23, EGFL7 0.17 (lowest), PTGS1 0.26.
  Within this type the pale, loose, spindled or degenerate areas are MUCH lower than the solid nests:
  roughly CPE 1.9 vs 3.7, CD109 0.7 vs 1.9, FLNB 0.4 vs 1.7, LY6E 0.9 vs 2.2, IFI27 0.7 vs 1.9, LRRC41 0.45 vs
  1.6 - about a quarter of the RNA. Fibrous stroma between nests sits in between (CPE 2.9, CD109 1.4).

TYPE B - "Inflamed eosinophilic tumour" (RNA rich)
  Look: very pink/magenta stain with weak haematoxylin, so nuclei look pale pink-purple rather than blue (do
  not mistake the weak nuclear stain for necrosis). Tumour cells with abundant eosinophilic to granular
  cytoplasm and enlarged nuclei, often discohesive with many small slit-like or irregular retraction spaces;
  densely mixed with small round inflammatory cells (neutrophils, lymphocytes, macrophages), so the tissue
  looks busy and crowded. Some patches show thin bands of collagen; little haemorrhage.
  Expression: the immune and interferon genes peak: S100A9 1.7 (98% of spots non-zero; elsewhere 0.15-0.35),
  CD14 1.6, TRBC1 1.6, IFI27 2.2, IFI6 1.75, ISG15 1.0, IRF7 0.67, PARP10 0.8, GPNMB 0.86, HMOX1 0.5.
  Also peak: NT5DC2 1.4, TFRC 1.4, LAPTM4B 1.0, DNAJB12 0.6, HNRNPK 0.85, GADD45B 0.9, RXRA 0.74, SCARB1 0.73,
  CPD 0.65, FLNB 1.1, CIB1 0.9, LY6E 1.7. Low for a rich tumour: LRRC41 0.4, CKB 0.1 (lowest of all types),
  CPE 0.45, TMEM91 0.3, SLC39A14 0.6.
  Within this type tumour and the collagen bands differ little (stroma patches: CD14 1.4, S100A9 1.7, TRBC1
  1.5) because inflammatory cells are everywhere; NOTCH3 and EGFL7 slightly higher in the stromal/vascular
  patches (0.74 and 0.65 vs 0.5 and 0.2).

TYPE C - "Classic alveolar clear-cell carcinoma" (RNA rich)
  Look: pink stain. Nests and alveoli of cells with water-clear or finely vacuolated cytoplasm, distinct cell
  membranes and small to medium round nuclei (grade 2-ish), each nest outlined by a delicate, crisp
  "chicken-wire" network of thin-walled capillaries. Some broad fibrous septa and capsule-like bands of dense
  pink collagen; occasional small lymphoid clusters at the edges. Very little haemorrhage or necrosis.
  Expression: the clear-cell module peaks: SLC39A14 2.2 (2.4 in tumour nests, 1.4 in septa), LRRC41 1.6
  (1.8 tumour), IFI27 1.8, TMEM91 0.75, SLC15A4 0.75, NOL3 0.67, SCARB1 0.7, PTGS1 0.7, AKAP8 0.6 (highest),
  STK39 0.57, TP53INP1 0.47, NOTCH3 0.7, EGFL7 0.47 (highest; capillary-rich), HTRA1 1.1, CPE 1.2, TRBC1 0.75.
  Low: CKB 0.28, GPNMB 0.26, S100A9 0.14, CD109 0.4, FLNB 0.37, NT5DC2 0.27.
  Fibrous septa: everything about 30-40% lower than tumour nests, but EGFL7 and NOTCH3 hold up (0.7 and 0.4).

TYPE D - "Spindled / fibrous tumour with eosinophilic epithelioid cells" (RNA moderately rich)
  Look: pink stain. Interlacing fascicles of spindle cells and wavy collagen, mixed with clusters and cords of
  epithelioid tumour cells with eosinophilic cytoplasm and vesicular oval nuclei; little clear cytoplasm;
  scattered lymphocytes and some dense lymphocyte clusters; small vessels. Possible sarcomatoid or desmoplastic
  pattern. About half the patches are spindle/fibrous, half epithelioid tumour.
  Expression: HTRA1 peaks at 2.7 (98% non-zero; 2.8 in epithelioid tumour, 2.5 in spindle/fibrous areas, 2.2 in
  lymphoid clusters) - in this type HTRA1 is made by the tumour and its spindle cells, not just by ordinary
  fibroblasts. NOTCH3 0.72 (high), LAPTM4B 0.72, LY6E 1.5, BTF3 0.43 and YWHAE 0.47 (highest of all types),
  IFI6 0.84, IFI27 0.9, CPE 0.9. Low: LRRC41 0.25, CKB 0.17, CD109 0.24, S100A9 0.18, TRBC1 0.22 (despite the
  lymphocytes), GPNMB 0.19, SCARB1 0.24, TMEM91 0.32, PTGS1 0.23, PARP10 0.25.
  Spindle/fibrous patches are only slightly lower than epithelioid tumour patches here.

TYPE E - "Dense small-cell clear tumour with many lymphocytes" (RNA moderate)
  Look: purple/blue stain, very cellular. Sheets and small nests of tumour cells with clear to faintly
  granular cytoplasm and small dark round nuclei, packed closely, with many interspersed lymphocytes so the
  patch looks dark-dotted; a thick fibrous capsule and fibrous bands with vessels, some dilated thin-walled
  vessels, a few lymphoid aggregates (sometimes with dark brown-black pigment), a little haemorrhage.
  Expression: middle of the range. LY6E 1.2, HSPA1A 1.0, LRRC41 0.86, IFI27 0.9, TMEM91 0.74, CIB1 0.64,
  DERL1 0.62, CPE 0.6, HTRA1 0.6, SLC39A14 0.55, CPD 0.5, TP53INP1 0.45 (relatively high), TRBC1 0.46.
  Low: NT5DC2 0.09, GADD45B 0.24, CD109 0.2, CKB 0.2, IRF7 0.15, S100A9 0.23, NOTCH3 0.24.
  Tumour vs fibrous band: LRRC41 1.0 vs 0.5, LY6E 1.2 vs 0.8, TMEM91 0.7 vs 0.45; lymphoid areas similar to
  tumour overall but TRBC1 0.74 vs 0.42 and ISG15 0.63 vs 0.24.

TYPE F - "Low-grade clear-cell tumour with haemorrhage and hyaline stroma" (RNA poor)
  Look: purple-blue stain. Large nodules of low-grade clear-cell carcinoma (clear cytoplasm, small round
  uniform nuclei, delicate capillaries), separated by broad zones of paucicellular, glassy pink hyalinised
  or oedematous loose stroma; extensive fresh haemorrhage (patches entirely filled with red cells), dilated
  vessels, haemosiderin-laden macrophages (golden-brown pigment), and occasional dense lymphoid aggregates.
  Expression: low overall; tumour nests are reasonable (LRRC41 1.2, HSPA1A 1.1, IFI27 0.9, VMP1 0.66,
  SLC15A4 0.58, LY6E 0.54, HTRA1 0.55, CPE 0.7, TRBC1 0.5, CHST15 0.45, SLC39A14 0.48, CD14 0.43, NOL3 0.34) but
  stroma, blood and hyaline zones are very poor (most genes 0-0.2). Section averages: HSPA1A 0.94, LRRC41 0.8,
  IFI27 0.63, HTRA1 0.52, CPE 0.42, LY6E 0.34 (low for a tumour), IFI6 0.37, and almost everything else
  0.1-0.3; BLVRB 0.08, BTF3 0.11, KRTCAP2 0.11, YWHAE 0.11, CD109 0.11, NT5DC2 0.09, IRF7 0.10.
  Blood-filled patches here: HMOX1 0.8, HSPA1A 1.4, H3F3A 0.66, EGFL7 0.5, GADD45B 0.43 - these are higher than
  in the tumour nests; LRRC41 0.3, LY6E 0.15, IFI27 0.3, CHST15 0.15, GPNMB 0 - much lower.
  Hyaline/acellular patches: nearly everything 0-0.2 (HSPA1A 0.5, LRRC41 0.3, IFI27 0.2).

TYPE G - "Stroma-dominated fibromuscular tissue with few tumour cells" (RNA poorest)
  Look: pink stain. Most patches are dense collagen and smooth-muscle-like fascicles (wavy or parallel pink
  fibres with sparse elongated nuclei), thick-walled small arteries and veins, scattered and clustered
  lymphocytes and plasma cells, haemosiderin and some haemorrhage, a few atrophic or normal-looking renal
  tubules (round profiles lined by cuboidal eosinophilic epithelium, some with pink casts), and only rare tumour
  cell groups. This is the pseudocapsule / fibrotic edge of a tumour or perinephric/hilar tissue.
  Expression: low for almost everything. Section averages: IFI6 0.74 and GADD45B 0.57 (relatively preserved),
  IFI27 0.55, HSPA1A 0.54, HTRA1 0.59, CPE 0.54, RXRA 0.39, NAMPT 0.38, NOTCH3 0.36, TRBC1 0.32, PTGS1 0.31,
  LY6E 0.27 (lowest of all), LRRC41 0.21, and 0.1-0.25 for the rest (BTF3 0.09, YWHAE 0.09, IRF7 0.09,
  SCARB1 0.10, GPNMB 0.10, NOL3 0.11, KRTCAP2 0.11, TMEM91 0.13, HMOX1 0.13).
  Lymphocyte clusters in this tissue are NOT richer than the stroma (total even lower). Renal tubules were
  slightly richer (GADD45B about 1.1, VMP1 0.76, IFI6 0.96, RXRA 0.5) but there were few of them.

TISSUE COMPARTMENTS INSIDE A SECTION
==============================
Classes used (judge what fills the central ~110 pixels of the patch):
  clear  = tumour nests with clear / vacuolated cytoplasm and a fine capillary network
  eosin  = tumour with eosinophilic, granular or amphophilic cytoplasm, or solid high-grade tumour
  paleS  = pale, loose, oedematous or degenerate-looking tumour areas with spindled cells and scattered red
           cells inside a high-grade tumour (seen only in Type A; about a fifth of the RNA of solid tumour)
  lymph  = dense lymphocytes (lymphoid aggregate or heavy lymphocytic band)
  strom  = fibrous / fibromuscular stroma, septa, capsule, vessel walls (including mixed tumour+stroma)
  haem   = patch mostly filled with red blood cells (fresh haemorrhage, blood-filled vessel lumen)
  tubul  = non-neoplastic renal tubules (few examples; treat as rough)
  acell  = hyalinised acellular collagen, oedema fluid, fibrin, empty space with few cells

Key reading of Table 2:
 * Viable tumour (clear or eosinophilic) is above the section average for nearly every gene, typically
   +0.05 to +0.25. The clear-cell module (LRRC41 +0.24, SLC39A14 +0.15, IFI27 +0.15, VMP1 +0.13, SLC15A4 +0.12,
   NOL3 +0.10, BTF3 +0.10) gains most in clear tumour; eosinophilic tumour gains IFI27 +0.22, CD109 +0.17,
   DERL1 +0.14, LY6E +0.13.
 * Stroma is a little below average for most genes (-0.05 to -0.17; LRRC41 -0.17, LY6E -0.12, CIB1 -0.11),
   and about level for NOTCH3 (+0.06), HTRA1, EGFL7, GADD45B, HSPA1A and CPE.
 * Haemorrhage: big losses for LRRC41 (-0.51), LY6E, TMEM91, CHST15, GPNMB, CIB1, VMP1, CPE, SEMA4B, SLC39A14,

 * Hyaline / acellular / oedema: lowest of all (total counts about half the section average); LRRC41 -0.43,
   SLC39A14 -0.36, IFI27 -0.34, TRBC1 -0.31, HSPA1A -0.29, HNRNPK -0.28, CPE -0.28, LY6E -0.27, IFI6 -0.24.
 * Lymphoid: TRBC1 +0.18, CD14 +0.17, ISG15 +0.13, GPNMB +0.11, S100A9 +0.09, EGFL7 +0.08; most tumour genes
   slightly down (SLC39A14 -0.16, TMEM91 -0.13, DERL1 -0.12, HTRA1 -0.12, IFI27 -0.11).

Tumour vs stroma, section by section (spot totals; and the gap is gene-wide):
 * In poor, haemorrhagic, hyalinised sections (Type F) the gap is largest: stroma has about 40% of the RNA of
   tumour nests; hyaline zones about 20%.
 * In classic clear-cell (Type C) septa have about 55% of the tumour's RNA; LRRC41 1.8 vs 1.3, SLC39A14 2.4 vs 1.4.
 * In high-grade (Type A) the fibrous septa have about half the RNA but still high module genes.
 * In inflamed (Type B) and spindled (Type D) sections stroma and tumour are close (80-90% of the tumour's
   RNA), because the "stroma" is itself cellular (inflammatory cells, spindle cells). In the dense
   lymphocyte-rich Type E the fibrous bands have about 65% of the tumour's RNA and the lymphoid areas match
   the tumour.
So: the more cellular the stroma (spindle cells, inflammatory cells), the closer it is to tumour; the more
glassy, pale and acellular, the lower everything is.

HOW TO WORK THROUGH ONE PATCH (by eye)
==============================
Step 1 - What is at the centre? Decide the compartment of the central ~110 pixels: clear tumour, eosinophilic or
  high-grade tumour, pale loose degenerate tumour, fibrous stroma, lymphoid, haemorrhage, hyaline/acellular,
  normal tubules. If the centre is mixed, weigh the compartments by area.

Step 2 - What kind of tumour/section is this? Use the cells you can see in the patch:
  * Nuclei large (clearly bigger than red cells, 2-3x a lymphocyte nucleus), vesicular, with visible nucleoli,
    cytoplasm abundant and pale/amphophilic -> Type A profile (high CPE, CD109, CKB, SEMA4B, CIB1, FLNB, LY6E).
  * Many small round inflammatory cells (neutrophils with lobed nuclei, lymphocytes, macrophages) mixed in
    among eosinophilic tumour cells, discohesive tissue with retraction spaces -> Type B profile (high S100A9,
    CD14, TRBC1, IFI6/IFI27/ISG15, TFRC, NT5DC2, GPNMB, LAPTM4B).
  * Textbook clear cells in round nests with crisp thin capillaries, no inflammation -> Type C profile (high
    SLC39A14, LRRC41, TMEM91, SLC15A4, NOL3, SCARB1, PTGS1, AKAP8, EGFL7).
  * Spindle cells in fascicles with interspersed epithelioid eosinophilic tumour cells -> Type D profile (high
    HTRA1, NOTCH3, LAPTM4B, LY6E; low LRRC41).
  * Crowded small clear cells with small dark nuclei and many lymphocytes, purple-dense look -> Type E profile
    (moderate everything; LY6E about 1.2, LRRC41 0.9, TMEM91 0.7, low NT5DC2/CD109/CKB).
  * Low-grade clear cells next to haemorrhage, hyalinised glassy stroma, haemosiderin -> Type F profile (tumour
    nests modest, everything else very low; HSPA1A and LRRC41 the strongest genes).
  * Fibromuscular stroma, thick-walled vessels, scattered lymphocytes, tubules, almost no tumour -> Type G
    profile (lowest of all; IFI6 0.7, GADD45B 0.6, HSPA1A/HTRA1/CPE/IFI27 about 0.55, rest 0.1-0.4).
  A single patch only shows a little of the section; use whatever clues are present (nuclear grade of any
  tumour cells in the patch is the best clue; for a pure stroma or blood patch, inflammatory cell content and
  stroma cellularity are the clues). If you cannot tell, use the pooled class table (Table 1).

Step 3 - Start from the type's section average, then add the compartment change (Table 2), or directly use the
  compartment numbers given inside each type description.

Step 4 - Adjust for richness you can see:
  * More viable, intact, cytoplasm-rich cells in the centre -> raise all genes a little (up to +0.2 for the
    high genes). Good nuclear detail, crisp membranes and intact cells suggest well-preserved, RNA-rich tissue.
  * Large empty spaces (lumen, tears, cyst spaces, oedema fluid) or glassy acellular material in the centre ->
    lower all genes in proportion to the empty fraction (half the centre empty -> roughly halve counts, i.e.
    subtract about 0.2-0.4 from genes around 1, less from low genes).
  * Patch filled with red cells -> use the haemorrhage pattern (HMOX1, HSPA1A, EGFL7 up; tumour genes down).
  * Golden-brown haemosiderin and foamy macrophages -> raise HMOX1, CD14, GPNMB a little (+0.1 to +0.3).
  * Many neutrophils -> S100A9 up strongly (toward 1.5-2); many lymphocytes -> TRBC1 up (+0.2 to +0.5 when the
    centre is a dense lymphocyte sheet in an otherwise rich section; little change in poor stroma).
  * Many small vessels / pericytes / smooth muscle -> NOTCH3 and EGFL7 up by about 0.1-0.3.

Step 5 - Keep values realistic: section-type averages for most genes lie between 0.1 and 1.0; only a handful
  of gene/type combinations exceed 1.5 (CPE in Type A up to about 3.7 in solid nests; HTRA1 in Type D about
  2.7; SLC39A14 in Type C about 2.2-2.4; IFI27 in B, C, A about 1.6-2.2; LY6E in A, B, D about 1.5-2.2;
  S100A9, CD14, TRBC1 in B about 1.6-1.7; LRRC41 in C about 1.6-1.8). Genes that are mostly zero (BLVRB,
  BTF3, TM2D3, IRF7, KRTCAP2, YWHAE, H3F3A, DNAJB12) rarely average more than 0.5 even in the richest tissue.

CAUTIONS ABOUT APPEARANCE
=========================
* Stain colour varies enormously between sections and is NOT itself a guide to expression. Three training
  sections were purple-blue with strong haematoxylin; four were intensely pink-magenta with weak, pinkish
  nuclear staining. Both colour groups contained an RNA-rich and an RNA-poor section (the richest high-grade
  tumour was purple; the poorest low-grade haemorrhagic tumour was also purple; among pink sections the
  inflamed tumour was rich and the stroma-dominated tissue was poor). Judge cell types, nuclear size and
  grade, cytoplasm and architecture instead of colour.
* In strongly pink sections, eosinophilic-looking cytoplasm may simply be stain; check for granularity and
  cell outlines. Clear cells still show as empty-looking cytoplasm with a crisp membrane, and red cells are
  still recognisable as small uniform biconcave discs (orange-red in purple sections, deep pink-red in pink ones).
* Weak haematoxylin makes lymphocytes look like faint pink dots; look for small round, evenly spaced nuclei
  without visible cytoplasm. Neutrophils show lobed nuclei.
* Artefacts seen in patches: dark folds or a black-brown fibre/hair across the patch, dark blurred smudges
  (out of focus), ink or pigment specks, knife chatter, tears. Judge the tissue around them; a fold over
  tumour does not reduce expression much. Out-of-focus dark smudges in pink sections usually overlie
  ordinary tumour.
* Brown-black granular pigment in lymphoid or stromal cells is haemosiderin or anthracotic pigment in
  macrophages; it marks old haemorrhage (HMOX1, CD14 slightly up).
* Patch edges: the measured spot covers mainly the central half of the patch width. A blood vessel or
  stroma band at the very edge matters little.
* Single-spot values are noisy (a gene whose average is 0.5 is 0 in about half of the spots of that kind).
* Lymphoid aggregates are not always "rich": in the stroma-dominated fibromuscular tissue they were among the
  poorest patches; in the dense lymphocyte-rich tumour they matched the tumour. Expect TRBC1 to go up
  relative to the surroundings, not necessarily the other genes.
* Necrosis was rare in the training material. Expect true coagulative necrosis (ghost cells, nuclear dust,
  no intact nuclei) to behave like the acellular class or lower: all genes near zero, HSPA1A and HMOX1 a
  little preserved. Viable tumour right next to necrosis may show raised S100A9 and CD14 (neutrophils).
* Normal kidney (glomeruli, proximal tubules with brush border, distal tubules) was barely present; the few
  tubule patches were similar to stroma in total level, with GADD45B (about 1.1), VMP1, IFI6, RXRA, YWHAE
  relatively higher and the tumour modules (LRRC41, CD109, CPE, DERL1, PXN) low.
